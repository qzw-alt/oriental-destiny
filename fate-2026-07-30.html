Indoor Lighting Feng Shui: 3 Lamps That Change the Whole Room (and 1 to Remove)

Yesterday's outdoor lighting piece closed the property's night-read loop: the dragon follows the warm porch light from the gate, the path lights along the side connector, the courtyard lantern at the center. Here's the read on the other side of the front door: the indoor lighting the dragon follows once the homeowner has stepped inside. A standalone pillar piece on the six indoor lighting typologies, the four room-by-room placement rules, the three defects, the chart-pair bulb read, and the seasonal shift the indoor-outdoor handoff carries as the late-July Earth month moves toward the late-August Metal preview.

Published: 2026-07-30 · Reading time: about 15 minutes

Why the indoor lighting is the read the outdoor piece ended with

Yesterday's outdoor lighting piece walked the dragon's first nine outdoor reads on the property: the porch light at the front door, the wall sconces flanking the gate, the path lights along the side connector, the underwater light at the still pond, the courtyard lantern at the center of the property. Each of those nine reads ended at the homeowner's threshold. The dragon walked up to the front door under the porch light and waited for the door to open. The dragon paused at the back patio under the back sconce and waited for the homeowner to step out. The dragon crossed the courtyard under the central lantern and waited at the entrance hall. Each read stopped at the wall the homeowner crosses to go inside. This piece starts on the other side of that wall, where the indoor lighting picks up what the outdoor lighting leaves off.

What the indoor lighting share with the outdoor lighting is the dragon's natural movement toward light after dark. The dragon does not stop following light when the homeowner closes the front door, and the dragon does not stop following light when the homeowner dims the living-room sconce. The dragon follows the homeowner's lamp choices from the entry hall through the hallway, into the living room, across the dining table, into the kitchen, down the corridor, into the bedroom, and finally to the bedside table. The homeowner's indoor lighting is the read the dragon has been building toward for nine outdoor reads, and the read the homeowner's BaZi chart has been signaling for every seasonal shift the homeowner has lived through.

What the indoor lighting does not share with the outdoor lighting is the rain, the wind, and the neighbor's window. The outdoor lighting is exposed to weather, exposed to the property line, exposed to the dragon's first arrival from the street. The indoor lighting is shielded from all three: the indoor lighting sits inside a four-walled envelope the homeowner controls, the indoor lighting follows a routine the homeowner chooses, and the indoor lighting meets the dragon where the dragon has already settled into the property. The indoor read is a softer read, a closer read, and a read the homeowner can change every evening with the flip of a switch. The outdoor read is a fixed read, a watched read, and a read that takes a professional installer to revise. The indoor lighting is the read the homeowner can adjust in real time.

The indoor lighting read, in one sentenceRead the indoor lighting typology in each of the six named rooms (floor lamp, table lamp, wall sconce, ceiling fixture, candle, bedside lamp — set by the fixture's reach into the room, not the bulb's wattage). Read the color temperature against the chart's missing element (warm white feeds Fire, cool white feeds Metal, daylight feeds Earth). Read the switching pattern against the dragon's settling pace (a settling circuit that follows the homeowner from room to room, a stationary circuit that runs all evening). Fix the three defects in order: a fixture that over-lights a room, a fixture that under-lights a corner, a candle left burning in an empty room. The homeowner who has done the four-room checklist has finished the indoor lighting element yesterday's outdoor piece handed off.

The six indoor lighting typologies

Every indoor lighting layout falls into six typologies, set by the fixture's reach into the room rather than by the bulb's wattage, brand, or expense. The reach is fixed by the room's size, the homeowner's existing electrical plan, and the furniture layout the homeowner has already chosen. The homeowner cannot change the typology without rewiring the room, and the read is to use the typology the room already has.

Floor lampVertical reachA standing fixture mounted on a tall pole or a heavy base, usually a torchiere (upward-firing shade), a downbridge (downward-firing arm), or a tree lamp (multiple arms at multiple heights). Reads as the most room-defining typology because the floor lamp establishes the room's vertical anchor and casts light in a wide cone that fills the corner the floor lamp occupies. The lean-into read is to keep the floor lamp behind the chair the homeowner reads in (a floor lamp in front of the chair washes the page with glare) and to keep the floor lamp at least eighteen inches from the wall when it has a torchiere shade (a torchiere eighteen inches from the wall reads as a corner anchor, a torchiere flush against the wall reads as a corner blot).
Table lampSurface reachA fixture mounted on a side table, a console, a desk, or a shelf, usually a drum shade, a cone shade, or a glass jar shade. Reads as the most personal typology because the table lamp sits at the homeowner's working height and casts light downward onto whatever the homeowner is doing (reading, writing, sewing, eating). The lean-into read is to keep the table lamp's shade opaque above the bulb but translucent at the sides (an opaque shade hides the bulb's source and the dragon sees only the lit surface, while a translucent shade lets the dragon see the bulb's flame and reads the lamp as a gathering element) and to keep the table lamp at the homeowner's non-dominant side (a right-handed reader's table lamp on the right side casts the reader's own shadow across the page).
Wall sconceLateral reachA fixture mounted on a wall at the homeowner's eye level or above, usually a half-cylinder lantern, a flat panel, an uplight, or a downlight. Reads as the most architectural typology because the wall sconce frames the wall's surface and draws the dragon's eye upward through the vertical plane. The lean-into read is to keep wall sconces in pairs (one on each side of a doorway, one on each side of a fireplace, one on each side of a bed) so the dragon sees a balanced framing rather than a single light source hanging on a wall, and to keep the wall sconce aimed at the wall rather than into the room (an inward-aimed sconce washes the room with glare, an outward-aimed sconce washes the wall with light and reflects gently into the room).
Ceiling fixtureOverhead reachA fixture mounted on the ceiling at the center of the room, usually a flush-mount dome, a semi-flush pendant, a chandelier, a recessed can array, or a track-light setup. Reads as the most general typology because the ceiling fixture illuminates the whole room from above without naming any single feature. The lean-into read is to keep the ceiling fixture dimmable (a fixture at a single brightness reads as the room's only light, and the homeowner's late-evening read is to a single bright source rather than a layered read) and to keep the ceiling fixture paired with at least two lower sources (a table lamp at the corner, a floor lamp by the chair, a wall sconce at the art) so the dragon sees the room layered rather than flat.
CandleLocal reachA flame or a wax melt inside a holder, usually a pillar candle, a tea light, a jar candle, a taper candle, or a wax warmer. Reads as the most contemplative typology because the candle's flame is visible to the dragon and the dragon sees the source of the light rather than the light's effect. The lean-into read is to keep the candle on a stable, non-flammable surface (a candle on a wood shelf is a fire risk, a candle on a stone or metal surface is a stable flame) and to keep the candle's scent subtle (a candle that fills the room with fragrance is a candle the dragon smells before the dragon sees the light, and the dragon's settling pace shifts from visual to olfactory).
Bedside lampPersonal reachA small fixture mounted on the nightstand or the wall above the bed, usually a mini-drum shade, a clip-on shade, a swing-arm lamp, or a wall-mounted reading light. Reads as the most intimate typology because the bedside lamp sits within arm's reach of the homeowner's pillow and casts light at the close distance the dragon follows the homeowner to sleep. The lean-into read is to keep the bedside lamp's light direction controllable (a swing-arm lamp the homeowner can aim at the page, a wall-mounted lamp with a downward shade) and to keep the bedside lamp's bulb warm (a cool blue bulb beside the bed reads as a wake-up light, not a settle-down light, and the dragon's settling pace reverses toward morning alertness rather than evening settling).

Most homes have more than one typology in each room. A living room might have a ceiling fixture in the center, a floor lamp in the corner, and a table lamp on the side console. A bedroom might have a ceiling fixture in the center and a pair of bedside lamps on the nightstands. A dining room might have a chandelier over the table and a candle on the sideboard. Each typology carries its own read, and the homeowner reads each fixture separately. The indoor lighting reads together form the full evening read the homeowner needs once the outdoor lighting reads are done.

The four room-by-room placement rules

Once the indoor lighting typology is named, the read looks at the placement the homeowner has already chosen in each room. The four room rules are the four compass quadrants the foundation reads have always used, and the rule is the same in every tradition: the dragon reads light differently depending on which room the light sits in. The homeowner's job is to know which room the lighting occupies and what that room means for the read.

Rule one: the entry hall lighting reads as the dragon's hand-off. The first rule is to place one warm-toned fixture in the entry hall, within sight of the front door. The entry hall is the Wood quadrant's indoor extension (the dragon's first step inside), and the entry hall lighting receives the dragon from the outdoor lighting and walks the dragon into the home's center. A pendant in the entry hall ceiling, a pair of sconces flanking the closet, a table lamp on the entry console are all valid placements. The lean-into read is to keep the entry hall lighting on the same dusk-to-midnight timer the outdoor lighting's settling circuit is on (a homeowner's entry hall that goes dark at 9pm while the porch light stays on until midnight reads as a home the dragon is being invited to but not settled into) and to keep the entry hall lighting warm-toned at 2700K (the same color the outdoor porch light uses, so the dragon sees continuity from the gate to the entry).

Rule two: the living room lighting reads as the household's settling volume. The second rule is to layer the living room lighting across at least three sources so the dragon sees a room rather than a single bright fixture. The living room is the home's center (the Taiji position's indoor extension), and the living room lighting carries the household's volume. A ceiling fixture at low brightness, a floor lamp in one corner, a table lamp on the side console, and a candle on the coffee table are all part of the layered read. The lean-into read is to keep the ceiling fixture at no more than forty percent of the room's possible brightness (a living room at full ceiling brightness reads as the room the homeowner uses for guests, not the room the homeowner settles into) and to keep the candles lit only when someone is sitting in the room (a candle left burning in an empty room reads as a room the homeowner has abandoned).

Rule three: the bedroom lighting reads as the dragon's settling close. The third rule is to place the bedside lighting on the homeowner's non-dominant side and to keep the ceiling fixture off in the evening. The bedroom is the Kun quadrant's indoor extension (the receiving position), and the bedroom lighting closes the dragon's settling pace. Two bedside lamps on matching nightstands, a single wall sconce above the headboard, a pair of swing-arm lamps mounted to the bed frame are all valid placements. The lean-into read is to keep the ceiling fixture on a separate switch from the bedside lamps and to keep the ceiling fixture off for the entire evening routine (a ceiling fixture the homeowner turns on at midnight to walk to the bathroom washes the room with cold light and resets the dragon's settling pace toward wakefulness).

Rule four: the kitchen lighting reads as the dragon's working pace. The fourth rule is to keep the kitchen lighting brighter than the rest of the home and to keep the kitchen lighting cooler-toned than the living room or bedroom. The kitchen is the Fire-leaning quadrant's indoor extension (the working position), and the kitchen's lighting carries the homeowner's working pace. A row of recessed cans in the ceiling, an under-cabinet LED strip, a pendant over the island are all valid placements. The lean-into read is to keep the kitchen at 3500K to 4000K (the working color temperature, which signals Metal's reflective quality and supports the homeowner's hands-rhythm work), and to keep the kitchen lighting on a separate circuit from the dining area (a kitchen the homeowner can dim while eating reads as a kitchen the homeowner has finished using, and the dragon's pace shifts from working to settling as the homeowner dims the kitchen and lights the dining candles).

The three defects

The three-defect walk is what the homeowner does once the typology is named, the four placement rules are checked, and the home's existing indoor lighting layout is read. The walk takes about twenty minutes at dusk and covers the three defects the indoor lighting read always catches. The defects are listed in the order they appear in the homeowner's check.

Defect one: a fixture that over-lights the room. The first defect is the most common and the most damaging. A ceiling fixture at full brightness, a floor lamp with a daylight bulb, a row of recessed cans all turned on at once reads as a room the homeowner has flooded with light. The dragon sees the brightness and pulls away from the room's center, settling instead at the room's corners. The fix is either to install a dimmer on the ceiling fixture (most modern fixtures are dimmer-compatible with a $15 swap), to swap the floor lamp's bulb to a warm 2700K, or to remove one of the recessed cans from the circuit. The layered read is the read the dragon settles into, and the flat read is the read the dragon avoids.

Defect two: a fixture that under-lights a corner. The second defect is a room with a dark corner the dragon avoids even when the rest of the room is well-lit. A living room with the floor lamp, table lamp, and candle all clustered in one half of the room, a bedroom with the bedside lamp on one side and bare socket on the other, a dining room with the chandelier dim and the sideboard dark. The dragon sees the dark corner and reads it as a feature the homeowner has skipped, and the dragon's settling pace avoids the corner in favor of the lit half. The fix is either to add a fixture to the dark corner (a small floor lamp, a wall sconce, a second candle) or to move one of the existing fixtures from the bright half to the dark half (a corner-balanced lighting layout reads as a settled room, an unbalanced lighting layout reads as a room the homeowner has only half-furnished).

Defect three: a candle left burning in an empty room. The third defect is the most subtle. A candle the homeowner lit at dinner and forgot to blow out when the family left the table reads as a flame the dragon sees but the homeowner has abandoned. A candle burning through the night in an empty bedroom reads as a room the dragon should not settle in, because the dragon's settling pace is the homeowner's settling pace and the homeowner is not in the room. The fix is a five-second check at bedtime: any candle lit in a room the homeowner has left for more than fifteen minutes should be blown out. The candle's value is in the homeowner's presence, not in the candle's flame; a candle the homeowner relights at the next sitting is the read the dragon follows, and a candle left burning is the read the dragon avoids.

The chart-pair bulb read

The six indoor lighting typologies carry the Five Elements across the indoor evening. A bedside lamp at a warm 2700K reads as Fire (the settling element). A table lamp at a warm 3000K reads as Fire leaning toward Earth (the settling-then-stabilizing element). A ceiling fixture at a neutral 4000K reads as Metal (the receiving-then-releasing element). A candle with a visible flame reads as Fire doubled (the gathering element). A wall sconce at a warm 2700K aimed at the wall reads as Fire reflected (the framing element). A floor lamp at a cool 5000K reads as Metal leaning toward Water (the awakening element). The chart's Day Master tells the homeowner which element their chart needs more of, and the indoor lighting typology the homeowner places in each room can be chosen to feed the chart's missing element.

A chart with a Wood Day Master (Jia or Yi) benefits from warm-toned table lamps and bedside lamps (Fire feeds Wood through the Sheng cycle, and the warm bulb signals the Wood chart's missing Fire). A chart with a Fire Day Master (Bing or Ding) benefits from warm-toned ceiling fixtures and visible-flame candles (Fire feeds Fire, and the doubling is welcome rather than exhausting for a Fire chart). A chart with an Earth Day Master (Wu or Ji) benefits from neutral-toned wall sconces and warm-toned floor lamps (Earth is fed by Fire, and the sconce's reflection reads as Earth's stabilizing quality). A chart with a Metal Day Master (Geng or Xin) benefits from cool-toned ceiling fixtures and cool-toned table lamps (Metal is fed by Earth, and the cool tone signals Metal's reflective quality). A chart with a Water Day Master (Ren or Gui) benefits from cool-toned bedside lamps and visible-flame candles (Metal feeds Water, and the candle's flame paired with a cool bulb signals the Water chart's missing Metal). The chart-pair bulb route is the read the homeowner who has done yesterday's outdoor lighting piece and today's indoor lighting piece can do in a single evening walk.

The seasonal shift cross-check for late July

The indoor lighting read is different in summer than in autumn, and the seasonal shift cross-check is the read the homeowner does at the solar-term checkpoint. The current checkpoint is late July, three weeks after Li Qiu (July 7) and the Fire-to-Earth handoff. The Earth month is now established, and the read is to keep the indoor lighting's color temperature leaning warm even as the indoor temperature falls. A living room at 2700K in mid-July is a living room at 3000K in late July, and the homeowner who has not reset the bulbs has lost the homeowner's settling pace for the cooling weather. The lean-into read is either to swap the bulbs at the start of each season (a simple act that takes five minutes and one trip to the hardware store) or to install smart bulbs the homeowner can shift from the phone.

The next checkpoint is Li Qiu + 45 days, around August 22, when the Earth month settles into Metal preview. At that checkpoint the read shifts: indoor lighting layouts that have been feeding the Earth element (the summer indoor lighting reads) shift to layouts that feed the Metal element (the autumn indoor lighting reads). A bedside lamp at warm 2700K reads the same. A table lamp at warm 3000K shifts to a neutral 3500K (Metal's reflective quality). A ceiling fixture at neutral 4000K shifts to a cool 4500K. The cross-check in late August is to re-read the indoor lighting's color temperature against the seasonal element the home is moving into, and the homeowner who has done the late-July cross-check knows which bulbs to shift before the late-August cross-check.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to have an indoor lighting layout for the read to apply?
No. The read applies as a check of the four room rules even when the home has no deliberate indoor lighting plan. The homeowner who has done yesterday's outdoor lighting piece and has only one indoor fixture (a ceiling fixture in the living room, a bedside lamp in the bedroom) has begun the indoor lighting read. The homeowner who adds a candle on the dining table, a floor lamp in the corner, a wall sconce above the bed has layered the read. The indoor lighting is an addition, not a requirement, just as the outdoor lighting was an addition to the nine gate, path, and water reads.
What if I rent and cannot install wall sconces?
Use portable fixtures that plug into any outlet. A pair of clip-on reading lights on the headboard, a small floor lamp in the corner, a plug-in wall sconce with a cord cover (the homeowner hides the cord along the baseboard with a $5 plastic cover). The portable fixtures read the same as the permanent fixtures for the dragon's indoor settling pace, with one caveat: portable fixtures can be moved by the homeowner, and the homeowner who moves them weekly has read a different home each week. The lean-into read is to settle the portable fixtures in one position and leave them there for at least a season so the dragon's pace is consistent.
Can I use smart bulbs that change color temperature?
Yes, with the same caveat the outdoor lighting piece gave yesterday. Smart bulbs are a workable compromise for the homeowner who wants to shift the lighting color across the season. The lean-into read is to keep the warm color (2700K-3000K) during the summer months, the neutral color (3500K-4000K) during the autumn months, and the cool color (4500K-5000K) during the winter months. A smart bulb that the homeowner sets to a different color every night has read a different home every night, and the dragon's settling pace is inconsistent. The seasonal shift is correct; the daily shift is a defect.
How does the indoor lighting read interact with the outdoor lighting read from yesterday?
The outdoor lighting walks the dragon up to the door, and the indoor lighting walks the dragon through the door to the bedside. The read at the threshold is the read both pieces share: a porch light at warm 2700K and an entry-hall sconce at warm 2700K is a continuous read the dragon follows without pause. A porch light at cool 4000K and an entry-hall sconce at warm 2700K is a jarring read the dragon hesitates at. The homeowner's evening read is the seamless handoff from one piece to the other, and the color-temperature match at the threshold is the read both pieces' chart-pair walk checks.
What color temperature should I use for the bedside lamp?
Warm 2700K for most sleepers. A 2700K bedside lamp reads as Fire leaning toward Earth, and the bedside is the Kun quadrant (the homeowner's settling position). Fire in the Kun quadrant stabilizes the Kun without weakening it, and the warm tone signals settling rather than alerting. A 3000K bedside lamp reads as Earth leaning toward Metal, which is correct for the late-summer Earth month but signals settling slightly later than 2700K. A 4000K bedside lamp reads as Metal and signals alerting rather than settling, and the dragon's settling pace shifts toward morning wakefulness rather than evening rest. The lean-into read is 2700K at the bedside, 3000K at the side table, and 3500K at the dresser (the dresser's working position supports a slightly cooler tone than the bedside's settling position).
Should the candles stay lit through dinner?
Yes, with the caveat from defect three. Candles lit at dinner and blown out when the family leaves the table read as a settling element the dragon follows. Candles left burning through the night in an empty dining room read as a flame the dragon sees but the homeowner has abandoned. The lean-into read is to light the dining candles when the family sits, and to blow them out when the family clears the plates. The candle's value is in the homeowner's presence at the table, and the candle that goes out with the family is the read the dragon remembers at the next dinner.
Does the indoor lighting read change for an apartment?
Yes, in two specific ways. An apartment shares walls with neighbors, so wall sconces aimed outward (into a shared wall) are a defect (the dragon's read of the apartment is partly the neighbor's read, and a sconce that washes the shared wall washes the neighbor's bedroom). An apartment has no entry hall (a hallway that opens directly into the living room), so the rule-one placement shifts to the hallway's far end rather than to a dedicated entry hall. The other two-thirds of the indoor lighting reads carry across property types unchanged. The three-defect walk catches the apartment's specific defects the same way it catches the house's defects.

What to do this evening

Walk the home at dusk and name the indoor lighting typology in each of the four key rooms (entry hall, living room, bedroom, kitchen). Check the four placement rules against the room's quadrant. Walk the three-defect checklist: a fixture that over-lights the room, a fixture that under-lights a corner, a candle left burning in an empty room. Choose one defect and one deliberate act: install a dimmer on the over-bright ceiling fixture, move a floor lamp into the dark corner, blow out the candle the family forgot in the dining room. If the home has no indoor lighting layer beyond the ceiling fixtures, choose one room and add a single warm-toned table lamp as the read's first addition.
