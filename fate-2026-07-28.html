Outdoor Water Feature Feng Shui: How Ponds, Fountains, and Basins Change the July Property Read

The July thread has now walked seven outdoor reads: the three horizontal gates at the front of the property, the vertical gate between floors, the in-between gate on the balcony, the wide gate in the courtyard, the narrow gate in the side yard, and the path surface under the dragon's feet. Today's piece walks the feature those seven reads all reference but never read themselves: the outdoor water feature. A standalone pillar piece on the five water-feature types, the four placement rules, the three defects, and the seasonal shift cross-check for late July.

Published: 2026-07-28 · Reading time: about 13 minutes

Why the water feature is the read the seven outdoor pieces all reference

The three horizontal gates at the front of the property (the three-gate walk from July 11) read the lines the dragon crosses on arrival. The vertical gate at the stairway (July 17) read the line the dragon climbs. The in-between gate at the balcony (July 18) read the half-step where the dragon pauses. The wide gate at the courtyard (July 19) read the volume the dragon settles into. The narrow gate at the side yard (July 21) read the connector the dragon crosses a hundred times a week. The path surface (yesterday, July 27) read the strip the dragon walks on between gates. Seven reads, each one timed at a different speed in the dragon's visit.

What all seven reads share, and what all seven skip, is the water feature the dragon settles next to. The horizontal gate reads at the front of the property reference a fountain the homeowner has put near the front door or a pond the homeowner has built in the front garden, but the gate read does not say what the water feature does to the gate's read. The vertical gate read at the stairway references a fountain on the landing between floors, but the stairway read does not say whether the water feature helps the dragon climb or stops the dragon in the middle. The path read at the side yard references a water feature the homeowner has placed at the end of a path, but the path read does not say whether the dead-end feature is a defect or a deliberate pause.

The water feature is its own read because the water feature is the only outdoor element where the dragon interacts with the element directly. The dragon walks through a horizontal gate without noticing the gate. The dragon climbs a stairway without thinking about the stairway. The dragon crosses a path without reading the path. The dragon hears water and crosses to where the water is. The water feature is the read the dragon moves toward rather than the read the dragon moves through, and the dragon's natural movement toward water is the read the foundation articles cannot measure without naming the water feature's typology, the water feature's placement, and the water feature's seasonal state.

The water-feature read, in one sentenceRead the water-feature typology (still water, falling water, flowing water, fountain, or basin — set by the feature's shape and motion, not the homeowner's choice). Read the water-feature placement against the four compass quadrants (the front, the back, the side, the central courtyard) and the seven outdoor reads the July thread walked. Read the water-feature state (clean, dirty, dry, leaking, or overgrown) on a thirty-day check. Fix the three defects in order: a water feature that has stopped flowing, a water feature that runs along a wall the dragon cannot reach, a water feature that sits in shade and grows algae. The homeowner who has done the four-step checklist has read the water element the seven outdoor reads all referenced.

The five water-feature typologies

Every outdoor water feature falls into one of five typologies, set by the feature's shape and motion rather than by the feature's size, material, or expense. The shape is fixed by the property's lot, the homeowner's existing grounds, and the budget the homeowner has set. The homeowner cannot change the typology without rebuilding the feature, and the read is to use the typology the feature already has.

Still pondSettlingAn enclosed basin of standing water, usually with a liner, a recirculating pump, and a planting at the edge (water lilies, iris, reeds, lotus). Reads as the most contemplative typology because the dragon hears no motion at the surface and settles at the edge to watch. The lean-into read is to keep the surface clear of debris (a pond with leaves on the surface reads as a dirty mirror, not a still pond) and to keep the planting rooted in pots rather than in the liner so the planting can be lifted for cleaning.
Falling waterCleansingA vertical drop of water from one level to another, usually a waterfall over a stone, a cascade down a wall, or a sheet of water from a spout into a basin. Reads as the most active typology because the dragon hears the falling sound from a distance and follows the sound to the source. The lean-into read is to keep the drop short (a fall of more than four feet reads as a wall of noise rather than a cleansing sound) and to keep the catch-basin deep enough that the splash does not drain the water level.
Flowing waterCirculatingA horizontal stream of water along a channel, usually a small river between two ponds, a creek along a property edge, or a finished channel with a recirculating pump. Reads as the most extended typology because the dragon walks alongside the flow and hesitates at the bends. The lean-into read is to keep the channel shallow (a channel deeper than a foot reads as a drainage ditch rather than a stream) and to keep the bends gentle (sharp bends at the channel crossings stop the dragon at each turn).
FountainSprayingA vertical spray of water from a single point, usually a center jet in a basin, a wall-mounted spout, or a tiered fountain with several jets. Reads as the most visible typology because the dragon sees the spray from a distance and moves toward the basin. The lean-into read is to keep the spray short (a jet that exceeds the basin's diameter reads as a fountain that overshoots, not a fountain that contains) and to keep the basin's rim planted (a bare rim reads as a bowl of water, not a fountain).
BasinReceivingA simple vessel of water without a motion, usually a stone bowl, a ceramic jar, a half-barrel, or a copper pot. Reads as the most portable typology because the basin can be moved to the read the property needs (a basin near the front door catches the dragon on arrival, a basin on the patio catches the dragon at the family gathering, a basin by the back door catches the dragon on return). The lean-into read is to keep the basin sized to the surface (a basin that overpowers the surface reads as a feature the placement was built around, not a feature the homeowner placed) and to refill the water every two days so the basin reads as a living feature rather than a stale one.

Most properties with one or more water features have more than one typology. A suburban lot might have a still pond at the back garden and a fountain at the front door. A courtyard property might have a falling water feature along the courtyard wall and a basin on the central table. Each typology carries its own read, and the homeowner reads each feature separately. The water-feature reads together form the full outdoor water read the homeowner needs once the seven gate and path reads are done.

The four placement rules

Once the typology is named, the read looks at the placement the feature has on the property. The four placement rules are the four compass quadrants the foundation reads have always used, and the rule is the same in every tradition: the dragon reads water differently depending on where the water sits. The homeowner's job is to know which quadrant the water feature occupies and what that quadrant means for the read.

Rule one: water at the front of the property reads as invitation. The first rule is to place one water feature at the front of the property, within sight of the front gate or the front door. The front of the property is the Wood quadrant (the dragon's first arrival), and water in the Wood quadrant feeds the Wood element without weakening it. A fountain near the front door, a basin on the front porch, a fountain in the front garden are all valid placements. The dragon hears the water as it approaches the gate and slows its pace to settle. The lean-into read is to keep the water feature at the front set at a low height (a basin at ankle level, a fountain at knee level) so the dragon hears the water without being struck by a tall spray.

Rule two: water at the back of the property reads as stability. The second rule is to avoid a heavy water feature at the back of the property, especially a still pond or a deep basin. The back of the property is the Kun quadrant (the receiving position), and water in the Kun quadrant drains the receiving energy rather than stabilizes it. A small basin at the back door is fine. A large pond at the back of the garden captures the dragon's pace and prevents the dragon from returning to the house at the end of the day. The lean-into read is to either keep the back water feature small (a basin, a half-barrel, a single pot) or to position it at the property's edge rather than at the center of the back garden.

Rule three: water at the side of the property reads as connector. The third rule is to place one water feature at the side of the property, between the front gate and the back garden. The side of the property is the Gen and Xun quadrants (the corner transitions), and water in the side quadrants connects the dragon's arrival to the dragon's destination. A fountain in the side garden, a falling water feature along the side wall, a basin halfway down the side path are all valid placements. The lean-into read is to keep the side water feature positioned along the path the dragon walks (the side yard connector from July 21) rather than off-path in a corner the side yard's read does not check.

Rule four: water in the central courtyard reads as the heart. The fourth rule is to place one water feature in the central courtyard if the property has a courtyard. The central courtyard is the Taiji position (the center of the property's compass), and water in the center reads as the property's heart. A still pond at the courtyard's center, a tiered fountain on the courtyard's central axis, a basin on the courtyard's central table all read as the heart. The lean-into read is to keep the central water feature sized to the courtyard (a courtyard feature that is too small reads as a placeholder, a feature that is too large reads as a feature the courtyard was built around) and to keep the water's surface free of debris so the dragon sees the courtyard's heart without a distraction.

The three defects

The three-defect walk is what the homeowner does once the typology is named, the four placement rules are checked, and the property's existing water features are read. The walk takes about thirty minutes and covers the three defects the water-feature read always catches. The defects are listed in the order they appear in the homeowner's check.

Defect one: a water feature that has stopped flowing. The first defect is the most common and the most damaging. A water feature that has stopped flowing (a fountain with a broken pump, a falling water feature with a clogged spout, a flowing water feature with a broken recirculator) reads as a still pond with no motion, and the still pond's typology does not match the feature's design. A broken fountain looks like a feature the homeowner has abandoned, and the dragon reads it as a defect even if the homeowner has not noticed. The fix is to repair the pump, clear the spout, or replace the recirculator within the month. The homeowner who has stopped using a water feature should drain it, fill it with soil, and plant a slow-growing shrub there rather than leave the broken feature as a visible decay.

Defect two: a water feature that runs along a wall the dragon cannot reach. The second defect is a water feature that sits in a position the dragon can hear but cannot reach. A falling water feature along a wall the dragon cannot approach, a fountain in a corner surrounded by dense planting, a basin on a high wall the dragon cannot see. The dragon hears the water and tries to settle at the feature, but the placement prevents the dragon from reaching the water's edge. The fix is to clear the path to the feature (cut a path through the dense planting, lower the wall, move the basin to a surface the dragon can stand at) so the dragon can reach the water and settle.

Defect three: a water feature that sits in shade and grows algae. The third defect is a water feature that sits in full shade for most of the day and has grown algae on the surface. A shaded water feature with algae reads as a dirty pond rather than a still pond, and the dragon's read defaults to the algae rather than to the water. The fix is either to move the feature to a sunnier position (a still pond needs at least four hours of direct sun to keep the algae in check) or to add a floating plant (a water lily, a lotus, a small iris) that shades the surface and competes with the algae for nutrients. The floating plant is the read the homeowner can do in a weekend without moving the feature.

The seasonal shift cross-check for late July

The water-feature read is different in summer than in winter, and the seasonal shift cross-check is the read the homeowner does at the solar-term checkpoint. The current checkpoint is late July, three weeks after Li Qiu (July 7) and the Fire-to-Earth handoff. The Earth month is now established, and the read is to keep the water feature's evaporation in balance. A water feature that loses more than an inch of water per day in July's heat is a feature that drains the dragon's pace rather than settles it, and the lean-into read is to either add water in the morning (a five-gallon bucket every two days is enough for most residential features) or to install a top-off valve that adds water automatically.

The next checkpoint is Li Qiu + 45 days, around August 22, when the Earth month settles into Metal preview. At that checkpoint the read shifts: water features that have been cleansing the Fire element (the summer water reads) shift to features that feed the Metal element (the autumn water reads). A still pond reads the same. A fountain shifts from a cleansing motion to a circulating motion. The cross-check in late August is to re-read the feature's motion against the seasonal element the property is moving into, and the homeowner who has done the late-July cross-check knows which features to adjust before the late-August cross-check.

How the water-feature read ties back to the chart

The five outdoor typologies carry the Five Elements across the surface of the property. A still pond reads as Water (the receiving element). A falling water feature reads as Metal (the releasing element). A flowing water feature reads as Wood (the growing element). A fountain reads as Fire (the rising element). A basin reads as Earth (the stabilizing element). The chart's Day Master tells the homeowner which element their chart needs more of, and the water-feature typology the homeowner places on the property can be chosen to feed the chart's missing element.

A chart with a Wood Day Master (Jia or Yi) benefits from a flowing water feature (Water feeds Wood). A chart with a Fire Day Master (Bing or Ding) benefits from a fountain or a basin (Wood and Earth feed Fire). A chart with an Earth Day Master (Wu or Ji) benefits from a still pond or a basin (Earth is fed by Fire, and the basin's stability mirrors the Earth). A chart with a Metal Day Master (Geng or Xin) benefits from a falling water feature (Metal is fed by Earth). A chart with a Water Day Master (Ren or Gui) benefits from a flowing water feature (Metal feeds Water). The chart-pair water route is the read the homeowner who has done the seven outdoor reads and the chart read can do in an afternoon.

The water feature is the read the dragon moves toward. The seven outdoor reads the July thread walked were the reads the dragon moves through. Today's piece is the read the dragon has been settling near all along.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to have a water feature for the read to apply?
No. The read applies as a check of the four placement quadrants even when the property has no water feature. The homeowner who has done the seven outdoor reads and has no water feature has read the property without the Water element, and the read is honest. The water feature is an addition, not a requirement. A homeowner who adds a single basin in the first available quadrant (the front of the property is the most common starting point) has begun the water-feature read.
What if my water feature is broken and I cannot repair it this month?
Drain it, fill it with soil, and plant a slow-growing shrub there. A broken water feature reads as a defect, and the lean-into fix is to remove the feature rather than leave it broken. A planted shrub in the same spot reads as a deliberate choice, and the dragon's read settles at the planting rather than at the broken feature. The homeowner can rebuild the water feature in a future month when the budget allows.
Can a water feature be inside a screened porch or a covered patio?
Yes, with a caveat. A covered water feature reads as a basin or a small fountain rather than as a falling water feature, because the covered ceiling prevents the dragon from hearing the sound from outside. The lean-into read is to keep the covered water feature visible (a basin on a side table, a small fountain on a central stand) so the dragon sees the water without relying on the sound. The covered water feature is a compromise, not the same read as an outdoor feature, and the homeowner should know the difference.
How does the water-feature read interact with the path read from yesterday?
The path is the surface the dragon walks on between gates, and the water feature is the feature the dragon settles at when the path ends at water. A path that ends at a still pond, a fountain, or a basin reads as a deliberate destination. A path that ends at a dead-end feature without water (yesterday's defect three) reads as a defect. The water feature is the read that turns a dead-end path into a destination, and the homeowner who has done yesterday's path read and today's water-feature read can connect the two in a single afternoon walk.
My property has a creek running along the back edge. Is that a water feature?
Yes, and the read is the most powerful one in the water-feature set. A creek running along the property's edge reads as a flowing water feature that the homeowner did not place, and the dragon's read is to settle at the creek's edge for an extended pause. The lean-into read is to keep the creek's edge clean (a planted edge with no debris, a visible bank the dragon can reach) and to add a single bench or a single stone at the spot where the dragon naturally pauses. The homeowner who has a creek has a built-in water feature that does not need maintenance.
What about a swimming pool?
A swimming pool reads as a large still pond, and the read is similar to the still pond's read with two caveats. A pool meant for the family's use (a pool the family swims in every week) reads as a living Water element. A pool the family never uses (a pool that has been closed for two seasons, a pool covered with a tarp) reads as a defect, and the lean-into fix is either to commit to using the pool weekly or to drain and fill it the way a broken fountain is drained. The pool is the largest water feature on most properties, and the read is the most visible.
