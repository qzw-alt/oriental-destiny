Outdoor Feng Shui Close-Out: The Whole-Property Walk Before Li Qiu on July 7

Four articles this month, four outdoor rooms walked one by one. The close-out walks them all at once, because the homeowner who treats them as four separate pieces is the homeowner who will lose the seasonal correction at the property's edge. A two-day checklist for July 5 and July 6, anchored to Li Qiu on July 7, with the undo list that decides which July cures stay through August and which get pulled.

Published July 5, 2026 · 11 min read

Why the close-out is its own article

The July thread has walked the four outdoor Fu Wei positions in the order the homeowner can change them. The front garden on July 1, the most changed. The back garden on July 2, less changed. The balcony on July 3, changed by clearing. The rooftop on July 4, changed by negotiation with the building. The thread was deliberately written as four rooms rather than one long piece, because each room reads differently and a homeowner who reads only the room they own is the homeowner who gets the seasonal correction that room can carry, and misses the rest. The thread-continuity sentence in each article pointed back to the previous one so the homeowner who landed mid-series could pick up the thread. Today's article does the inverse. It walks all four rooms as one property, in two days, with one undo list, because the Fire-to-Earth transition on July 7 does not read one room at a time. The transition reads the whole property.

Li Qiu, which falls on July 7 in 2026, is the seasonal checkpoint that most homeowner-level articles skip. The Fire Month has been running since early May. Li Qiu is the moment the Fire hands off to Earth, and the homeowner who has been running Fire-leaning cures all month is the homeowner whose outdoor rooms will feel off by August unless the cures are walked back before the transition. The walk-back is not a fire-and-forget operation. It is a sequence: walk the four rooms on July 5, undo on July 6, observe on July 7. Three days, four rooms, one transition. The homeowner who treats the close-out as a single afternoon is the homeowner who will pull the wrong cure on the wrong day.

The thread-continuity reason this article exists: the four previous articles each closed with a "What to undo at Li Qiu" section. Those undo sections were written for the individual room. This article is the property-wide undo list, with a single sequence and a single calendar. A homeowner who only reads one of the four July articles can follow that article's undo. A homeowner who reads the series can follow this one instead. The two readings are compatible.

The four outdoor rooms read individually through the month. They read as one property at Li Qiu. The transition does not happen room by room. It happens at the property's edge, on a single day, and the homeowner whose outdoor rooms are aligned that day is the homeowner whose chart reads from the front gate to the rooftop by August.

The July 5 walk: four rooms, one perimeter

This is the day the homeowner reads the property as one shape. Start at the front gate. Walk the perimeter in the direction the homeowner usually walks it (clockwise in most cultures, counterclockwise in a few, the cycles accept either). Note each of the four outdoor rooms as the homeowner passes through it. The point of the walk is not to do more cures. The point is to see which July cures are still in place and which July cures the season has already started to undo. The Fire Month is fading. The Earth Month is not yet in charge. The two days between them are the moment the property's edge is most readable, and the homeowner who can read it cleanly is the homeowner who knows what to keep and what to pull.

  1. Front garden, July 5 read. The Fire-leaning red blooms, the warm-toned solar lanterns, the citronella candles, the welcome mat in a Fire color. Most of these will still be in place. The question for the front garden on July 5 is: which one is the homeowner most attached to, and which one can go without a fight. The Fire cures that have done the most work in June are the ones the cycles have already absorbed. Those are the ones to keep through the transition, because pulling them before July 7 is pulling them out of the season's hands. The Fire cures that were decorative in June (the third lantern, the fifth red bloom, the second welcome mat) are the ones to pull on July 6. The front garden is the easiest room to read on the close-out walk because it is the most visible from the street.
  2. Back garden, July 5 read. The water feature, the rock garden, the shaded seating area, the climbing vines on the back fence. The back garden reads slower than the front garden because it is hidden from public view, and the cures the homeowner placed in May are still doing their work. The question for the back garden on July 5 is whether the water feature should be turned off, turned down, or left running through the transition. A water feature that has run all month is a Water cure the cycles have fully absorbed. Leave it on. A water feature that was added in late June is a Water cure the cycles are still integrating. Turn it off on July 6 and bring it back in August. The back garden is the room where July 5 is mostly an observation day.
  3. Balcony, July 5 read. The single tall plant, the wind chime, the railing decorations, the small metal anchor. The balcony was the easiest room to clear on July 3 because it had the least stuff. The July 5 read for the balcony is whether the cleared state has held. A balcony that drifted back to cluttered in two days is a balcony the cycles read as needing one more pass. The July 6 work for the balcony is the second pass, not the first undo. The single tall plant is the cure to keep. The wind chime is the cure to silence through the transition. The metal anchor stays.
  4. Rooftop, July 5 read. The corner finished on July 4, the metal anchor, the late-Fire lighting, the temporary shade structure. The rooftop reads slowest of the four rooms because the homeowner reaches it least often. The July 5 read for the rooftop is whether the homeowner has been up there since July 4. If yes, the rooftop is in working state. If no, climb up on July 5 for ten minutes and look. The rooftop is the room where the close-out walk is most likely to surface one missed item, because the rooftop is the room the homeowner is most likely to have skipped. The Fairy lights, the candle garden, the pop-up canopy. These are the rooftop items the July 6 undo needs to address.

The July 5 walk is not a work day. It is a read day. The point is to come back inside with a list of the items that are still in place and the items that drifted. The list does not need to be long. Three to five items per room is the typical list for a property that was actively walked through July. Eight to ten items per room is the typical list for a property where the cures were placed in late June and the homeowner has not looked at them since.

The July 6 undo: order of operations across the four rooms

The July 6 undo is a work day. The point is to pull the items the July 5 read flagged as decorative rather than curative, in the order that minimizes re-work. The order matters here more than it does for a single-room undo, because the four outdoor rooms share items sometimes (a wind chime bought for the front garden and moved to the balcony, a string of fairy lights shared between the back garden and the rooftop). The homeowner who pulls without checking the shared list is the homeowner who will end up with one string of lights in the trash and one still on the rooftop. The shared list is short, but it should be written down on the morning of July 6 before the first pull.

The order below walks the property from the most public room to the least public, which is the order that keeps the homeowner's decisions consistent across the day. Pulling in any other order is fine; pulling in this order means the homeowner ends the day in the room where they are least observed (the rooftop) and can pause there for the seasonal observation that closes the close-out.

  1. Front garden, July 6. Pull the late-added Fire decorations. The third lantern, the second welcome mat, the warm-toned doormat if it was added after June 15. The single red bloom that has done the most work (usually the one at the center of the front bed, the one the homeowner notices first) stays through the transition. The other two or three red blooms stay too, because pulling all of them at once is pulling the front garden out of the season's hands. Leave one bloom if there is only one, two if there are two, half if there are five or more. The rule is "some Fire, not all Fire." The Earth Month is the month of "some Fire" at the front garden, because the front garden is the room the street reads first, and the street expects some Fire color even in July.
  2. Back garden, July 6. Turn down the water feature if it was added in late June. Leave it running if it has been on all month. Pull the wind chime that was added to the back fence after the original July 2 article. The back garden is the room where the July 6 undo is smallest, because the back garden's cures were mostly placed in May and the cycles have already absorbed them. The work here is the late additions, not the originals.
  3. Balcony, July 6. Silence the wind chime for the transition. Keep the single tall plant. Keep the metal anchor. The balcony is the room where the July 6 undo is the cleanest, because the balcony had the least to undo in the first place. The wind-chime silence is a three-day silence (July 6 through July 8), and the chime returns on July 9 with the same Metal anchor it had in late June.
  4. Rooftop, July 6. Pull the late-Fire lighting. The fairy lights, the candle garden, the portable fire pit. Take down the temporary shade structure if it was added after June 1. The rooftop is the room where the July 6 undo is the most physical, because the rooftop items are the heaviest and the most likely to need a trip down the building's stairs or elevator. Save the rooftop for last so the homeowner ends the day without a heavy object to carry back up.

The total time for the July 6 undo is forty-five minutes to two hours for most properties, depending on how much was added in late June. The work is mostly pulling and turning down, not building and turning on. The Earth Month begins with a quieter outdoor space than the Fire Month ended with, and the quiet is part of the season.

The shared-item rule Before pulling anything on July 6, write down the items that moved between rooms during July. A wind chime bought for the front garden and moved to the balcony. A string of fairy lights shared between the back garden and the rooftop. A planter that started on the back garden and ended on the front porch. The shared-item list is usually three to five items long for a property that was actively walked. The list prevents double-pulls and missed pulls, which are the two errors the close-out is most likely to produce.

The July 7 observation: what to read on Li Qiu itself

Li Qiu in 2026 lands on a Tuesday. The cycles hand off between midnight and dawn, and the homeowner who is awake for the handoff is the homeowner who will see the property's edge change color, in the same way the homeowner who is awake for the sunrise on Xia Zhi sees the summer solstice land. Most homeowners are not awake at midnight on Li Qiu, and the cycles accept the morning observation as the working read. The morning of July 7 is the moment to walk the property one more time, with the undo work from July 6 already in place. The point of this third walk is to notice what the season did on its own overnight, and what the homeowner's undo set up for the season to do.

The morning of July 7 is also the moment to commit to the Earth Month's outdoor state. A homeowner who finished the July 6 undo and walked the property on July 7 has set the property's edge for the next six weeks. A homeowner who skipped the July 7 walk has set it for the next two weeks, and the Earth Month will read as half-aligned until the homeowner comes back to it. The morning walk is fifteen minutes for most properties. The point is not the duration. The point is the read.

What the cycles do overnight

The Fire-to-Earth handoff happens at the property's edge first, then moves inward. The front garden is the first room to feel the handoff. The back garden is the second. The balcony is the third. The rooftop is the last, because the rooftop is the room the sky reads, and the sky does not hand off until the ground does. A homeowner who walks the front garden at 7am on July 7 will see the season's change. A homeowner who walks only the rooftop will not see it until noon. The order of the read is the order of the rooms, and the homeowner who wants the full Li Qiu read needs to walk at least two rooms in the morning, the front garden and one other.

The morning read is mostly visual. The Fire decorations the homeowner kept through the transition will look slightly different on July 7 than they looked on July 5. Not because the homeowner changed them, but because the cycles around them changed. A red bloom that was reading as Fire on July 5 is reading as Earth-on-Fire on July 7. The difference is subtle, and most homeowners will not see it unless they are looking for it. The homeowner who is looking for it is the homeowner who has been reading this series and knows what to watch. The homeowner who is not looking for it will still feel the change by the second week of July, when the rooms start to settle into the new seasonal rhythm.

The day-master-by-element close-out: who undoes what

The close-out reads slightly differently for each of the five Day Masters, in the same way each July article read differently for each chart. This is the part most homeowner-level articles skip, because they assume the cycles are the same for every homeowner. The cycles are the same. What changes is the chart, and a Fire-heavy chart whose outdoor rooms are stripped of all Fire on July 6 is asking the property's edge to fight the chart. The strip-back needs to match the chart, not just the season.

A Wood-heavy chart — Jia or Yi Day Master — benefits from a slower strip-back on July 6. Wood feeds Fire, and a Wood-heavy chart whose outdoor Fire is pulled all at once is a chart that loses the Sheng direction it was relying on. The recommendation is to keep two Fire anchors through July 7 (one in the front garden, one on the rooftop or balcony, depending on which room the chart reads through) and pull the rest. The Wood-heavy chart wants Fire in moderation through the transition, because the Wood-heavy chart's Earth Month is read through the Sheng cycle (Wood → Fire → Earth), and a full strip-back breaks the chain.

A Fire-heavy chart — Bing or Ding Day Master — benefits from the cleanest possible strip-back on July 6. The Fire-heavy chart has too much seasonal energy, and the Fire-to-Earth transition is the moment the chart catches a break. Pull the Fire decorations as completely as the homeowner's tolerance allows. Leave one anchor if there is only one. The Fire-heavy chart's Earth Month is read through the Ke cycle (Fire melts Metal, Metal produces Water, Water calms Fire), and the strip-back on July 6 is the move that lets the Ke direction settle in.

An Earth-heavy chart — Wu or Ji Day Master — benefits from a metal anchor added on July 6, not just a Fire strip-back. The Earth-heavy chart's outdoor rooms are already producing Earth (the ground, the planters, the stoneware), and the Earth Month makes that stronger. The cure is one metal anchor per room, in the same way each July article recommended. The strip-back on July 6 is secondary. The metal anchor is primary.

A Metal-heavy chart — Geng or Xin Day Master — benefits from the strip-back plus a small water feature confirmation. The Metal-heavy chart's Earth Month is read through the Sheng direction (Earth produces Metal, Metal produces Water), and a water feature in the back garden or on the balcony confirms the chain. The strip-back on July 6 should preserve the metal anchor in each room and reduce the Fire decorations around it, not replace the anchor entirely.

A Water-heavy chart — Ren or Gui Day Master — benefits from a quiet strip-back, the kind that does the work without making the property feel empty. The Water-heavy chart is the chart most sensitive to sudden change, and a July 6 strip-back that pulls five items in two hours is asking the property's edge to shift faster than the chart can follow. Spread the strip-back over July 5 and July 6, not just July 6, and let the rooftop stay quiet through the transition rather than be cleared in a single morning.

The Fire-to-Earth transition is not a clean line on the calendar. It is a two-day strip-back, a one-day observation, and a six-week settling-in. The homeowner who treats it as a single afternoon is the homeowner whose outdoor rooms will read slightly out of phase for the rest of July. The homeowner who treats it as a sequence will read the property's edge in sync with the season by the second week of August.

The undo list, written once for the whole property

The seven-item list below is the close-out for a property that was actively walked through July. A property that was only partially walked (front garden done, balcony skipped) should adapt the list to whichever rooms were touched, and accept that the rooms that were skipped will need their own observation pass on July 7. The list is not exhaustive. It is the seven moves the close-out most often misses.

  • Sheng Keep one Fire anchor per room through July 7. The single red bloom in the front garden, the warm-toned doormat, the candle at the back porch, the rooftop lantern that has been on all month. Pull the rest of the Fire decorations on July 6. The one-anchor rule is what stops the property's edge from feeling empty after the strip-back.
  • Ke Silence the wind chimes on July 6 and July 7. The wind chimes were the Metal cure most often overused in the June Fire Month, and the Fire-to-Earth transition is the moment to give them a rest. Bring them back on July 9, after the cycles have settled into the Earth element. A wind chime that rings through the transition is a Metal sound that interrupts the Earth's settling.
  • Hold Leave the water features as they are. A water feature that has been running all month is integrated into the property's edge. Turning it off on July 6 is removing a cure the cycles have already absorbed. The water feature stays on through July, and the homeowner decides in August whether to keep it through the autumn transition.
  • Sheng Add one metal anchor per room if the room was not assigned one in July. The metal anchor is the Earth Month's load-bearing cure for the outdoor rooms, and a room that did not get one in July should get one on July 6, before the transition. A brass planter, a steel lantern, a small metal bench. The anchor can be borrowed from another room for the transition week.
  • Ke Take down the late-added shade structures. The pop-up canopy, the temporary shade sail, the beach umbrella. These were Wood decorations added to fight the Fire Month's heat, and the Fire-to-Earth transition is the moment they stop earning their place. A permanent metal pergola stays. A temporary shade structure comes down.
  • Hold Move nothing between rooms on July 6 or July 7. The shared-item rule from earlier in this article applies most strongly on the transition day itself. Moving a planter from the back garden to the front garden on July 7 is moving a cure across the property's edge in the middle of the handoff, and the cycles read the move as an interruption. Save the moves for July 8 or later.
  • Sheng Walk the property on the morning of July 7. Not to do work. To read. Fifteen minutes is enough. The morning walk is the homeowner's confirmation that the close-out landed, and the read is the part the homeowner will remember when the August articles start.

Frequently Asked Questions

I did not do any of the July outdoor walks. Is the close-out still for me?
Yes, with the understanding that the close-out is doing two months of work in two days. A homeowner who skipped July 1 through July 4 should walk the property on July 5, do the strip-back on July 6, observe on July 7, and accept that some outdoor rooms will not fully settle until the second week of August. The close-out is not a replacement for the walks. It is the property-wide undo the walks built toward. A homeowner who skips the walks and does the close-out gets a partial alignment. A homeowner who does the walks and the close-out gets the full alignment. The choice is the homeowner's.
My front garden and back garden are the same room. Does that change the close-out?
A property where the front and back are visually continuous (a corner lot, a property with no fence, a small urban courtyard) reads as one room, not two. The July 1 and July 2 articles apply as one combined read. The close-out list shortens by one or two items because the shared room does not need separate Fire anchors. The homeowner with a single continuous outdoor room should walk the perimeter once on July 5 and once on July 7, and accept that the observation read is one read rather than two.
I rent. I cannot change anything in the front garden or on the rooftop.
A renter's close-out is the smallest of the four, because the renter has the least to undo. The renter's close-out is also the most portable, because the cures the renter places on July 5 and July 6 can move to the next property in August. The Fire anchor the renter keeps should be portable (a single planter, a small lantern). The wind-chime silence applies to renters too. The renter who strips back on July 6 and walks the property on July 7 will read the seasonal change even without changing the building itself.
What about the indoor rooms? Do they need a close-out at Li Qiu too?
The indoor rooms need their own close-out, and that article is coming later in July. The July thread was written deliberately as outdoor-only because the indoor close-out reads differently and deserves its own walk-through. A homeowner who only does the outdoor close-out will see the seasonal correction land in the outdoor rooms by the second week of July and feel it land in the indoor rooms by the third week. The indoor close-out is the natural next step in the thread, and the homeowner who is reading this article in early July is the homeowner who should expect an indoor piece in the second half of the month.
I have a pool. Where does the pool fit in the close-out?
A pool is the most Water-dominant outdoor feature on the property, and the cycles read it as a year-round Water anchor rather than a seasonal one. A pool does not need to be stripped back at Li Qiu, because the pool is not a Fire-Month cure. A pool whose surrounding decor was added in late June (a poolside lantern, a string of pool lights, a warm-toned umbrella) does need the strip-back. The pool itself stays. The decorations around the pool follow the same close-out rules as the back garden.
My outdoor rooms feel fine. Do I really need to do the close-out?
An outdoor room that feels fine at the end of the Fire Month is an outdoor room whose cures are in sync with the season. The close-out is what keeps it in sync through the transition. An outdoor room that feels fine on July 5 and feels off by July 25 is an outdoor room that drifted through the transition without the close-out. The close-out is preventive, not corrective. The homeowner who does it on July 5 and 6 is the homeowner whose outdoor rooms keep feeling fine through July and into August.
