Why Birth Time Is Critical

If you are uncertain of your exact birth time, this matters more in BaZi than in any other astrological system. The Hour Pillar is not an estimate — it is a specific, real pillar that changes every two hours. A person born at 10:00 AM has a different Hour Pillar than someone born at 10:30 AM of the same day.

Hospital records, birth certificates, or family recollection are your best sources for birth time. If you truly do not know, enter your earliest known estimate and note in your reading request that time is approximate — some analysts can work around this, though accuracy will be reduced.

What birth time format do I need?

Enter the hour you were born in 24-hour format (for example, 14:30 for 2:30 PM). If you know it was between two specific hours, use the earliest confirmed hour — the Hour Pillar changes at approximately two-hour intervals starting from 11 PM.

Does Solar Time correction really matter?

Yes, especially if you live in a large country with a single time zone. China uses a single national time zone even though it spans five geographical time zones. Someone born at "9 AM" in Chengdu experienced a very different celestial configuration than someone born at 9 AM in Beijing. Solar Time correction adjusts for this difference, making the Hour Pillar more accurate.

What if I do not know my birth time?

If you know the approximate hour (morning, afternoon, evening), that is better than nothing — the reading will note that your Hour Pillar is approximate. For the most accurate reading, try to confirm your exact birth time from hospital records or your birth certificate.

Western zodiac uses your birth date to assign one of twelve sun signs. Our BaZi calculator uses your birth year, month, day, AND hour — giving eight characters instead of one.
