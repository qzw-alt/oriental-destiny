Back Garden Feng Shui: The 'Hidden Work' Corner Everyone Forgets (Fu Wei, Explained)

The front garden gets all the attention. The back garden does all the work.

In feng shui terms, the back holds the Fu Wei — the steady, hidden, workhorse corner of the property. It's the difference between a home that feels 'solid' and one that feels like it's running on luck.

Here's what to do back there this month.

Published July 2, 2026 · 12 min read

Why the back garden gets the July 2 article

Yesterday's front-garden piece ended with a note about the back. The back garden is the Fu Wei, sometimes translated as the steady back area or the back court. The Fu Wei is the enclosed, private counterpart to the Ming Tang. The Ming Tang is the room the seasonal qi enters first. The Fu Wei is the room the homeowner has been working in all year, and the qi there reads the homeowner's habits, not the season. A Ming Tang that is well-set for Earth Month can still be undermined by a Fu Wei that is fighting the homeowner's chart. The reverse is also true: a balanced Fu Wei holds the seasonal correction in place across the six weeks between the July 7 transition and the next checkpoint in mid-August.

The classical feng shui term for the back garden, Fu Wei, has two common translations: the steady back and the seated back. The steady-back reading is the one most homeowner-level articles use. The seated-back reading is older and, in my experience, the more useful one. The back garden is the place where the homeowner sits after the day is done. The Ming Tang is for the people who arrive. The Fu Wei is for the person who has already done the arriving. The seasonal qi enters at the gate, crosses the bright hall, enters the house, moves through the indoor rooms, and finally settles in the back garden. By the time the qi reaches the Fu Wei, it has been through every cycle and every room, and the only thing left to do is rest.

Earth Month, which begins on July 7, is the month when the Fu Wei matters most. The Earth element is the season's grounding element, and the back garden is the garden's grounding area. A Fu Wei that is already showing Earth signals in early July is a Fu Wei that the seasonal shift will reinforce. A Fu Wei that is still in late-Fire mode on July 7 is a Fu Wei that the season will have to push through before it can settle. The homeowner who walks the back garden in the first week of July is the homeowner whose Earth Month starts from a place of rest, not from a place of catch-up.

The front garden is the room the seasonal qi enters. The back garden is the room the homeowner has already been working in for years. The cycles read the first one; the chart reads the second one.

What to do this week, in the order the back garden asks

These are the moves I usually suggest for the back garden in the first week of July. The order is the opposite of the front-garden order. The front garden is built from the gate inward. The back garden is read from the house outward. The first move is the one that affects what the homeowner sees every evening. The last two are the structural moves, and skipping them is fine for a homeowner who is short on time.

  1. Sit in the back garden at the time of day you usually use it. Not at noon, not at sunset. Sit at the time you really do go out back, even if it is just to hang the laundry or to check the plants. The Fu Wei at 7am in early July is not the same Fu Wei as the Fu Wei at 9pm, and the feng shui read of the back garden is the read at the time the homeowner is in it. Most homeowners read the back garden on a quiet weekend morning and wonder why it feels off on a busy Tuesday evening. It feels off because the light, the temperature, the insects, the neighbor's lights, and the homeowner's own energy are all different.
  2. Look at the back garden from the back door. The view from the back door is the view the homeowner has every time they step out. The view from the back fence is the view the neighbor has, and the cycles do not read the neighbor's view first. The classical preference is a Fu Wei that opens from the back door and narrows toward the back fence, the way a bowl narrows toward its base. A back garden that is wider at the fence than at the door is a Fu Wei that disperses rather than gathers. If the back garden is wider at the back, the cure is a low planting bed, a row of pots, or a small trellis at the fence line, anything that gives the eye a place to land before the boundary.
  3. Clear the back garden of unfinished work. A half-bagged pile of soil, a wheelbarrow that has been out since the spring planting, a stack of pots that were supposed to be moved and never were. The Fu Wei is the homeowner's relationship to the work they have already done, and a Fu Wei that is full of unfinished work reads the unfinished work first. This is a maintenance move, not a cycle move, but the cycles do not work if the back garden itself is fighting the homeowner.
  4. Find the compost corner. Every back garden has one, even if the homeowner does not call it that. The corner where the leaves pile up, where the trimmings go, where the weeds end up before the bin day. The compost corner is the Earth element in its most honest form: decomposition, recycling, the slow turning of one season's growth into the next season's soil. A Fu Wei with a working compost corner in early July is a Fu Wei that the Earth Month will recognize. A Fu Wei with a neglected corner is the place the cycles start to slip.
  5. Place one Metal element in the Fu Wei before July 7. A stone bench, a metal garden tool hung on the wall, a brass bell at the gate between the back garden and the side passage, a small metal bird feeder. The Metal element is the element that comes after Earth in the Sheng cycle, and the Fu Wei is the place where the cycles look forward as well as back. A Fu Wei that already has a Metal anchor on July 1 is a Fu Wei that the Earth-to-Metal transition at the end of July will pass through without a fight. A Fu Wei that has to add the Metal element on August 1 is three weeks behind.

None of these are expensive. The most expensive single item is a stone bench, and a homeowner who has a back garden already has a flat rock or a paving stone that will do. The total cost is under thirty dollars in most markets, and most of the moves are free. The point is the order: sit in the garden, look from the door, clear the unfinished work, find the compost corner, place the Metal. Five moves, in that order, before July 7.

What the back garden is asking for in early July

This is the part most homeowner-level articles skip, and the part that separates a back garden that reads well from a back garden that reads correctly. The front garden has its own element balance, and the back garden has its own, and the two are not the same. The front garden in early July wants Wood. The back garden in early July wants rest. The cycles are the same in both places. What changes is the speed of the qi. The Ming Tang qi moves fast because it is seasonal. The Fu Wei qi moves slowly because it is the homeowner's own.

The practical version: a back garden that is already over-stuffed in early July is a back garden in trouble. Too many decorative objects, too many raised beds, too many starts of projects that did not finish. The Fu Wei is the place for finished work, not for work in progress. A Fu Wei that has a single finished area — a corner with a chair and a table, a single bed that is fully planted, a stone path from the door to a seat — is in better shape than a Fu Wei that has ten half-finished ideas. The homeowner who has spent the spring starting things is the homeowner who needs to finish one of them before July 7. The cycles accept a small finished garden more readily than a large unfinished one.

The back garden is also where the homeowner's chart reads most strongly. The front garden reads the season. The back garden reads the chart. A Wood-heavy chart whose Fu Wei is full of finished Wood projects is reading correctly. A Water-heavy chart whose Fu Wei is full of still water features is reading correctly. An Earth-heavy chart whose Fu Wei is over-decorated is reading incorrectly, because the chart is already producing Earth and the Fu Wei does not need to add more. The chart-side reading is the part the homeowner-level articles skip, and the part the practitioner-level conversations start with.

The fence question, July edition

The fence question comes up in every back-garden consultation, and the answer in July is different from the answer in October. A solid fence is the back garden's container. The cycles read the container before they read the contents. A solid wooden fence in the back garden is the Earth element in its most still form: a barrier, a wall, a defined boundary. A wire fence or an open rail fence is the Metal element in its communicative form: a line of sight, a passage, a relationship with the neighbor's garden. The Earth Month asks for a contained Fu Wei. A homeowner with an open rail fence in early July is making a Metal statement about the seasonal element, which is the opposite of what the back garden is asking for in the next six weeks. The recommendation is to leave the open fence as it is for the back garden (the Metal element is the post-Earth element, and a back garden that already has Metal is a back garden that is ahead of the curve) and to add a row of potted plants along the inside of the fence to give the back garden a soft Earth boundary that the season can recognize.

The day-master-by-element reading for the back garden

If the homeowner knows their Day Master, the back garden reads slightly differently for each of the five elements. This is the part most homeowner-level articles skip, because they assume the cycles are the same in every room of the property. The cycles are the same. What changes is the speed of the qi, and that depends on the Day Master.

A Wood-heavy chart — Jia or Yi Day Master, or a chart with strong Wood in the seasonal pillar — reads the Fu Wei as a Ke target. Wood parts Earth, and the Wood-heavy chart is already doing the parting job. The back garden in early July should soften the Wood: a stone bench (Earth tames Wood), a finished path (Earth contains Wood), a quiet corner (Wood is Yang, the Fu Wei wants Yin). The Wood-heavy chart wants the back garden to be a rest, not a workshop.

A Fire-heavy chart — Bing or Ding Day Master, or a chart with strong Fire in the summer pillars — reads the Fu Wei as a Sheng target. Fire creates Earth, so a Fire-heavy chart is already producing the seasonal element. The back garden should accept that production. A finished seating area, a low planting bed, a contained herb corner. The Fire-heavy chart benefits from a Fu Wei that is already producing Earth, and the homeowner with a Fire-heavy chart is the one who benefits most from finishing one project in the back garden before July 7.

An Earth-heavy chart — Wu or Ji Day Master, or a chart with strong Earth across the seasonal pillars — reads the Fu Wei as a Ke target in the opposite direction. The Earth-heavy chart is already producing Earth, and the back garden does not need more. The cure is Metal: the brass bell, the metal tool, the stone bench, the small rock garden. An Earth-heavy chart whose Fu Wei is full of wooden raised beds is asking for a Metal anchor, and the Earth Month is the moment to add it.

A Metal-heavy chart — Geng or Xin Day Master, or a chart with strong Metal in the autumn pillars — reads the Fu Wei as a Sheng target. Metal produces Water, and Water feeds Wood, and Wood feeds the Earth. The Metal-heavy chart wants the Fu Wei to be a place where the cycles can complete themselves. A small still-water feature, a bird bath, a small fountain turned off in July, is the load-bearing move for a Metal-heavy chart in early July.

A Water-heavy chart — Ren or Gui Day Master, or a chart with strong Water in the winter pillars — reads the Fu Wei as a quiet space. Water is the deepest of the five elements, and the Water-heavy chart is the chart that benefits most from a finished, contained back garden. The cure is not a new element. The cure is finishing what is already started. A Water-heavy chart whose Fu Wei has three half-finished projects in early July is the chart most at risk of an unsettled summer. The Water-heavy chart wants rest, and rest comes from finishing.

The front garden reads the season. The back garden reads the chart. The homeowner who knows their Day Master reads the back garden with the chart in the room, and the five moves above become five moves with both the season and the chart in the room.

What to undo at Li Qiu

The Fire-to-Earth transition ends on July 7, and the back garden that was right for the transition window is not the back garden that is right for the next six weeks. The undo is part of the practice, and the homeowner who skips the undo is the homeowner whose back garden feels off by August.

The first thing to undo is the late-Fire outdoor lighting. A string of warm fairy lights on the back fence, a tiki torch, a citronella candle, a portable fire pit that has been in use all month. The Fire lighting helped during the Fire Month. In Earth Month it is decorative, not curative, and a Fu Wei full of Fire light is a back garden that is fighting the season. Move the warm fairy lights to a single string, swap the tiki torch for a low-wattage solar lantern, and let the porch light (the one on the front, the one We covered) be the working Fire. The back garden does not need to be a Fire room in Earth Month. It needs to be an Earth room with a single quiet Metal anchor.

The second thing to undo is the assumption that the front-garden piece covered the work. Let's walk the front garden. The previous article did not touch the back. A homeowner who walked the bright hall on July 1 and skipped the back yard is the homeowner who will wonder why the seasonal correction does not seem to land in the evenings. The seasonal qi enters at the gate, crosses the bright hall, enters the home, and settles in the back garden. The Fu Wei is the resting place. A Fu Wei that is still in late-Fire mode is contaminating the homeowner's rest, and the contamination shows up in the evenings, when the homeowner finally has time to sit and notice.

The third thing to undo is the working pile. The half-bagged soil, the stack of pots, the wheelbarrow. A Fu Wei that was cleared on July 2 will start collecting again by July 20 if the homeowner is not careful. The seasonal qi moves through the back garden in six-week waves, and the working pile is the part that returns the fastest. The maintenance rule for Earth Month is: every time the homeowner uses the back garden, they leave it with one less unfinished item than they found it. The first visit clears the working pile. The second visit clears a single pot. The third visit clears the tools. By the August checkpoint, the Fu Wei is the resting place the cycles read it as.

Frequently Asked Questions

My back garden is mostly patio. Is the Fu Wei still the back garden?
Yes. The Fu Wei is the enclosed, private, homeowner-only area behind the house, regardless of what is on the ground. A back garden that is mostly patio reads as hard Earth, and a hard-Earth Fu Wei in Earth Month is the case that benefits most from the Metal anchor and the finished corner. The homeowner with a patio back garden does not need to add plants to read the cycles correctly. They need to finish one corner. A single chair, a single table, a single pot on the corner of the patio, and the back garden becomes a Fu Wei the season can recognize.
My back garden faces a park. Does the open view change the reading?
It does, in the same way an open rail fence changes the reading. A Fu Wei that opens onto a wide green space is a back garden that is already getting Metal from the view. The cycles read the line of sight before they read the objects, and a back garden with a clear view of trees and grass is a back garden that has a Metal-and-Wood source at the back fence. The homeowner in this case should focus on the front of the Fu Wei — the part closest to the back door — and let the back fence take care of itself. A single finished seating area near the door is the move for a back garden that opens onto a park.
I share the back garden with neighbors. Whose Fu Wei is it?
The Fu Wei is the area the homeowner can call their own. In a townhouse or a row house, the Fu Wei is the part of the back garden behind the homeowner's own back door, even if the rest of the yard is shared. The line is the line of sight from the homeowner's back door to the shared boundary. The homeowner should treat the area they can see and touch from their own back door as their Fu Wei, and the rest as a separate, neighbor's garden. A Fu Wei that the homeowner cannot see from the door is not a Fu Wei, and the cycles do not read it as part of the homeowner's chart.
What about a vegetable garden? Does the Fu Wei include the vegetable beds?
The vegetable garden is its own feng shui object. The vegetables are Earth element in its most active form, and the vegetable beds are a Sheng target for the seasonal qi in the same way the lawn is. A homeowner whose back garden includes vegetable beds is a homeowner whose Fu Wei already has Earth, and the work for the Earth Month is to keep the beds tidy, harvested, and finished. A vegetable bed in mid-summer that has bolted lettuce, half-eaten tomatoes, and weeds is a vegetable bed that the cycles read as unfinished Earth, which is the harder case. The recommendation is to harvest aggressively in the first week of July and clear the bolted plants before July 7. A clean, harvested, mid-summer vegetable bed is the strongest Earth signal the back garden can have.
My back garden has a pond. Do I leave it running in July?
A still pond is different from a fountain, and the cycles read them differently. A still pond is the Water element in its still form, and a still pond in Earth Month is fine — Water is the upstream element to Wood, which feeds Earth, and a still pond is a Sheng target for the back garden's Earth work. A fountain or a spraying water feature is the Water element in its active form, and a fountain in Earth Month is a Ke move against the seasonal element. The recommendation: keep the pond full and still, turn the fountain off in early July, and leave both that way until the August checkpoint. A still pond in mid-summer is one of the strongest Fu Wei configurations the homeowner can have, and the homeowner who already has one is the homeowner who can skip the Metal anchor move in favor of finishing one corner of the back garden.
I have a dog. Does the dog change the Fu Wei reading?
The dog is a Yang presence in the back garden, and a Yang presence in the Fu Wei is fine in moderate amounts. A single dog in a back garden is a single Yang presence, and the cycles accept that. Multiple dogs, or a dog that runs the fence line, is a different reading — that is a back garden where the Yang is dominant, and the Earth Month will struggle to land. The cure is the finished corner. A dog whose owner has a finished seating area in the back garden is a dog that the cycles read as part of the homeowner's chart, and a dog whose owner has no finished area is a dog that the cycles read as the only finished thing in the Fu Wei. The dog does not need to change. The corner does.
I just moved in. The back garden is a mess. Where do I start?
A new homeowner's Fu Wei is the rare case where the order can be relaxed. The first move is still to sit in the back garden at the time of day the homeowner will use it, but the second move is to identify what is already there. A new back garden often has the previous owner's finished projects, and the cycles read finished projects even if they are not the new homeowner's style. The recommendation is to leave the previous owner's finished corner alone for the first three months, finish one new project of the new homeowner's own, and then in October (the next big checkpoint) decide what stays and what goes. A Fu Wei that has at least one finished area is a Fu Wei that reads correctly, and the homeowner who waits six months to start customizing is the homeowner who gets the best read of the property.
