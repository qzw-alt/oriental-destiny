The Snake in the Chinese Zodiac — Origin, Mythology & Position

The Snake (She 蛇) is the sixth creature of the twelve-year zodiac cycle and the first of the four "mystical" animals — Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey — that classical Chinese astrology groups under the southern summer quadrant. Its position in the cycle is, in many ways, its defining feature. While the Rat opens the calendar with quick initiative and the Dragon opens the second half with cosmic ambition, the Snake is the one that arrives having already watched. The Snake does not begin the journey; it has been studying the path the entire time.

In the founding myth recorded across the Shiji, the popular banquet race and the commentaries of later dynasties, the Snake's quiet intelligence and timing allowed it to overtake the more powerful animals of the zodiac by strategic patience. That single quality — patient, observant, decisive only when the moment is right — has defined the Snake's reputation for nearly two thousand years. It is the strategist of the zodiac, the healer, the philosopher, the one who knows the unspoken thing in any room.

From a BaZi perspective, the Snake belongs to the Fire element (Si-Fire 巳火) and is Yin in polarity. Its season is the height of late spring, when the sun has fully matured and the first hints of summer heat press into the air. The Si branch is sometimes called "the fire of transformation" because it is the moment in the year when spring's growth begins to be refined into summer's ripening. Energy at Si is no longer starting — it is being shaped. This is why Snake-native Fire is described as still, focused, and inward — a candle flame rather than a bonfire.

The Si branch also carries one of the most distinctive hidden-stem configurations in the entire Earthly Branch system. Its main Qi is Bing-Fire (Yang Fire), its middle Qi is Wu-Earth (Yang Earth), and its residual Qi is Geng-Metal (Yang Metal). Together they form what classical texts call a "transformation trinity" — the fire that burns away the unnecessary, the earth that stabilises what remains, and the metal that gives the result its edge. The Snake is the only zodiac branch where the main Qi is Bing-Fire, and the Ding-Snake variant (Fire Snake, born 1977) is the only one where the heavenly stem matches the branch's main element — making it the most archetypal Snake of all the variants.

The Snake's opposite on the zodiac wheel is the Pig (Hai 亥) — a relationship so charged it has its own classical name: the 巳亥冲 (Si-Hai clash). Si is Yin Fire, Hai is Yang Water; they are direct opposites in polarity and direct opposites in the calendar's natural arc. Anything you learn about the Snake is in dialogue with the Pig, and a BaZi reader always checks for Pig energies in a Snake chart, because Pig is where the Snake's quiet fire either gets challenged or gets sharpened.

Water-dominant Snake. The most psychic Snake variant. Water Snakes feel things before they happen and intuit the unspoken currents in any situation. They are the Snake that knows the answer before the question is finished — and the one who often keeps the knowledge to themselves.

Snake Health — Elemental Balance and Wellbeing

Health in BaZi is read through the Five Elements — which element is in surplus and which is missing. A Snake-native born in a chart dominated by Fire and Earth is typically robust, with strong heart function (Fire), digestion (Earth) and circulation. Snake health is fundamentally a question of Yin-Fire regulation — too little Fire and the body cannot transform what it takes in; too much Fire and the body overheats.

Organ Systems the Snake Should Protect

The Snake governs the heart, small intestine, blood circulation, eyes, and the tongue. Spring-born Snakes are more sensitive to heat. Excess Water in the annual or luck pillar (such as when a Pig year arrives, or a Water-Da-Yun decade) puts extra pressure on Fire organs, which is why Snake-natives need to balance Water years with deliberate Fire-nourishing routines — warmth, movement, red foods, sunlight, and the company of people who bring warmth rather than dampen it.

Snake Wealth — Money, Investment & Abundance

The Snake is the zodiac's quiet money sign. Wealth comes to the Snake not through quick wins or visible hustle but through patient positioning, deep research, and the willingness to wait for the right opportunity. They read markets, products and people better than almost any other sign, which is why they are best-positioned for value investing, real estate, equity in specialist businesses, and long-horizon compound wealth rather than high-frequency trading.

Snake Money Personality

Snake money personality is conservative by default and decisive when the data is conclusive. The Snake will sit on cash patiently, then deploy it decisively the moment they identify a structural mispricing. The biggest wealth risk for Snakes is over-hesitation in growth environments, or — paradoxically — impulsive "feeling-sure" bets driven by strong Seven Killings energy in their chart. The Snake's greatest financial danger is its own conviction, which is usually right but can occasionally be too absolute.

Best Vehicles for Snake Wealth

Index funds, real estate, equity in their own businesses, blue-chip equity positions, specialist alternative investments, and long-tenor assets that compound quietly. The Snake should generally avoid leverage-heavy, short-tenor trades and any investment that requires them to out-pace a market they cannot see. Frequently Asked Questions About the Snake

What are the Snake years?

Snake years follow the 12-year cycle and include 1917, 1929, 1941, 1953, 1965, 1977, 1989, 2001, 2013, 2025, and the next being 2037. Each Snake year is also tagged with one of the Five Elements through its heavenly stem — see the Snake element variants table above for the full mapping.

What element is the Snake?

The Snake's fixed branch element is Yin Fire (Si-Fire 巳火), the only branch whose main Qi is Bing-Fire. On top of this, each Snake birth year has its own heavenly-stem element — Wood Snake (1965, 2025), Fire Snake (1917, 1977), Earth Snake (1929, 1989), Metal Snake (1941, 2001), or Water Snake (1953, 2013). The element variant matters as much as the branch itself.

What is the 巳亥冲 (Si-Hai clash)?

The 巳亥冲 (Si-Hai clash) is the direct branch opposition between the Snake (Si 巳, Yin Fire) and the Pig (Hai 亥, Water). It is one of the six fundamental clashes of the Chinese zodiac — fire and water meeting head-on, in opposite polarities, on opposite ends of the calendar. Snake–Pig pairings in love, business or living arrangements always require conscious communication, clear boundaries, and elemental balancing to work. The clash is not a verdict; it is a pattern to be navigated.

Who is the Snake most compatible with?
