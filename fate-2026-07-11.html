Three-Gate Feng Shui: Standing at the Outer, Inner, and Mouth Gate

Yesterday's dragon vein article walked the four-line property read. Today's read stands at the three gates the dragon has to cross: the outer gate, the inner gate, the mouth gate. Each gate is a single threshold; together they are the read that decides whether the dragon actually enters the home. By the end of this article the homeowner has a twenty-minute walk that fixes the gates with nothing but a notepad and the next Saturday morning.

Published July 11, 2026 · 11 min read

Why the three-gate read gets its own article

The dragon vein article yesterday ended with the seven-day dragon vein pass, and the second day of that pass is the three-gate read. The four-line article walked the property from outside in (the approach line, the entry line, the interior line, the water line). The three-gate article walks the same property from the same outside, but instead of reading four lines the homeowner reads three thresholds. The four-line article is the dragon's path on paper. The three-gate article is the dragon's path through walls and doors. The four-line article says where the line should run. The three-gate article says whether the line can run.

The reason this article exists separately from yesterday's: the four-line read can be done entirely in the head. The homeowner can trace each line, draw each line on a notepad, and walk each line in their mind's eye without moving through the property. The three-gate read cannot be done in the head. The three-gate read requires the homeowner to stand at each gate and look through it. The dragon vein only reveals itself at the gate when the homeowner is looking down the line of sight. A gate read from the kitchen table is a guess. A gate read with both feet on the threshold is a read.

The three-gate read is also the fastest of the July week's three reads. The four-line read takes twenty minutes. The three-gate read takes ten. The day-master adjustment (tomorrow) takes another ten. Forty minutes total for the dragon vein foundational pass, and the three-gate read is the middle third of that pass. The homeowner who skipped yesterday's article can still do this one — the three-gate read is readable on its own, though it lands harder with the four-line context in hand.

The three gates, in one sentence The outer gate is the line between the public way and the property. The inner gate is the line between the property and the home. The mouth gate is the line between the entry and the room the homeowner lives in. Three lines, three thresholds, one dragon.

The outer gate: where the dragon enters the property

The outer gate is the threshold the homeowner crosses when leaving the property to walk to the street. For a house, the outer gate is the front fence, the front gate, the front yard's edge, or the line of the curb cut where the driveway meets the sidewalk. For an apartment, the outer gate is the building's front door — the door the homeowner walks through to enter the building from the lobby or the street. For a townhouse, the outer gate is the unit's front door (the townhouse has no separate building lobby). The outer gate is the dragon's first encounter with the property's territory, and a clear outer gate is the first condition for a settled dragon.

The outer gate read is the simplest of the three. The homeowner stands on the public side of the outer gate (on the sidewalk, on the driveway apron, at the building's front step) and looks back at the gate. The read is what the homeowner sees. A wide gate — a gate two people can walk through side by side without turning — is the settled read. A narrow gate — a single-person door, a fence the homeowner has to turn sideways through — is the compressed read. A blocked gate — a delivery box planted directly outside, a hedge that has overgrown the path, a stairway that ends at a wall, a building door that opens into a utility room — is the depleted read.

The outer gate is the gate most homeowners have never deliberately read, because the homeowner crosses it every day on the way to the car or the bus or the train. The homeowner who walks the outer gate on a Saturday morning with the deliberate read sees the gate their commute has trained them to ignore. The compressed read at the outer gate is the most common of the three failure modes, and the easiest to fix. The fix is to clear the path for at least two-person width (a delivery box moved around the side, a hedge trimmed, a stairway widened by clearing the storage at its base). The outer gate's compressed read is a logistics problem. The homeowner's first move is logistics.

The depleted read at the outer gate is rarer and harder. A depleted outer gate means the property has lost its public-side connection entirely. A house where the front yard has been converted to a parking pad is depleted. An apartment whose building's front door opens to an alley the homeowner cannot see the street from is depleted. A townhouse whose front door is at the back of the unit (the public-side door is a service door) is depleted. The fix for a depleted outer gate is structural and slow: a path rebuilt, a sight line recovered, a sign put up so the homeowner can find the gate again. The depleted outer gate is the failure mode the July thread does not promise to fix in seven days — it is the read that gets calendared for the August outdoor work.

The inner gate: where the dragon enters the home

The inner gate is the threshold between the property's lot line and the property's main living space. For a house, the inner gate is the front door. For an apartment, the inner gate is the apartment's own front door (the building's lobby door is the outer gate; the apartment door is the inner gate — the two are stacked, which is why apartment reading is harder). For a townhouse, the inner gate and the outer gate are usually the same physical door, and the townhouse article from the spring thread is the read for that ambiguity. The inner gate is the dragon's entry into the home itself, and a clear inner gate is the condition for the dragon to live in the house rather than just visit.

The inner gate read is the read the homeowner does while looking in the door. The homeowner opens the front door from the outside, steps across the threshold, and turns to look back at the door from inside. What the homeowner sees is the inner gate read. A door that opens onto a foyer with a sight line to the back of the property is the settled read — the dragon can run straight through. A door that opens onto a hallway that turns sharply within ten feet is the compressed read. A door that opens onto a wall (the foyer is two feet deep, or the closet door is directly across from the front door) is the depleted read.

The inner gate is the gate the homeowner has the most direct control over, and the gate whose fixes are the smallest. The compressed inner gate (a hallway turning sharply, a door opening into a closet view) is fixed by moving furniture: a hall tree moved from the inside corner to the outside corner, a shoe rack relocated, a console table pulled two feet deeper into the foyer to straighten the sight line. The depleted inner gate (a door opening onto a wall, a closet directly facing the door) is fixed by closing the closet door when the front door is open and putting the hall tree on the side wall, so the sight line from the open front door runs diagonally across the foyer to the back of the house rather than into a wall.

The single most useful move at the inner gate is the mirror read. A mirror facing the front door reflects the dragon back out before it can enter. A mirror on the side wall (parallel to the line of sight from the front door to the back of the house) reflects the dragon deeper into the home. A mirror on the wall behind the homeowner when they stand at the front door has no effect on the gate at all — it is just decoration. The mirror read alone can lift a compressed inner gate to a settled inner gate without moving any furniture. The mirror is the homeowner's friend at the inner gate.

The mouth gate: where the dragon enters the day

The mouth gate is the threshold between the foyer or hallway and the property's main living room. The mouth gate is the gate the homeowner crosses without thinking — every time the homeowner walks from the entry to the couch, the kitchen, the dining table, they cross the mouth gate. The mouth gate is the dragon's entry into the homeowner's daily round, and a clear mouth gate is the condition for the dragon to live with the homeowner rather than just in the house. A blocked mouth gate is the failure mode that reads as "I live here but the house doesn't feel like mine" — and most homeowners have felt that without knowing why.

The mouth gate read is the read the homeowner does from the entry side looking into the main room. The homeowner stands in the foyer, the hallway, or just inside the inner gate, and looks at the opening that leads to the living room (or the kitchen, in a one-room studio). What the homeowner sees is the mouth gate read. A wide opening — a cased opening without a door, a sliding door that is left open, a French door propped against the wall — is the settled read. A narrow opening — a single standard door, a cased opening that has been framed down — is the compressed read. A blocked opening — a sofa placed across the threshold, a bookshelf flush with the opening, a curtain rod hung low — is the depleted read.

The mouth gate is the gate where most homeowners err by treating it as decoration rather than threshold. The mouth gate is the read the dragon has to cross to live with the homeowner in the day. A curtain rod hung at seven feet instead of eight, a sofa two feet further into the threshold than it needs to be, a bookshelf that has crept to the edge of the wall — each of these compresses or depletes the mouth gate by inches, and the inches add up. The seven-day pass the dragon vein article assigned to today is the pass where the homeowner walks the mouth gate with a notepad and notes the four-inch moves that would clear the threshold. The four-inch moves are the mouth gate's work.

The mouth gate is also the gate where day-master element matters most. A Wood-heavy chart benefits from a tall plant at the mouth gate (Wood feeds the dragon on entry). A Fire-heavy chart benefits from a single warm-toned element at the mouth gate (a candle, a small lamp, a piece of red fabric). An Earth-heavy chart benefits from nothing at the mouth gate — the mouth gate is the chart's own element, and adding more Earth just damps the read. A Metal-heavy chart benefits from a metal element at the mouth gate (a small sculpture, a brass dish, a silver vase). A Water-heavy chart benefits from a glass element at the mouth gate (a glass vase, a window with a water view, a small tabletop fountain). The day-master element and the mouth gate read together give the homeowner the chart-specific mouth gate adjustment.

The three gates are the same dragon at three scales. The outer gate is the dragon entering the property's territory. The inner gate is the dragon entering the property's home. The mouth gate is the dragon entering the property's daily life. A dragon that can run through all three is a dragon the homeowner can live with.

How to read the three gates in a single twenty-minute walk

The three-gate read is one continuous walk. The homeowner starts on the public side of the outer gate with a notepad (or the notes app on the phone) and a pen. The walk has six steps, and each step takes under four minutes. By the end of the walk the homeowner has a one-line read for each of the three gates, and a one-line fix for any gate that read compressed or depleted. The walk is the read.

  1. Stand on the public side of the outer gate. Note what you see through the gate. Wide = settled. Narrow = compressed. Blocked = depleted. Write one word: settled, compressed, or depleted.
  2. Cross the outer gate and stand at the inner gate (the front door). Note what you see through the open front door from outside. A foyer with a sight line to the back of the house = settled. A hallway turning sharply = compressed. A wall directly across the doorway = depleted. One word.
  3. Open the front door and step inside. Stand in the foyer or hallway. Note what you see from the inside looking out. Two-person width into the foyer = settled. Narrow doorway with a closet view = compressed. Wall two feet from the door = depleted. One word.
  4. Walk from the foyer to the mouth gate. Stand in the foyer and look at the opening that leads to the main room. Cased opening or wide doorway with no door = settled. Single standard door = compressed. Sofa or bookshelf across the threshold = depleted. One word.
  5. For any gate that read compressed, write the smallest move that fixes it. One piece of furniture moved. One shoe rack cleared. One curtain rod raised. The compressed-gate fixes are always logistics.
  6. For any gate that read depleted, write whether the fix is a twenty-minute move, a Saturday move, or a project. The depleted-gate fixes are always calendared, not improvised.
The settled/compressed/depleted vocabulary, in one sentence Settled means the dragon can run freely through the gate without anything slowing it. Compressed means something is squeezing the line of qi (a chair, a closet, a narrow doorway). Depleted means the line of qi has nowhere to go (a wall, a blocked stairway, a missing sight line). The vocabulary is the read.

Frequently Asked Questions

What if my property doesn't have three distinct gates? Some houses only have one or two thresholds.
Most houses have all three, but the gates can collapse. A studio apartment has an outer gate (the building door) and a mouth gate (the opening from the hallway to the main room), but the inner gate is the apartment door that opens directly into the room — the gate is one threshold and it carries the weight of two reads. A house with no front fence has an outer gate at the curb cut (the line where the driveway meets the sidewalk). A property with collapsed gates is a property the dragon vein reads as compressed overall; the fix is to deliberately mark the gate that doesn't physically exist (a row of plants where the fence would be, a pathway light at the line where the gate would be, a doormat that defines the threshold the home enters through). The deliberate marking is the gate.
How is this different from reading the bagua map at the front door?
The bagua map reads the front door's sector (which of the eight life areas the door falls in). The three-gate read reads the front door's function (whether the dragon can enter through it). The two are different scales and they overlap. A front door in the Career sector of the bagua map is a door the homeowner should treat as the property's mouth — and that door is also the inner gate the dragon crosses. A door in the Helpful People sector is a door the homeowner can treat as outer-gate-adjacent (the door is the public-side threshold). The bagua map is the door's address. The three-gate read is the door's traffic. A door with a great bagua address and a depleted three-gate read is a door the cycles read as hard-to-enter; the bagua address alone doesn't fix the gate.
My front door opens to a long hallway that runs straight through the house to the back. Is that the settled read for all three gates?
A long straight hallway from the front door through to the back of the house is the ideal mouth gate read (the dragon can run straight through the homeowner's daily round) and a strong inner gate read (the dragon can enter the home on a single sight line). It does not, however, mean the outer gate is settled — the outer gate is the property's public-side threshold, not the front door. A house with a straight interior hallway and a blocked outer gate (a hedge that has overgrown the front path, a delivery box on the sidewalk side) is still depleted at the outer gate. The interior hallway is the dragon's body; the outer gate is the dragon's mouth; the body can be settled while the mouth is blocked. The three gates are independent reads, and a settled mouth gate does not absolve a depleted outer gate.
Do I do the three-gate read at night or during the day?
Daylight, ideally mid-morning on a weekend. The three-gate read depends on sight lines, and sight lines depend on light. A gate that reads depleted at midnight may read compressed at dawn and settled at noon — the dragon reads as moving through the property, and the read is the moment the dragon is currently at. Mid-morning on a Saturday is the read most homeowners can do with the property quiet and the light good. The three-gate read once at mid-morning on a Saturday is the read; doing it again at a different hour is supplementary, not primary. The first read is the read.
What if I have a back door I use more than the front door? Does the back door become a gate?
Yes. A back door the homeowner uses daily is a gate in the three-gate sense, and the gate hierarchy rotates. The door the homeowner uses most is the inner gate. The outdoor space the homeowner enters from that door is the property's lot in that orientation (the back garden is the property from the back door's perspective). A homeowner whose back door is their primary entry has a property whose three-gate read is best done from the back, not the front. The article's "front door" language is conventional, not prescriptive — the three-gate read applies to whichever door is the homeowner's primary entry. Walking the gate at the actual primary door is the read.
