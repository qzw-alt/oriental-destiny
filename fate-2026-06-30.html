June 30 Feng Shui Checklist: 7 Moves Before Earth Month Starts (Do Them Today)

The Fire Month ends today. Earth Month starts in a week. In between is the window when a quick walk-through settles your home's energy for the next month.

Here's the checklist — seven moves, each under five minutes, in the order that matters.

Grab a coffee. You'll be done before it cools.

What the cycles are about to do, and why today matters

A feng shui month does not end the way a calendar month ends. The Fire Month that began around Xia Zhi (June 21) is still running, but it is winding down. The peak heat of the month has passed, the longest days are behind us, and the body's relationship to the Fire element has shifted even if the weather has not. The classical division puts the start of Earth Month at Li Qiu, which this year lands on July 7. Between today and July 7 is a transition window, and what gets done in the home during that window matters more than what gets done during the rest of the month.

The reason is the two cycles. The previous article on Sheng Qi and Ke laid out the basic geometry. Sheng is the generating direction: Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates Earth, Earth bears Metal, Metal collects Water, Water feeds Wood. Ke is the controlling direction: Wood parts Earth, Earth absorbs Water, Water extinguishes Fire, Fire melts Metal, Metal cuts Wood. A room short on Wood can be cured by adding Water (the Sheng direction upstream of Wood) or by adding Wood directly. A room with excess Metal can be cured by adding Wood (the Ke direction against Metal). The two cycles are two different ways of answering the same question. The homeowner who can read both is past the point of guessing what to put in the corner.

The transition window changes the question. During the Fire Month, the seasonal element is Fire, and the home is naturally short on Water (Water is what extinguishes Fire, so the room is asking for Water). During the Earth Month, the seasonal element is Earth, and the home is naturally short on Wood (Wood is what parts Earth, so the room is asking for Wood). A cure that helped in late June can be wrong in early July. A blue cushion that was right for the south-facing living room all month can be the wrong move on July 7 if the homeowner keeps it past the seasonal shift. The cycles are time-sensitive. This is the part most homeowner-level articles skip.

The cycle a cure belongs to is the cycle the room is asking for at that moment in the season. The same blue cushion can be a Sheng cure in late June and a stale move in early July.

The seven-day timeline from today to Li Qiu

Here is what the homeowner is working with. The dates are fixed by the solar term calendar, not by the homeowner.

June 30 (today) Last day of Fire Month. Run the room-by-room check below. Note which rooms are short on Water and which have excess Fire.
July 1–6 Transition window. Fire still dominant, but Earth is gathering. Apply the Sheng cures that support the late-Fire phase and start the gentle Wood additions that will carry into Earth Month.
July 7 (Li Qiu) Earth Month begins. Swap the late-Fire Water cures for Wood cures if the room is going to need them through August.
July 22 (Xiao Shu) "Small heat" solar term. Earth Month at full strength. Review the season-to-date checklist and remove any cure that no longer matches the room's element balance.
August 7 (Li Qiu — no, that's wrong) A check-in point. By now the seasonal element is Earth for two months. The chart-side BaZi read becomes more useful than the room-side read for any homeowner whose chart is heavy on Earth or Wood.

One correction to the table above: I listed August 7 as "Li Qiu" by reflex, and it is not. Li Qiu is July 7. August 7 is Li Xia, the start of autumn proper in some calendars and the deeper Earth month in others. The point is the same: pick a check-in point in the second week of Earth Month and review what the room is asking for. The exact solar term is less important than the discipline of the review.

The room-by-room check for tonight

This is the checklist for the homeowner who wants to walk the house tonight or tomorrow morning with the two cycles in hand. I have grouped the rooms by element signature and tagged each move with the cycle it belongs to. The tags are how the homeowner can tell at a glance whether the move is Sheng (generating, supportive) or Ke (controlling, restraining) or neither (a maintenance move that does not belong to either cycle).

Room one: the south-facing living room (Fire-dominant)

What the room is asking for: Water. A south-facing room with afternoon sun is in the Fire phase of the Sheng cycle. The room needs Water to feed the next step (Water feeds Wood, Wood feeds Fire, so Water is the upstream element that lets the Fire express itself without burning). A Water cure is a Sheng move.

  • ShengAdd one Water object. A blue cushion, a dark ceramic vase, a small tabletop fountain if the room can carry the sound. One object, in a visible spot. Two Water objects in one room is overkill and the cycles do not require it.
  • KeRemove or cover one Fire object if the room is overheating. A red throw, a candle that is always lit, a string of warm fairy lights on a timer. The Fire object does not have to leave the home, it has to leave this room until October.
  • NeitherOpen the south window for ten minutes tonight. Not a feng shui cure in the strict sense, but it lets the late-evening qi cycle through the room. Do this at sunset, not at noon.

Room two: the kitchen (Fire-dominant, with Water risk at the sink)

What the room is asking for: Balance. The kitchen is the classic Fire-water collision in a home: the stove is Fire, the sink is Water, and they sit a few feet apart. The Fire Month exaggerates this collision. The Sheng cure is to put a Wood buffer between them (Wood feeds Fire and absorbs Water in the Ke direction), and the Ke cure is to keep them from facing each other directly.

  • ShengPlace a small plant or wooden cutting board between stove and sink. The Wood element softens the Fire-water line. A basil plant on the windowsill is Sheng if it is between the two fixtures, not at the far end of the counter.
  • KeDo not put a mirror on the wall facing the stove. The mirror doubles the Fire visually and the cycles read that as a Fire surge. This is a Ke-level restraint, even if most homeowners think of it as a decor choice.
  • NeitherWipe down the stove tonight. A clean stove at the end of the Fire Month is the maintenance close-out. The cycles do not require it, but the discipline of the seasonal close-out does.

Room three: the bedroom (Earth-and-Metal, short on Fire)

What the room is asking for: A small Fire presence. Most bedrooms are short on Fire during the Fire Month because the homeowner instinctively pulls warm colors and warm light out of the room in summer. The Sheng direction out of Wood is Fire, so adding a small Fire element is a Sheng move for a Wood-heavy room and a direct cure for a Fire-short room.

  • ShengAdd one warm light source. A bedside lamp with a warm bulb (2700K or below), used for the last hour before sleep. Not a ceiling light. The bedside position is the Sheng position for the Fire element in a bedroom.
  • ShengOne small red or terracotta accent. A pillow, a throw, a small framed print. The bedroom does not need a lot of Fire; it needs the Fire to be visible from the bed.
  • KeCheck the headboard for sharp Metal edges. A metal-frame headboard in a Fire-short room is the wrong direction. The cycles read it as Metal cutting the small Fire the room has. A wood or upholstered headboard is the cure.

Room four: the home office (Metal-dominant, short on Wood)

What the room is asking for: Wood. A metal-heavy office (laptop, phone, white walls, glass desk, file cabinet) is asking for Wood in two directions at once. Wood feeds Fire in the Sheng direction, and Wood parts Earth in the Ke direction. The Wood element is doing both jobs at once, which is the rare case where one cure serves two cycles.

  • ShengOne plant on the desk or within arm's reach. Not a cactus (cactus is Metal-protective and reads as a defensive move). A pothos, a snake plant, a small fern. The plant should be alive and not silk.
  • ShengReplace the metal pen cup with a wooden one. Small move, same cycle. The pen cup is in the hand-line of the desk and the cycles read it as part of the room's working surface.
  • NeitherClose the laptop at end of day. The cycles do not require it, but the seasonal close-out does. The homeowner's relationship to the Fire Month ends with the workday ending, and a closed laptop is the symbolic marker.

Room five: the entryway (depends on facing direction)

What the room is asking for: Depends on which way the door opens. A south-facing entry is in the Fire phase of the compass. A north-facing entry is in the Water phase. The homeowner who does not know the facing direction is the homeowner who should find out before applying any cure. A wrong-direction cure is a Ke move against the room, not for it.

  • ShengSouth or southwest entry: add one Water object near the door. A dark doormat, a blue ceramic bowl for keys, a small mirror on the inside wall opposite the door (the mirror doubles the Water symbolically, do not put it on the door itself).
  • ShengNorth or east entry: add one Fire object near the door. A warm light on a motion sensor, a red seasonal wreath, a small wooden bench in a warm stain.
  • NeitherAll entries: clear the shoes and the mail pile tonight. The entry is the room where the seasonal qi enters the home, and a cluttered entry blocks the Sheng direction for every room behind it.

The order of operations

The homeowner who reads the cycles carefully knows that the order of moves matters as much as the moves themselves. The classical sequence is Sheng first, then Ke, and the reason is that a Sheng move gives the room somewhere to send the energy. A Ke move on its own can make the room contract. A Ke move on top of a Sheng move lands cleanly because the room already has an outlet.

For the end-of-month close-out, the practical order is this:

  1. Read the room. Stand in each room for a minute, look at the dominant element, note what is missing and what is in excess. This is the diagnostic step. The cycles are useless without the diagnosis.
  2. Add one Sheng cure. One Water object in a Fire-dominant room, one plant in a Metal-dominant room, one warm light in a Fire-short room. The cure is small, visible, and reversible.
  3. Wait three days. The classical waiting period. A room that has been in one element balance for a season does not reset in an hour. The cure needs time to read the room.
  4. Add the Ke restraint if needed. If the room still feels wrong after three days, the Ke move comes in. Cover the Fire object, move the Metal object, replace the Water object. The Ke move is the second pass, not the first.
  5. Review at Li Qiu. On July 7, walk the house again. The Sheng cure that worked in late June may be the wrong cure in early July. The Ke restraint may no longer be needed. The seasonal review is the moment to swap.
The cycles are a language, not a list. The homeowner who learns the language can read the room. The homeowner who only memorizes the list ends up with a blue cushion in every room and a fireplace they never light.

The one mistake the cycles predict in advance

Every year, in the recently of the Fire Month, the homeowner who has been reading feng shui all season makes the same mistake. They overcorrect. The south-facing room got a blue cushion in early June. The bedroom got a warm lamp in mid-June. The home office got a plant in the third week. By the end of the month the home is full of seasonal cures, and the homeowner is reluctant to take them down because the cures were carefully chosen and they worked. So the cures stay through July. By mid-July the home is in the wrong element balance for the new season, and the homeowner blames the cycles for not working.

The cycles were working. The homeowner stopped reading the room. A cure is a seasonal response, not a permanent fixture. The blue cushion comes out of the south-facing room on July 7, when Water is no longer the upstream element the room is asking for. The warm lamp in the bedroom stays, because the bedroom is Fire-short year-round, but the seasonal blue-on-the-couch and the seasonal Water-on-the-desk both come down at Li Qiu. The cycles are running whether the homeowner is reading them or not. A cure left past its season becomes a Ke move against the room.

This is the part of the practice that does not fit on a listicle. A listicle gives the homeowner ten things to do and never tells them when to undo them. The cycles tell the homeowner when to undo them. The undo is part of the practice.

What to do if the diagnosis does not match the cure

The honest version of the end-of-month checklist is that not every room will respond as the cycles predict. A south-facing room with three Water objects and a still-hot stove will not feel like a Water room. The cycles are a reading, not a command. When the room and the cycles disagree, the room wins. The homeowner should:

  • Re-check the element diagnosis. The dominant element of a room is not always the most obvious one. A white-walled room is not always Metal-dominant. A dark-wood-floored room is not always Wood-dominant. The element is the one the room feels like, not the one the room looks like.
  • Re-check the cure. A blue cushion on a couch the homeowner never sits on is not a cure, it is decor. The cure has to be in the working surface of the room, not in the corner.
  • Re-check the cycle. A Sheng cure in a room that is asking for a Ke move will land wrong. A Ke cure in a room that is asking for a Sheng move will land wrong. The cycles are two different arrows, and the cure has to be the right arrow.
  • Try the other cycle. If the Sheng cure did not work, the Ke cure is the next test. If the Ke cure did not work, the Sheng cure is the next test. The two cycles are not competing answers. They are sequential answers.

The homeowner who runs through all of these and the room still does not respond is the homeowner who should book a BaZi read. The room-side read has limits. The chart-side read carries the next layer. The cycles are the bridge between the two, and the bridge has two ends.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to do all five rooms tonight, or can I spread it across the week?
Spread it. The Fire Month does not end at midnight tonight. It ends on July 7, at Li Qiu, which is the start of Earth Month by the solar term calendar. A homeowner who does one room per night between now and July 7 finishes the seasonal close-out exactly on time. A homeowner who tries to do all five rooms in one evening ends up tired and second-guessing every move by the third room. The cycles reward slowness. The order of operations in the previous section is designed for a week, not a night.
What if my home does not have all five room types in the checklist?
Run the checklist for the rooms you have. The five rooms above are the common case. A studio apartment is a single room, and the cure is a single-cycle diagnosis: figure out what the one room is short on and what it has in excess, and add the Sheng cure for the missing element. A home with a separate dining room but no home office gets the dining room treated the same way as the kitchen (Fire-dominant, Water risk at the sink). The principle travels; the room list does not have to be exact.
Is the Li Qiu date always July 7?
No. Li Qiu lands on August 7 in the Western calendar some years. For 2026 the date is July 7. The date moves because the solar terms are tied to the sun's actual position, not to the Gregorian calendar. A homeowner who plans around "the seventh of July" instead of "the solar term Li Qiu" will be off by a few days in some years. The reliable approach is to check the solar term calendar each year rather than memorizing the date.
Can I apply the cycles to outdoor space too?
Yes, and the Fire Month is the season where the outdoor space matters most. A south-facing patio or balcony in late June is in the peak Fire phase of the seasonal cycle. A small water feature (a tabletop fountain, a shallow bowl refilled daily) is the Sheng cure for a Fire-dominant outdoor space. A blue or dark-colored outdoor cushion is the same move at smaller scale. The cycles do not stop at the door. The entryway in the checklist above is the boundary, but the cure can extend a few feet past the boundary if the outdoor space is in active use.
What if I am renting and cannot repaint or renovate?
The whole checklist is built around removable objects. Cushions, lamps, plants, ceramic bowls, doormats. None of the cures require a paintbrush or a contractor. A renter who runs through the checklist tonight can undo every move on July 7 by putting the objects back in the closet. The cycles are designed for the homeowner who lives with the season, not for the homeowner who redesigns the home for it. The latter is a different practice, and it is not the one I am writing about.
How do the cycles relate to the BaZi chart I got from the instant reading?
The chart-side read tells you which element the household carries. The room-side read tells you which element the home carries. The cycles are the layer where the two readings are translated into a single decision. A chart that runs hot (excess Fire) plus a south-facing living room that runs hot is a diagnosis the cycles can address with a Water cure in the room and a Water-friendly season for the chart-side decisions (the chart-side decisions are larger, but the seasonal reading on the chart will point to a Wood year or a Water year when the homeowner should weigh the chart-side moves more heavily). The free instant reading prints the Day Master and the element balance; combined with the room-side diagnosis, the cycles make the bridge between the two readable.
What about Xia Zhi, the summer solstice? Is the fire month starting on June 21 or ending today?
Xia Zhi on June 21 is the peak of the Fire Month, not the start of it. The Fire Month begins around Li Xia (May 5-7) and runs through Li Qiu (July 7-8). Xia Zhi is the midpoint. A homeowner who started the Fire Month in early May has had eight weeks of seasonal practice. A homeowner who started on June 21 has had nine days, which is not enough time to test the cycles properly. Either way, the close-out on Li Qiu is the same: the seasonal cures come down, the room is re-read, and the Earth Month opens.
