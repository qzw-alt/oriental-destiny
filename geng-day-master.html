The Symbol of Geng Metal — Axe, Sword, and the Seventh Stem

庚 (Geng) is the seventh of the Ten Heavenly Stems and the original Yang Metal of the Five Elements (Wu Xing 五行). In the classical reading, Geng is the broad axe — the weapon that clears a forest — or, more refined, the ceremonial sword that draws the line between right and wrong. In mineral terms, Geng is the raw ore, the unrefined metal pulled from the mountain, heavy and pure. It is not the delicate jeweller's file (that is Xin Metal, the Yin counterpart); it is the bold, decisive instrument that shapes by cutting.

From a BaZi perspective, Geng belongs to the Metal element, sits in Yang polarity, and carries the upright, decisive, justice-oriented energy of late summer turning into autumn — the moment when the heat of the year breaks and the first cold clarity arrives. Unlike Xin Metal (辛), which is the needle, the jewellery, the precision tool, Geng is the broad blade. Where Xin refines a single point, Geng sweeps the whole field. Where Xin negotiates, Geng announces. This is the foundational difference between the two Metal Heavenly Stems, and it is the single most important thing to remember when reading a chart that contains Geng anywhere — Year, Month, Day, or Hour pillar.

Geng is also the Heavenly Stem whose classical role is to control Wood, the Heavenly Stem paired with the Monkey branch (Shen 申) as its primary same-stem branch, and the element that decides whether a chart is governed by restraint or by licence. It is the original decider — which is why every classical BaZi text treats the presence of Geng in a chart as a signal of strong, often public, often controversial, almost always decisive life force. Geng Metal Through the Five Element Lens — The Yang Metal Cycle

No Heavenly Stem lives alone. Geng is the head of the Metal system, and every Geng chart is read against the Five Element generating and overcoming cycles that Geng participates in. Understanding those relationships is the difference between knowing Geng's name and reading a Geng person.

Earth produces Geng Metal. Earth is the ore-bed, the forge, the mountain that holds the metal inside itself. Wu Earth (Yang) and Ji Earth (Yin) are the resource pillars of any Geng chart. A Geng chart with abundant Earth is well-fed and capable of being forged into something durable and sharp. A Geng chart with no Earth is brittle and ungrounded — sharp above, but with no handle. This is why the Earth branches (Chen Dragon, Wu Horse, Xu Dog, Chou Ox) are the natural resource branches for Geng Metal, and why a late-summer birth is classically considered the most nourishing environment for a Geng native.

Geng produces Water. When metal is heated, it condenses moisture; when a sword is drawn in cold air, dew forms on the blade. In the human chart, the Water that Geng produces shows up as output, expression, and the decisive word or action that cuts through confusion. Ren Water (Yang) is Geng's direct output — the great river, the public speech, the unambiguous announcement. A Geng chart with abundant Water tends to produce generals, public prosecutors, surgeons who publish, and leaders who name the problem out loud.

Fire controls Geng Metal. Fire is the forge, the tempering flame, the heat that turns raw ore into steel. Bing Fire (Yang) is the great furnace — the fire that forges the strongest blades; Ding Fire (Yin) is the refining flame — the precision heat that polishes. In a Geng chart, Fire shows up as discipline, refinement, external pressure and the people or events that test whether the metal will hold. A measured amount of Fire is essential — without it, the metal never becomes a blade, only an ore. Too much Fire, and the metal melts.

Geng controls Wood. Wood is the forest, the obstacle, the thing that must be cut for the path to be clear. In the chart, Wood appears as wealth, the material result of Geng's cutting — but also the very thing Geng is always working against. Jia Wood (Yang) is the towering tree — Geng's direct wealth; Yi Wood (Yin) is the vine — Geng's indirect wealth. A Geng chart controls its Wood by virtue of its sharpness — the sharper the blade, the more decisively it shapes the terrain. This is the basis of Geng's natural relationship to wealth and the source of its lifelong productive drive — and its lifelong friction with anything that grows.

The Geng Metal Personality — Axe, Sword, General

Geng Metal Day Masters are the generals and surgeons of the BaZi world. Like a broad axe that swings in a single arc, Geng carries the energy of decisive, principled, public action. These are people who make hard decisions cleanly, who organise chaos with one cut, and who often end up in roles where others are unwilling to go first.

Geng's first and most consistent gift is courage. Where other stems may weigh the options indefinitely, Geng tends to look once, decide, and commit. The courage is sometimes mistaken for recklessness, but it is not — it is the metal's natural resistance to indecision. Geng waits only until the line is clear; once it is, Geng crosses it without looking back.

Geng's second gift is justice. Of all ten Heavenly Stems, Geng is the one most concerned with right and wrong — not in the abstract moral sense, but in the practical sense of who owes what, who has broken what, and what must be done about it now. A Geng person asked to mediate a dispute will, if honest, take a side. They are not diplomats; they are judges.

Geng's third gift is endurance. Metal does not tire quickly. Geng people can sustain high-output, high-pressure work for years in a way that few other stems can match. They are the partners, executives and surgeons who hold the line through a decade-long crisis and emerge having outlasted everyone around them.

The Ten Gods that Meet a Geng Metal Day Master

Every BaZi chart is read through the Ten Gods (Shi Shen 十神) — the relational archetypes that determine how each pillar, branch and hidden stem speaks to the Day Master. For a Geng chart, the Ten Gods are:

Direct Wealth (Zheng Cai 正财) — Jia Wood. The visible income, the salary, the material structure that Geng's cutting produces. For Geng, Jia Wood is the towering tree it controls; it represents the steady accumulation of assets through decisive, large-scale action.

Indirect Wealth (Pian Cai 偏财) — Yi Wood. The windfall, the investment return, the speculative gain. For Geng, Yi Wood is the vine it cuts; it represents the side revenue, the unexpected opportunity, the deal that arrives sideways rather than through direct action.

Direct Officer (Zheng Guan 正官) — Ren Water. The authority, the law, the legitimate constraint. For Geng, Ren Water is the great river; it represents the institutional structure that the blade respects — the constitution, the chain of command, the framework inside which Geng can act at full power.

Seven Killings (Qi Sha 七杀) — Gui Water. The pressure, the competitor, the crisis. For Geng, Gui Water is the deep underground current; it represents the hidden force that, in moderate amounts, sharpens the metal's resolve, and in excess drowns it. A Geng chart's relationship to Gui Water is one of the most consequential single indicators in the entire chart.

Direct Resource (Zheng Yin 正印) — Wu Earth. The mother's love, the formal education, the quiet support. For Geng, Wu Earth is the mountain that contains the ore; it represents the deep nourishment that arrives through inheritance, tradition, or institutional trust. Wu in a Geng chart is a profound stabiliser.

Indirect Resource (Pian Yin 偏印) — Ji Earth. The unconventional mentor, the deep intuition, the inheritance. For Geng, Ji Earth is the field; it represents the nourishment that arrives sideways — through craft, through apprenticeship, through the kind of wisdom that does not come from a classroom but from years of patient making.

Direct Output (Shi Guan 食神) — Gui Water. The refined expression, the careful word, the steady dew. For Geng, Gui Water is the underground current expressed with precision; it represents creativity that is precise, restrained, and well-distributed. A Geng chart with abundant Gui Water produces writers, surgeons, and patient strategists rather than loud generals.

Hurting Officer (Shang Guan 伤官) — Ren Water. The bold expression, the sharp tongue, the public announcement. For Geng, Ren Water is the great river; it represents creativity that is loud, immediate, sometimes careless. A Geng chart with abundant Ren Water produces provocateurs, founders, and movement leaders — but also isolated ones.

Friend (Bi Jian 比肩) — Geng Metal. The same-stem ally, the peer, the sibling energy. For Geng, another Geng in the chart represents competition and companionship simultaneously — the forge of same-stem metals, where they either temper each other or crack each other.

Rob Wealth (Jie Cai 劫财) — Xin Metal. The Yin counterpart, the sibling who is similar but different. For Geng, Xin in the chart represents the friend who is also a competitor — sometimes the closest ally, sometimes the one who quietly takes the role Geng thought it had secured.

Industries and Roles to Approach Cautiously

Geng can survive anywhere, but tends to underperform in roles requiring endless consensus-building, ambiguity, or quiet diplomacy. Geng's principled nature starves in purely relational or compromise-driven settings. The Ten Gods in BaZi framework explains why — Geng charts dominated by Hurting Officer or Seven Killings energy are uniquely unsuited to long chains of command or slow consensus processes.

Geng Metal as a Leader

Geng leaders lead by example rather than announcement. Their authority is forged — the blade the team trusts. They tend to act first, explain second, and outlast louder personalities. Their weakness is occasional rigidity and a tendency to over-cut in situations that needed time. The fix is the same as the chart fix: build a team whose Day Masters complement yours, with Earth (resource) and Water (output) support.

Geng Metal in Love and Relationships

In love, Geng is the rarest contradiction: deeply loyal internally, severely selective externally. Geng people fall slowly and once. They commit for life. Their reputation for being "the difficult one" in a partnership is less a personality feature than a Geng-native Metal expression — Yang Metal is the blade, and the blade only fits one sheath.

For marriage, Geng is best matched with someone whose Day Master brings either softness (Wood, so the blade has something to shape) or grounding (Earth, so the metal has somewhere to rest). Yi Wood + Geng is the classic axe-vine couple; Ji Earth + Geng is the blade-foundation couple; Bing Fire + Geng is the forge-tempered couple. Geng tends to fall hardest in autumn (their seasonal affinity month) and to commit through long acts of public loyalty rather than verbal declarations. Geng shows love by being present — protecting, providing, defending — and they need partners who recognise that fierce structural devotion as love.

Geng Metal Health — Elemental Balance and Wellbeing

Health in BaZi is read through the Five Elements — which element is in surplus and which is missing. A Geng-native born into a chart dominated by Metal and Earth is typically robust, with strong lung (Metal) and spleen/stomach (Earth) function. A Geng-native born into a chart with weak Earth or excessive Fire may experience lung stress, dryness, skin conditions, or chronic respiratory tension. Geng's health is fundamentally a question of Earth and Water balance.

Organ Systems Geng Should Protect

Geng governs the lungs, large intestine, skin, and skeletal structure. Autumn-born Geng are more sensitive to dryness and respiratory conditions (which, in classical Chinese medicine, is stored in the lungs). Excess Fire in the annual or luck pillar (such as the summer months of 2026's Fire Horse year) puts extra load on Metal organs, which is why Geng years need more hydration, rest, and breathing support than usual.

Lifestyle Recommendations for Geng

Prioritise sleep, hydration, breathwork, and gentle lung-supporting routines. Limit smoking and pollution exposure (the lungs' main toxin) and prolonged screen exposure (the skin and eyes' main strain). Movement practices like walking, swimming, martial arts, and slow strength training serve the Geng body better than purely passive routines. Geng Metal Wealth — Money, Investment and Legacy

Geng is the zodiac-stem most associated with decisive wealth. Wealth comes to Geng not through patient accumulation but through sharp action at the right moment. Geng people read risk and decisive variables better than almost any other stem, which is why they are best-positioned for trading, surgery-as-business, executive ownership, crisis-driven real estate, and any investment that rewards the willingness to act first.

Geng Money Personality

Geng money personality is decisive by default and aggressive when the situation is right. The Geng person will walk away from a deal that violates their standard, and will pour capital into a deal they believe in with full force. The biggest wealth risk for Geng is over-committing to a single position, or — paradoxically — under-committing because no opportunity matches the standard.

Best Vehicles for Geng Wealth

Equity in their own businesses, blue-chip positions held with conviction, real estate acquired decisively, infrastructure investments, and crisis-driven opportunities. Geng should generally avoid slow consensus funds, overly diversified passive vehicles, and any investment that requires them to out-wait a market they cannot see.
