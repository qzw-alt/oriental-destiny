Dragon Vein Feng Shui: Why Your Street's Layout Matters More Than Your Furniture

Ever driven down a street and felt that one house just... works? Calm, settled, like the ground itself agrees with it. The house two doors down? Same layout, same builder, feels off.

That's the dragon vein — the line of mountain qi that runs through a site. And no, it's not just for countryside mansions with actual mountains behind them. Your street has one too.

Here's how to find the line running through your property, and why it matters more than any single furniture placement.

Published July 10, 2026 · 14 min read

Why the dragon vein gets its own pillar piece

This series opened with the front garden on the first and read the property room by room from there. Each article used the word dragon in passing — the dragon sits behind the front garden, the dragon rises through the back garden, the dragon reads through the balcony as channel rather than anchor. The dragon was always the protagonist. It just never got the article. The dragon vein (Long Mai, 龍脈) is the line of mountain qi that runs through a site, and most homeowners have been told the term applies only to rural or imperial properties. That is half-true. The classical form school of feng shui reads the dragon through real mountains, and a Beijing courtyard with the Western Hills forty kilometers away is doing the classical read. The compass school reads the dragon through the line of qi that runs through any site, and a city apartment with a river three blocks away is doing the compass read. Both reads are correct. Both reads are what this article walks.

The reason this article exists separately from the outdoor room walk: the room walk reads the dragon vein's expression on the property (where the qi surfaces, where the qi damming, where the qi is moving fast). The dragon vein pillar piece reads the dragon vein's structure (where the line enters, where the line bends, where the line exits). Structure and expression are different scales of the same phenomenon. The homeowner who has followed this series has learned what the dragon feels like at the back garden wall. This article is what the dragon is doing underneath the wall.

The dragon vein is not a metaphor for the property's energy. It is the line of qi that runs through the property, and the form school and the compass school have spent two thousand years arguing about how to measure it. The argument is the proof. A metaphor does not need a measurement method.

What the dragon vein is, in the form school read

The form school reads the dragon vein as a real geographic feature. The dragon is a mountain ridge, the vein is the line of qi running along the ridge, and the property sits on the vein the way a bead sits on a string. The form school inherits the imperial surveyor tradition, where feng shui was the practical skill of finding a capital city's site by reading the mountains. Beijing sits where it sits because the Western Hills give the city a dragon. Nanjing sits where it sits because the Purple Mountain gives the city a dragon. Xi'an sits where it sits because the Qinling give the city a dragon. The form school reads the dragon at the scale of the province, and a homeowner standing in their yard is standing at the very tail of a line that runs for hundreds of kilometers.

The classical form school reads the dragon through four features: the head, the body, the turn, and the tail. The head is where the dragon rises. The body is where the dragon runs steady. The turn is where the dragon bends. The tail is where the dragon descends. A property that sits on the head reads as exposed (the qi is rising fast and the homeowner is in the rushing water, not the still pool). A property that sits on the body reads as settled (the qi is running calm and the homeowner is in the long middle stretch). A property that sits on the turn reads as compressed (the qi is bending and the bend is what makes the property interesting). A property that sits on the tail reads as depleted (the qi is running out and the homeowner is in the last stretch before the dragon leaves the site).

The form school read is the read for rural and suburban properties with real mountains within sight. A homeowner in the Berkeley Hills, the Wasatch, the Front Range, the Catskills, the Scottish Highlands, the Swiss Alps, the Italian Apennines, the Japanese countryside, or any of the mountain belts where the form school developed is doing the form school read. The mountains are visible. The dragon is visible. The reading is the line between the highest peak the homeowner can see and the lowest valley they can see, traced through the property. A property that sits on that line is sitting on the dragon vein.

Why the form school still applies when the mountains are far away

The homeowner who lives in a city of eight million people with no mountain within fifty kilometers is not exempt from the form school read. The form school reads at the scale of the watershed. A city in a flat watershed has a weak dragon. A city in a hilly watershed has a strong dragon. A city that sits at the foot of a watershed's mountain front has the dragon at its door. The homeowner who lives in a flat city still has a watershed read, and the read is the lay of the land at the regional scale. A flat city that sits on a coastal plain has a water-dragon (the river that drains the plain to the sea is the dragon). A flat city that sits on a plateau has an earth-dragon (the plateau edge is the dragon). The form school is not about whether the mountains are close. It is about whether the homeowner can read the line.

What the dragon vein is, in the compass school read

The compass school reads the dragon vein as the line of qi that runs through a property in a direction the luo pan can measure. The compass school inherits the imperial calendar tradition, where feng shui was the practical skill of finding an auspicious burial site or a capital palace's orientation by reading the earth's magnetic grid. The compass school reads at the scale of the property. A homeowner with a luo pan stands in their yard, takes a bearing, and reads the line of qi that crosses the property at that bearing. The line is not always aligned with a real mountain. It is aligned with the property's own energetic structure.

The compass school reads the dragon through three features: the entry, the crossing, and the exit. The entry is where the line of qi enters the property from outside. The crossing is where the line passes through the property's main living space. The exit is where the line leaves the property on the other side. A property with a clear entry, a long crossing, and a gentle exit is a property the compass school reads as settled. A property with an obstructed entry, a short crossing, or a sharp exit is a property the compass school reads as compressed. The line is always there. The line is always moving. The reading is whether the line can move freely through the site.

The compass school is the read for urban apartments, townhouses, and any property where the form school is hard to apply (no mountains in view, no watershed edge, no obvious ridge or river). The compass school reads the apartment's dragon vein as the line of qi that runs from the front door through the living room to the back door (or to the largest window). The apartment is doing the compass school read if the homeowner can stand at the front door, look toward the back of the apartment, and see a straight line. The line is the dragon vein. The reading is the line.

The form school and the compass school, in one sentence The form school asks where is the mountain? The compass school asks where is the line? A property that is doing both reads is doing the full classical read. A property that is doing one read is doing the practical read for the site. A homeowner who has neither a mountain nor a luo pan can still do a hand-drawn read (described below).

The four-line property read, for the homeowner without a luo pan

The four-line read is the practical read for the homeowner who has neither a mountain nor a compass and who wants to walk the dragon vein on a flat city lot. The four lines are: the approach line, the entry line, the interior line, and the water line. Each line is read by walking the property from the outside in, then from the inside out. The four lines together give the homeowner the dragon vein read at the scale of the lot, even when the form school's mountain read is not available.

The approach line is the path the homeowner takes to reach the property. It runs from the nearest major landmark (a transit station, a main road, a church spire, a tall tree line) to the front door. The approach line is the first line the dragon vein draws, and it is the line most homeowners do not think about because they walk it every day. A straight approach line (the homeowner walks toward the property on a single bearing for the last 200 meters) is a settled read. A bent approach line (the homeowner turns twice in the last 200 meters) is a compressed read. A blocked approach line (the homeowner cannot see the property from the nearest landmark) is a depleted read.

The entry line is the line from the property's front edge (the gate, the front door, the building entrance) to the property's first interior threshold (the foyer, the hall, the lobby). The entry line is the second line the dragon vein draws, and it is the line most directly under the homeowner's control. A straight entry line with no obstacle is a settled read. A bent entry line (the front door opens to a hallway that turns) is a compressed read. An entry line that runs through a mirror, a staircase going up, or a bathroom door is a depleted read. The entry line is the read for the dragon's mouth, and the homeowner's first work is at the mouth.

The interior line is the line from the entry threshold to the deepest point of the property the homeowner can stand in (the back garden, the back bedroom, the kitchen sink, the apartment's largest window). The interior line is the third line the dragon vein draws, and it is the line that tells the homeowner where the dragon lives on the property. A long interior line with a settled destination (a window with a view, a garden with a tree, a quiet corner) is the settled read. A short interior line with a wall or a window looking into a wall is a depleted read. A bent interior line that turns at least twice is a compressed read. The interior line is the read the July outdoor thread has been walking, room by room.

The water line is the line of any water feature on or near the property (a river, a stream, a pond, a fountain, an indoor water feature, the kitchen tap, the bathroom drain). The water line is the fourth line the dragon vein draws, and it is the line that stops the dragon. The classical rule is that water collects qi, and a dragon vein without water at its terminus runs off the property. A property with water at the deepest interior point (a fountain in the back garden, a river three blocks beyond the back fence) is a property with a settled dragon. A property with water at the entry (a fountain by the front door, a pool in the front yard) is a property with a depleted dragon, because the water has collected the qi before it can run through the interior. The water line is the read for the dragon's tail.

The three-gate read, on the property's main entry

The three-gate read is the practical read for the entry line, and it is the read that decides whether the dragon can enter the property. The three gates are: the outer gate, the inner gate, and the mouth gate. Each gate is a threshold the dragon must cross to enter the property's living space. A property with all three gates clear is a property the cycles read as open. A property with one gate blocked is a property the cycles read as compressed. A property with two or more gates blocked is a property the cycles read as closed.

The outer gate is the threshold between the public way and the property's lot line. The outer gate is the gate the homeowner walks through when leaving the property to go to the street. For a house, the outer gate is the front fence or the front yard's edge. For an apartment, the outer gate is the building's front door. For a townhouse, the outer gate is the front door of the unit. The outer gate should be wide enough for two people to walk through side by side without turning. A narrow outer gate (a single-person door, a gate the homeowner has to turn sideways through) is a compressed read. A blocked outer gate (a delivery box directly outside, a hedge that has overgrown the path, a stairway that ends at a wall) is a depleted read.

The inner gate is the threshold between the property's lot line and the property's main living space. The inner gate is the gate the homeowner walks through when entering the home. For a house, the inner gate is the front door. For an apartment, the inner gate is the apartment's front door (the outer gate and the inner gate are stacked in an apartment building, which is why apartment reading is harder). For a townhouse, the inner gate is the same as the outer gate. The inner gate should open to a space where the homeowner can stand and see the next room. A front door that opens to a wall is a depleted read. A front door that opens to a hallway turning sharply is a compressed read. A front door that opens to a foyer with a sight line to the back of the property is the settled read.

The mouth gate is the threshold between the foyer or hallway and the property's main living room. The mouth gate is the gate the homeowner crosses every day without thinking. For a house, the mouth gate is the opening from the foyer into the living room. For an apartment, the mouth gate is the opening from the hallway into the living room. For a townhouse, the mouth gate is the same. The mouth gate should be wide enough that two people can talk in the doorway without one of them having to step back. A narrow mouth gate (a single door, a tight opening between two walls) is a compressed read. A mouth gate directly facing a window or a back door is the settled read, because the dragon can run straight through.

The three gates are the same dragon at three scales. The outer gate is the dragon entering the property's territory. The inner gate is the dragon entering the property's home. The mouth gate is the dragon entering the property's daily life. A dragon that can run through all three is a dragon the homeowner can live with.

The dragon vein on the city lot, the apartment, and the townhouse

The four-line and three-gate reads are the universal reads, but each property type has its own shortcut for finding the dragon vein. The shortcut is the read most homeowners can do without walking the property for an hour. The shortcut does not replace the four-line and three-gate reads. It points to where the dragon vein is most likely to be on the property type.

On the city lot (a house on a small lot in an urban neighborhood, the kind with a six-foot front yard and a deep back yard), the dragon vein shortcut is the long axis of the lot. The lot is usually deeper than it is wide, and the lot's depth is the dragon's run. The homeowner stands at the back fence and looks toward the front door. The line of sight is the dragon vein. A line of sight that is straight and unobstructed is the settled read. A line of sight that is broken by a tree, a shed, a fence, or a parked car is the compressed read. A line of sight that ends at a wall (the neighbor's garage, a brick wall on the alley) is the depleted read. The city lot dragon vein is the simplest read to do, and it is the read most city homeowners skip because the lot is small enough to dismiss.

In the apartment, the dragon vein shortcut is the line of sight from the front door to the largest window. The apartment's dragon vein is the apartment's depth, and the depth is the line between the two endpoints the homeowner cannot move (the door and the window). The homeowner stands at the front door and looks toward the largest window. The line of sight is the dragon vein. A line of sight that is straight and clear is the settled read. A line of sight that bends around a kitchen island, a partition wall, or a large piece of furniture is the compressed read. A line of sight that ends at a wall (the apartment is a corner unit with no window in the living room, or the window is on the same wall as the door) is the depleted read. The apartment dragon vein is harder to fix than the city lot because the homeowner cannot move the door or the window. The fixes are at the walls between.

On the townhouse, the dragon vein shortcut is the central stair. The townhouse's dragon vein is the stairwell, because the stairwell is the line that runs from the entry floor to the top floor without turning. The homeowner stands at the bottom of the stair and looks up. The line of sight is the dragon vein. A stairwell that is open (a stair with railings, not solid walls) is the settled read. A stairwell that is enclosed (a stair with solid walls on both sides, or a stair that turns at a landing) is the compressed read. A stairwell that ends at a ceiling (a stair to a finished attic with no opening above) is the depleted read. The townhouse dragon vein is the stairwell, and the homeowner who treats the stairwell as the property's spine is doing the classical townhouse read.

The day-master-by-element dragon vein reading

The dragon vein reads slightly differently for each of the five Day Masters, because each chart is sitting on a different element and reads the dragon vein through that element. The default dragon vein read above is the Earth-leaning standard, because the dragon vein is itself an Earth phenomenon (the line of qi running through the land). A chart whose Day Master is sitting on a different element will read the dragon vein through that element first. The breakdowns below are the per-chart adjustments for the second week of Earth Month, July 9 to July 16, which is the same week the indoor series is reading.

A Wood-heavy chart — Jia or Yi Day Master — reads the dragon vein as a Sheng source. Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates Earth, so a Wood-heavy chart whose property has a clear dragon vein is a chart whose Sheng chain is working at the property level. The recommendation is the standard dragon vein read with one addition: a single tree or large plant at the entry line. Wood is already the chart's element, and the plant at the entry confirms the dragon's source without the homeowner needing to do anything else. A Wood-heavy chart whose property has a tree at the entry by July 16 is a chart whose dragon vein will hold for the rest of July.

A Fire-heavy chart — Bing or Ding Day Master — reads the dragon vein as a Sheng mid-cycle. Fire creates Earth, and a Fire-heavy chart whose dragon vein is settled is a chart whose Sheng chain is feeding the property's land. The recommendation is to confirm the water line at the back of the property (the dragon's tail). A Fire-heavy chart whose dragon vein runs to a dry back garden or a back fence with no water feature is a chart whose Sheng chain is feeding but the water is not collecting. The fix is the smallest water feature the property can carry (a bird bath, a small fountain, a rain barrel with a spout). The Fire-heavy chart's dragon vein is the read where the water line does the most work.

An Earth-heavy chart — Wu or Ji Day Master — reads the dragon vein as home. The Earth-heavy chart's Day Master is the seasonal element, which means the Earth Month is the chart's month, and the dragon vein is the chart's own line. The recommendation is to leave the dragon vein alone and instead focus on the rooms the dragon vein passes through. The Earth-heavy chart's dragon vein is already reading correctly for the season, and the homeowner's attention is better spent on the rooms than on the line. This is the one chart where the dragon vein article is mostly a confirmation read rather than an action list.

A Metal-heavy chart — Geng or Xin Day Master — reads the dragon vein as a Sheng output. Earth produces Metal, so a Metal-heavy chart whose dragon vein is running clear is a chart whose Sheng chain is feeding the chart's element through the property. The recommendation is to confirm the inner gate. A Metal-heavy chart whose inner gate is clear (a front door that opens to a foyer with a sight line to the back of the property) is a chart whose Sheng chain is producing. The fix for a Metal-heavy chart with a blocked inner gate is the simplest door read (clear the shoes from the foyer, open the closet door if it faces the front door, remove the mirror that reflects the front door back at the entry). The Metal-heavy chart's dragon vein is the read where the inner gate does the most work.

A Water-heavy chart — Ren or Gui Day Master — reads the dragon vein as a Ke target. Earth damming Water is the Ke direction, and a Water-heavy chart whose dragon vein is running fast is a chart whose Ke cycle is being asked to do too much work. The recommendation is to slow the dragon at the interior line. The fix is one of three: a piece of furniture perpendicular to the dragon's path, a heavier rug across the dragon's run, or a tall plant at the interior line's deepest point. A Water-heavy chart whose dragon vein runs at full speed through the property is a chart whose Ke cycle will be exhausted by the end of Earth Month. Slow the dragon at the interior line, and the chart's Ke cycle holds.

The dragon vein and the chart, in one sentence The dragon vein is the Earth's line. The chart's Day Master is what the chart brings to the line. A Wood-heavy chart brings growth. A Fire-heavy chart brings warmth. An Earth-heavy chart brings the line itself. A Metal-heavy chart brings clarity. A Water-heavy chart brings flow. The homeowner's job is to read what the chart is bringing and adjust the line so the chart's contribution is the right one for the season.

The seven-day dragon vein pass: what to do this week

The dragon vein is the slowest-moving feature on the property, which is why the foundational read sits at the second week of Earth Month rather than the first. By July 10, the homeowner has walked the outdoor rooms, done the Li Qiu handoff, and walked the hallway and the home office. The dragon vein is what holds those reads together, and the seven-day pass below is the working read for the second week of Earth Month. By July 16, the dragon vein will have settled into its Earth Month baseline, and the homeowner who walked the line through the week will be reading the rest of the indoor rooms from a settled foundation.

July 10 (Fri)
The four-line read. Walk the property from outside in. Trace the approach line from the nearest landmark to the front door. Trace the entry line from the lot line to the first interior threshold. Trace the interior line from the entry to the deepest point. Trace the water line from the entry to the deepest water. Note where each line is straight, bent, or blocked.
July 11 (Sat)
The three-gate read. Stand at the outer gate and look inward. Stand at the inner gate and look inward. Stand at the mouth gate and look inward. Note where each gate is clear, narrow, or blocked. The gates are the dragon's three thresholds.
July 12 (Sun)
The property-type shortcut. Do the city lot read (line of sight from back fence to front door), the apartment read (line of sight from front door to largest window), or the townhouse read (line of sight up the central stair). Note where the shortcut line agrees with the four-line read and where it does not.
July 13 (Mon)
The compressed-line correction. For each line that was bent or blocked in the four-line read, identify the smallest change that straightens or clears it. One piece of furniture moved. One shoe rack cleared. One plant trimmed. The compressed-line corrections are the week's main work.
July 14 (Tue)
The water-line confirmation. Walk the water line from entry to deepest water. Confirm the dragon's tail has water. If the tail is dry (no water feature at the deepest point, a back garden without a fountain, an apartment without a water feature at the back), add the smallest water feature the property can carry.
July 15 (Wed)
The day-master adjustment. Wood-heavy: add a tree at the entry line. Fire-heavy: confirm water at the tail. Earth-heavy: leave alone, focus on the rooms. Metal-heavy: confirm the inner gate is clear. Water-heavy: slow the dragon at the interior line with one of the three fixes above.
July 16 (Thu)
The settled read. Walk the four-line read one more time. Note the differences from July 10. The lines should feel longer, straighter, less interrupted. The gates should feel wider. The water line should feel collected. The dragon vein has settled into its Earth Month baseline.

Frequently Asked Questions

My property is on a corner lot with two street frontages. Does the dragon vein split?
A corner lot is a lot the dragon vein reads as having two entries. The dragon does not split. The dragon chooses one of the two frontages as the entry line and the other as the water line. The choice is made by the homeowner walking the lot and noting which frontage has the deeper interior line (the frontage the homeowner can walk further into before reaching a wall). The deeper frontage is the dragon's entry. The shallower frontage is the dragon's water. A corner lot with two equal-depth frontages is a corner lot the dragon reads as ambiguous, and the recommendation is to commit one frontage as the entry by treating it as the property's front (the path the homeowner takes to walk the dog, the path guests take to reach the front door). The commitment is the read.
I rent an apartment and cannot change the front door position. What is the dragon vein read for a renter?
A renter's dragon vein is read through the lines the renter can move, which are the interior line and the water line. The renter cannot move the front door or the largest window, so the entry line and the approach line are fixed. The interior line (from the foyer to the deepest point the renter can stand in) is movable through furniture placement. The water line (from the entry to the deepest water feature, which in a rental is usually the kitchen sink or the bathroom) is movable through small water features (a tabletop fountain, a fish tank, a large plant that needs daily watering). The renter's dragon vein is the dragon that the renter can build inside the lines the renter cannot move. The build is the read.
My property is on a hill. Does the dragon vein run uphill or downhill?
The dragon vein runs from the highest point of the property to the lowest point. On a hill property, the dragon's head is at the top of the hill and the dragon's tail is at the bottom. A property whose front door is at the top of the hill is sitting at the dragon's head, which is the exposed read (the qi is rising fast and the homeowner is in the rushing water). A property whose front door is at the bottom of the hill is sitting at the dragon's tail, which is the depleted read (the qi is running out and the homeowner is in the last stretch). The settled hill read is a property whose front door is partway up the hill, with the dragon's body running from above the front door to below it. A hill property with the front door at the middle is the hill version of the body read.
What about a flat property with no slope at all?
A flat property is a property the dragon vein reads as body. There is no head, no tail, no turn. The dragon is running calm through the whole property. The flat property's reading is the interior line (long and unobstructed is the read), the three gates (clear is the read), and the water line (settled at the deepest point is the read). A flat property with all three reads settled is a flat property doing the full classical read without the form school's mountain requirement. The flat property is doing the compass school read natively, and the homeowner's work is at the gates and the water line rather than at the slope.
Does the dragon vein change when I move furniture?
The dragon vein itself does not change. The expression of the dragon vein at the interior line changes when the homeowner moves large pieces of furniture perpendicular to the dragon's run. A sofa placed across the dragon's interior line compresses the dragon's expression at that point. A sofa placed parallel to the dragon's interior line opens the dragon's expression. A bookshelf placed at the end of the interior line stops the dragon's run. A bookshelf placed off the interior line lets the dragon keep running. The homeowner who understands the dragon vein reads furniture placement as a way to adjust the dragon's expression rather than to decorate the room. The furniture is the read.
How does the dragon vein relate to the bagua map?
The dragon vein and the bagua map are two different scales of read. The dragon vein is the line that runs through the property. The bagua map is the eight-section overlay that divides the property into life areas. The dragon vein crosses all eight bagua sections along its run, and the section where the dragon enters is the section the homeowner should treat as the property's entry area (usually the front door falls in one of three bagua sections: Knowledge, Career, or Helpful People). The section where the dragon exits is the section the homeowner should treat as the property's settling area (usually the back garden or the back bedroom). The dragon vein's run is the bagua map's spine, and the homeowner who reads both is reading the property at two scales at once.
