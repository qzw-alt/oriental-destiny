Stairway Feng Shui: Reading the Vertical Line Between Floors

The July thread has walked the horizontal gates from the sidewalk to the living room over the last week. Today's piece walks the vertical gate most homeowners forget — the stairway that connects the floors, runs the dragon vein between the public story and the private story, and reads as either a settling line or a draining line depending on where it begins and where it ends. A forty-five minute stairway read with the three posture cases (ascending, descending, level-breaking) and the four-stair fix checklist for the homeowner who climbs the stairs twice a day without ever reading them.

Published July 17, 2026 · 11 min read

Why the stairway is the gate the horizontal reads miss

Every gate the July thread has walked so far runs along a single floor: the outer gate at the sidewalk, the inner gate at the front door, the mouth gate at the threshold into the main living room, all on the ground floor. A homeowner who lives in a single-story home has done the full property read by walking the three horizontal gates. A homeowner who lives in a two-story home, a townhouse with a basement, a duplex with a loft, or any property the vertical dimension matters in has read three gates but missed the line that runs between them. That line is the stairway, and the stairway is the gate the horizontal reads forget.

The stairway is the gate because the stairway is the line the dragon vein takes when the property has more than one floor. The dragon vein enters the property through the outer gate, runs across the ground floor through the inner and mouth gates, and reaches the stair landing at the bottom of the first flight. From the landing the dragon moves vertically up the stair, across the landing at the top of the flight, and into the second floor's main room. The horizontal gates (outer, inner, mouth) catch the dragon as it arrives. The stairway catches the dragon as it changes level, and the change-of-level is the moment the dragon's read either settles or drains.

A stairway that runs from a settled ground-floor gate to a settled second-floor room carries the dragon up cleanly. A stairway that runs from a settled ground-floor gate to an unsettled second-floor room drags the dragon up into the unsettled room, and the settled ground-floor read becomes irrelevant because the dragon never stays long enough on the ground floor to settle there. A stairway that runs from an unsettled ground-floor gate to a settled second-floor room drags the dragon up past the unsettled ground floor into the settled room above. The stairway is the gate that determines which floor the dragon settles on, and the homeowner who has done the horizontal reads but skipped the stairway has done a one-floor read on a two-floor property.

The stairway read, in one sentence Read the stair landing the dragon reaches first (bottom of the first flight, the spot where the dragon stops between ground floor and second floor). Read the posture of the stair: ascending (dragon climbs), descending (dragon drains down), level-breaking (dragon is stopped halfway). Fix the four stair-defects in order: a stairway that ends at a wall, a stairway that begins under a beam, a stairway that lands facing a bathroom, a stairway that runs opposite the front door. The homeowner who has done the four-step checklist has read the vertical gate.

The three posture cases

Every stairway in residential feng shui is one of three postures: ascending, descending, or level-breaking. The posture is set by the relationship between the bottom landing and the top landing, and the relationship is read by standing on the bottom landing and looking up. Ascending means the top landing is higher than the bottom landing (the staircase climbs). Descending means the top landing is lower than the bottom landing (the staircase drops, the homeowner is going down). Level-breaking means the top landing is the same level as the bottom landing but the stairway passes through an intermediate landing halfway up (the staircase splits the dragon's vertical run into two separate flights). Each posture has a different read, and each posture has a different set of fixes.

Ascending stairways are the most common in two-story houses, townhouses with second-floor living, and Cape Cods with finished attics. The dragon climbs the stair, and the read at the top landing is the read that matters. A stairway that ascends to a settled second-floor room (a bedroom with a good window seat, a study with a settled view, a bathroom with a deep tub) carries the dragon into the settled room, and the second floor becomes the floor the dragon prefers. A stairway that ascends to an unsettled second-floor room (a bedroom that opens onto a noisy street, a study that shares a wall with a neighbor's kitchen, a bathroom at the end of a long bare hallway) carries the dragon into the unsettled room, and the second floor becomes a draining floor the homeowner avoids without knowing why.

Descending stairways are the most common in split-level homes, raised ranches, and any property where the homeowner walks DOWN a flight to reach the main living floor (the kitchen and dining are below the front entry, the bedrooms are below the main living floor). The dragon drains down the stair, and the read at the bottom landing is the read that matters. A stairway that descends to a settled ground-floor room carries the dragon down into the settled room, and the ground floor becomes the settling floor. A stairway that descends to an unsettled ground-floor room carries the dragon down into the unsettled room, and the ground floor becomes a draining floor the homeowner avoids.

Level-breaking stairways are the most common in homes with a basement, in duplexes with a mezzanine, and in any property where the staircase includes a halfway landing that turns the staircase by 90 or 180 degrees. The dragon stops at the halfway landing, and the read at the halfway landing is the read that matters. The level-breaking stairway is the most-missed posture in residential feng shui because the homeowner who has the level-breaking stairway reads it as either ascending or descending, depending on which floor they enter from. The homeowner who lives in a property with a basement-level family room, a main-floor kitchen, and an upper-floor bedroom set is reading a level-breaking stairway twice a day, and the read is set by the halfway landing the homeowner walks past without naming it.

The four-stair fix checklist

The four-stair fix checklist walks each of the four most common stairway defects and names the read each defect causes and the fix that lands. The checklist is the part of the stairway read every homeowner can do in a single pass with a notepad, and the checklist is the bridge between the three postures and the deliberate act at the stairway. The four defects are sorted by how often the homeowner can fix them without a contractor: defects 1 and 4 are logistics fixes (the homeowner moves something), defects 2 and 3 are structural fixes (the homeowner may need a contractor for the underlying issue even though the feng shui fix is non-structural).

  1. A stairway that ends at a wall. The dragon climbs (or drains) to the top (or bottom) landing and meets a wall. The wall is the compressed read at the gate the horizontal reads call the mouth gate, but it is also the depleted read at the stairway. The dragon has nowhere to go on the other side. The fix is to give the dragon a sight line past the wall: a mirror on the wall that doubles the depth, a runner that extends the visual length of the stair, a piece of art on the wall that the dragon can reach (an image of a path, a horizon, an open field). The wall stops the dragon because there is nothing past it; the fix is to give the dragon something past it.
  2. A stairway that begins under a beam. The dragon enters the stairway from a hallway or room where the ceiling beam runs perpendicular to the stairway, and the beam sits directly above the first three or four steps. The beam is the ke read at the bottom landing, and the ke cuts the dragon as it enters the stairway. The homeowner under a beam feels drained by the stairs, not uplifted by them. The fix is to soften the ke with a runner that runs perpendicular to the beam (the runner reroutes the dragon around the ke), a hanging plant at the bottom of the beam (the plant absorbs the ke), or a low ceiling fixture that breaks the beam's line. The ke cannot be removed (it is a structural beam), but the ke can be redirected.
  3. A stairway that lands facing a bathroom. The dragon climbs the stair and lands at a top landing (or bottom landing, in a descending stairway) that opens directly onto a bathroom door. The bathroom is the draining element (water drains qi), and the dragon drains as soon as it reaches the landing. The fix is to close the bathroom door when the dragon is at the landing (always), to set a solid screen at the landing (a tall plant, a folding screen, a heavy curtain) if the bathroom door is open, and to keep the bathroom's lid down and its floor dry. A bath fan that runs for fifteen minutes after every shower pulls the wet qi out of the room before it reaches the landing. The drainage cannot be eliminated; the drainage can be reduced.
  4. A stairway that runs opposite the front door. The dragon enters the property through the front door, crosses the inner gate, and meets the bottom of the stairway. If the stairway runs directly opposite the front door (the homeowner can see the bottom step from the front door), the dragon takes the line of sight straight up the stairs, across the second floor, and out through the second-floor window or skylight at the top of the stair. The dragon never settles on either floor. This is the most draining of the four defects because the dragon leaves the property entirely. The fix is to break the line of sight at the bottom step: a tall plant positioned perpendicular to the stair, a bookcase against the wall at the stair's foot, a screen or curtain that the homeowner walks around to reach the stairs. The line of sight is the leak; the fix is the obstruction.
The four-stair checklist, in one sentence A stairway that ends at a wall (give the dragon a sight line past the wall with a mirror, a runner, or an open-field image). A stairway that begins under a beam (reroute the dragon with a perpendicular runner, a hanging plant, or a soft ceiling fixture). A stairway that lands facing a bathroom (close the bathroom door, set a solid screen, run the bath fan fifteen minutes after every shower). A stairway that runs opposite the front door (break the line of sight with a tall plant, a bookcase, or a screen). One checklist, one pass, four reads, four fixes.

The stairway and the Day Master overlay

The Day Master overlay from July 15 applied to the three horizontal gates (outer, inner, mouth) on a single floor. The stairway extends the overlay to the vertical dimension, and the extension works the same way the overlay works on the horizontal gates: the Day Master names which floor the dragon prefers, and the lean-into / leave-alone / control cycle read applies to the stair landing the dragon reaches first. A homeowner who has done the Day Master overlay on the horizontal gates can re-run the overlay on the stair landing in under five minutes, and the stair-landing overlay is the piece that turns the one-floor read into the multi-floor read.

A Wood Day Master (a homeowner whose day pillar is Jia or Yi) reads the stairway as a Wood read: the dragon climbs, Wood wants the vertical line, and the lean-into is the top landing. The Wood Day Master should fix whatever defect is at the top landing first, because the top landing is the gate the dragon reaches after the climb. A Wood Day Master can leave the bottom landing alone for the next month, because the bottom landing is the gate the dragon has already used to enter the property, and the Wood Day Master is in surplus there. A Metal Day Master (Geng or Xin) reads the stairway as a Metal read: the dragon wants the line of the stair marked with a single solid element (a runner, a handrail, a stair light). A Fire Day Master (Bing or Ding) reads the stairway as a Fire read: the stair lighting is the read, warm bulbs at the bottom and the top, no cool-white anywhere in the stairwell. An Earth Day Master (Wu or Ji) reads the stairway as an Earth read: the stair materials matter (wood over carpet, stone over tile), and the homeowner who is replacing the stair runner should pick the heaviest runner the property permits. A Water Day Master (Ren or Gui) reads the stairway as a Water read: the dragon wants to keep moving, and the stairway should feel open (no door at the top, no closet under the stairs, no storage wall blocking the underside).

The chart branch overlay from July 16 (the quieter partner the Day Master was born paired with) extends the stairway read the same way the Day Master overlay does. A Day Master that is fed by its own chart branch (Yin Wood on a Water branch, Yin Fire on a Wood branch) reads the stairway as reinforced by the chart branch, and the lean-into is the top landing for an ascending stair, the bottom landing for a descending stair. A Day Master that is controlled by its own chart branch (Wood on a Metal branch, Fire on a Water branch) reads the stairway as resisted by the chart branch, and the lean-into is the landing that gets the fix first because the chart branch is not going to do the carrying work. The stairway is the place where the Day Master and the chart branch show their productive-or-controlled relationship most clearly, because the stairway is the line the dragon takes when it changes direction. A Day Master that carries the dragon up the stair is a Day Master whose chart branch agrees. A Day Master that lets the dragon drain down the stair is a Day Master whose chart branch resists.

The stairway is the gate the horizontal reads forget. The homeowner who has done the three horizontal gates but skipped the stairway has done a one-floor read on a two-floor property. The fix is the four-stair checklist — one pass, four reads, four fixes, Saturday afternoon.

The forty-five minute stairway read pass

The pass is one continuous walk that takes forty-five minutes, a notepad, the Day Master from yesterday's free BaZi calculator, and a soft tape measure (for the sight-line check at defect 4). The walk has seven steps, and each step takes under seven minutes. By the end of the walk the homeowner has a one-word read for each of the three posture positions, a one-word Day Master lean for the stair landing, and a one-line deliberate act for the highest-priority stair defect from the four-stair checklist. The walk is the stairway read.

  1. Stand at the bottom landing and read the posture. Look up. The posture is ascending if the top landing is above the bottom landing. The posture is descending if the top landing is below. The posture is level-breaking if a halfway landing splits the run. Note the posture in one word.
  2. Walk to the stairway the dragon reaches first. For an ascending stairway, walk to the top landing. For a descending stairway, walk to the bottom landing. For a level-breaking stairway, walk to the halfway landing. Stand on that landing. The landing is the gate the dragon reaches after the vertical run. The landing is the read this article cares about.
  3. Run the four-stair checklist against the landing. End at a wall? Under a beam? Facing a bathroom? Opposite the front door (sight line measured from the front door)? Note which of the four defects apply. Note which is the worst defect (the one the homeowner can fix this week, the one that drains the most qi).
  4. Identify the Day Master's lean-into landing. Wood Day Master = top landing of an ascending stair. Fire Day Master = top landing of an ascending stair with good lighting. Earth Day Master = bottom landing of a descending stair. Metal Day Master = top or bottom landing of any stair that has a single solid element (a runner, a handrail). Water Day Master = the landing with the most open sight line. The lean-into landing is the landing the homeowner should fix first.
  5. Identify the Day Master's leave-alone landing. The leave-alone landing is the opposite end of the stair from the lean-into landing. Wood Day Master leaves alone the bottom landing. Fire Day Master leaves alone the landing without lighting. Earth Day Master leaves alone the top landing. Metal Day Master leaves alone the landing without a single solid element. Water Day Master leaves alone the landing with no sight line. The leave-alone landing is the landing the homeowner should not touch this month.
  6. Name the deliberate act at the worst defect. Pull the deliberate act from step 3. End at a wall: mirror, runner, or open-field image. Under a beam: perpendicular runner, hanging plant, or soft ceiling fixture. Facing a bathroom: close the door, set a solid screen, run the fan fifteen minutes after every shower. Opposite the front door: tall plant, bookcase, or screen to break the line of sight. One deliberate act, this Saturday.
  7. Re-walk the stairway in two weeks. Re-walk with the same notepad. Note whether the worst defect is resolved (and whether the read at the lean-into landing has shifted). Note whether the leave-alone landing has the depleted read the Day Master said it would have (if it does, the Day Master overlay is working). Re-walk is the part most homeowners skip, and re-walk is the part that tells the homeowner whether the stairway read is landing.

What the stairway read is good for (and what it is not)

The stairway read is good for three things. It is good for the homeowner who has done the three horizontal gates and has noticed the read doesn't settle on either floor — the stairway is the missing gate, and the four-stair checklist names the defects that explain the unsettled read. It is good for the homeowner moving from a single-floor property to a multi-floor property and wondering why the same Day Master overlay that worked in the old place doesn't land in the new place — the stairway is the new gate, and the Day Master's lean-into landing is the new lean. It is good for the homeowner who has been told the stairway is bad feng shui by a checklist (never put a bathroom under the stairs, never put a stairway under a beam, never put a stairway opposite the front door) and wants to know which of the checklist items applies to the homeowner's property in this home — the four-stair checklist is the version of the same checklist that tells the homeowner which defect is the drain and which defects are tolerated.

The stairway read is not a substitute for the property-type read on the horizontal gates. A homeowner who tries to read the stairway without first reading the outer, inner, and mouth gates is reading a vertical gate on a property whose horizontal reads are unknown, and the stairway read cannot tell the homeowner which floor the dragon prefers to settle on. The stairway read is also not a substitute for the chart read. A homeowner whose chart has a complex Day Master relationship (a Day Master that is controlled heavily by the chart branch, a Day Master that is fed heavily by the luck pillar) will get a more useful stairway read from a deeper report. The four-stair checklist and the Day Master overlay are the bridge from the foundation articles to the chart-level stairway read, not a replacement for the chart-level read.

The stairway read is also not a calendar-override. The horizontal gate reads are read in the current month (July is Earth Month, the third week after Li Qiu). The stairway read inherits the month, and the leaning the Day Master does at the top landing of an ascending stair in July (Earth Month) is slightly different from the lean in March (Wood Month). A Wood Day Master in July is in a month where Earth is dampening Wood's productive cycle, and the stairway lean in July is toward the bottom landing (the closer floor) rather than the top landing (the lean-into in Wood's own month would be the top landing). The homeowner who wants the month-specific stairway read can order the deeper report. The stairway read in this article is the month-general read, which is the most useful version for a homeowner who is doing the four-stair checklist for the first time.

Frequently Asked Questions

I live in a one-story home with no stairway. Does this article still apply?
The four-stair checklist does not apply to a single-story home, but the stair principles do. A single-story home has no vertical gate, so the horizontal gates (outer, inner, mouth) are the only gates. The homeowner who lives in a single-story home should walk the horizontal gates from the property-type articles (July 12, 13, or 14) and skip the four-stair checklist. A homeowner in a single-story home who notices the home still feels drained despite a settled horizontal read should check whether the home has a sunken living room, a step-down kitchen, a step-up entry foyer, or any other change-of-level that the homeowner has not been reading as a stairway. The sunken living room and the step-down kitchen are level-breaking stairways on a single floor.
My stairway has a closet under it. Is that a defect?
A closet under the stairway is the tolerated defect, not the worst defect. The closet under the stairway is the ke read at the bottom of the stair (the storage compresses the dragon's vertical run), but the closet is also the most common solution to the space-under-the-stairs problem and the homeowner who removes it has less storage and the same drain. The tolerated fix is to keep the closet door closed (the closed door contains the storage qi), to keep the closet organized (a cluttered closet drains as much as an open one), and to leave a single small item on the closet shelf that pulls the eye (a book, a folded blanket, a small plant). The dragon passes the closed closet, and the closed closet reads as settled. The dragon passing an open cluttered closet reads as compressed. The fix is the door and the order, not the removal.
What if my stairway has more than one of the four defects?
Most stairways have two or three of the four defects. The fix order matters. End at a wall is the worst defect (the dragon has nowhere to go). Under a beam at the bottom three steps is the second worst (the beam cuts the dragon as it enters). Facing a bathroom is the third worst (the bathroom drains the dragon after the climb). Opposite the front door is the fourth worst (the dragon leaves the property). The fix order is the rank order: fix the worst defect first (Saturday one), fix the second worst next (Saturday two), and so on. The homeowner who has four defects and fixes them all in one Saturday usually fixes none of them well, because the deliberate act at each defect needs a week to settle before the next deliberate act lands. One defect per Saturday, four Saturdays, four settled reads.
My Day Master's lean-into landing is the landing with the worst defect. Do I fix the defect or move the lean?
You fix the defect. The lean-into landing is the landing the Day Master wants to settle on, and the worst defect at that landing is the reason the Day Master is not settling there. The fix at the lean-into landing is the deliberate act that lands best because the Day Master is supporting the fix at that landing. The fix at a leave-alone landing (the opposite end of the stair) is a fix the Day Master is not supporting, and the same deliberate act at the leave-alone landing will land less well than the same deliberate act at the lean-into landing. This is the most useful case, not a contradiction: the worst defect at the lean-into landing is the highest-yield deliberate act on the four-stair checklist, and the homeowner who has done the deliberate act at the lean-into landing has done the stairway read.
Does the stairway read change when the homeowner adds a stair runner?
Yes — a stair runner is a single deliberate act that resolves multiple defects at once. The runner reroutes the dragon around the ke at a beam (defect 2), gives the dragon a sight line down the stair length (a partial fix for defect 1), and marks the stair with the single solid element the Metal Day Master needs (defect 5 is implied). The runner does not resolve defect 3 (bathroom drainage) or defect 4 (line of sight to the front door), which need separate fixes. The homeowner who is adding a stair runner as a deliberate act should pick the heaviest runner the stair can carry (a wool runner with a thick pad, not a thin synthetic), and should anchor the runner with a brass rod at the top and the bottom so the runner reads as a single solid line.
