The chart changes which gate the homeowner leans into, and the chart changes which fix the homeowner does first, but the chart does not change the read itself.

The Day Master is the most useful chart piece for a property read because the Day Master is the one chart piece that names the homeowner. The Four Pillars (year, month, day, hour) each tell a different story — the year pillar is the family line, the month pillar is the social season, the hour pillar is the inner life, and the day pillar is the self. The day pillar's Heavenly Stem is the Day Master, and the Day Master is the element the homeowner is reading the property through. A Wood Day Master reads the property with Wood's preferences. A Fire Day Master reads the property with Fire's preferences. The same gate reads settled, compressed, or depleted for everyone, but the fix that lands best is the fix that matches the Day Master's element.

The overlay this article does is the simplest useful overlay. There are more elaborate overlays (the full chart branch overlay, the luck-pillar overlay, the annual pillar overlay), and the homeowner who wants the elaborate read can order the deeper report. The Day Master overlay is the one the homeowner can do today, with the day pillar from any free BaZi calculator, against the property read from the last three days. The Day Master overlay is the bridge between the property-type foundation that just completed and the personal overlay that makes the read stick.

The Day Master overlay, in one sentence Read the property with the property-type trifecta (apartment, townhouse, city lot, three gates each). Read the self with the Day Master (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water, one element). Lean into the gate that matches the Day Master's productive cycle (the gate the Day Master is feeding) and leave alone the gate the Day Master is already in surplus of. The homeowner who has done both reads has done the July thread at home.

The Day Master in one paragraph (for readers who have not read the June series)

The Day Master is the Heavenly Stem on the day pillar of the BaZi chart — the Chinese character that names the element the homeowner is at their core. There are ten Heavenly Stems in the sixty-year cycle, and the ten Stems pair into five element pairs: Jia and Yi (Yang Wood and Yin Wood), Bing and Ding (Yang Fire and Yin Fire), Wu and Ji (Yang Earth and Yin Earth), Geng and Xin (Yang Metal and Yin Metal), Ren and Gui (Yang Water and Yin Water). The Yang Stem is the outward expression of the element (the loud version, the public version, the version the neighbors see). The Yin Stem is the inward expression (the quiet version, the private version, the version the homeowner takes to bed). A homeowner whose day pillar is Bing is Yang Fire — the sun at noon, the open flame, the version of Fire that lights up a room by walking into it. A homeowner whose day pillar is Ding is Yin Fire — the candle in the window, the stove burner, the version of Fire that warms the room without anyone noticing.

The five productive cycle is the cycle the Day Master uses to read what it needs: Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates Earth (ash), Earth carries Metal, Metal collects Water, Water feeds Wood. Each element is fed by the previous element in the cycle, and the element doing the feeding is the element the Day Master is comfortable with. Wood is comfortable around Water (Water feeds Wood). Fire is comfortable around Wood (Wood feeds Fire). Earth is comfortable around Fire (Fire creates Earth). Metal is comfortable around Earth (Earth carries Metal). Water is comfortable around Metal (Metal collects Water). The Day Master overlay on the property read is built on this comfort: the gate that matches the feeding element is the gate the homeowner should lean into, and the gate that matches the controlling element (the cycle that breaks the Day Master) is the gate the homeowner should leave alone.

The control cycle is the second cycle the Day Master overlay uses: Wood parts Earth, Earth absorbs Water, Water quenches Fire, Fire melts Metal, Metal cuts Wood. The control cycle is the cycle the Day Master uses to break things, and the gate the Day Master controls is the gate the homeowner can change with intent. A Wood Day Master can break an Earth gate (move a heavy planter, change a path) because Wood parts Earth in the cycle. A Fire Day Master can break a Metal gate (move a metal gate, replace a metal fixture) because Fire melts Metal. The control read is the most-asked-about read in the Day Master overlay, because the homeowner who knows their controlling element knows which gate they can re-shape with a single deliberate act.

The five Day Masters at the three gates

The chart below walks each of the five Day Masters through the same three gates (outer, inner, mouth) and names the lean-into gate, the leave-alone gate, and the gate the Day Master can control with intent. The read is the same for every property type; what changes between apartment, townhouse, and city lot is which physical gate (front stoop, front door, hallway threshold) maps to which Day Master preference. The mapping is given after the table.

Wood Day Master
Lean into the outer gate (Wood feeds Fire, the outer gate is the first gate the dragon reaches, the gate the Day Master wants to extend upward). Leave the inner gate alone (Wood is already in surplus at the inner gate because the inner gate is the gate the homeowner has built their daily routine around). Control the mouth gate with a single deliberate act — a tall plant moved, a vertical line drawn, a shelf raised — because Wood parts Earth and the mouth gate is the Earth gate (the gate the dragon stops at).
Fire Day Master
Lean into the inner gate (Fire is fed by Wood, the inner gate is the gate where the homeowner has hung the photos and the mirror, the gate the Day Master wants to light up). Leave the mouth gate alone (Fire is already in surplus at the mouth gate because the mouth gate is the warmest spot in the home). Control the outer gate with a single deliberate act — a light moved, a planter set, a path straightened — because Fire melts Metal and the outer gate is the Metal gate (the gate the dragon enters through).
Earth Day Master
Lean into the mouth gate (Earth is fed by Fire, the mouth gate is the gate where the homeowner has put the dining table, the gate the Day Master wants to settle around). Leave the outer gate alone (Earth is already in surplus at the outer gate because the outer gate is the most-stable spot in the property). Control the inner gate with a single deliberate act — a heavy mirror moved, a path rerouted, a doormat turned — because Earth absorbs Water and the inner gate is the Water gate (the gate the dragon crosses).
Metal Day Master
Lean into the outer gate (Metal is fed by Earth, the outer gate is the gate the Day Master wants to mark with a single solid line). Leave the inner gate alone (Metal is already in surplus at the inner gate because the inner gate is the gate the homeowner has installed the door hardware around). Control the mouth gate with a single deliberate act — a round object placed, a metal bowl set, a circular mirror hung — because Metal collects Water and the mouth gate is the Water gate (the gate the dragon stops at).
Water Day Master
Lean into the inner gate (Water is fed by Metal, the inner gate is the gate the Day Master wants to keep moving through). Leave the outer gate alone (Water is already in surplus at the outer gate because the outer gate is the most-open gate in the property). Control the mouth gate with a single deliberate act — a mirror moved, a depth-creating rug placed, a path extended — because Water quenches Fire and the mouth gate is the Fire gate (the gate the homeowner has put the warmest room around).

How the three property types change the mapping

The Day Master overlay is the same five elements at the same three gates, but the physical gate that maps to outer / inner / mouth is different in each property type. The mapping is the part the homeowner cannot skip, because the mapping is the part that turns the abstract overlay into a specific act at a specific gate.

In an apartment, the outer gate is the building's front door (read from the lobby, the call button, the elevator ride), the inner gate is the apartment door, and the mouth gate is the threshold into the main living room. The apartment Day Master overlay leans into the inner gate (the apartment door) and leaves the outer gate (the building's lobby) alone, because the apartment owner does not control the lobby. The control cycle in an apartment is most often applied at the mouth gate (the threshold into the main living room), because the apartment owner can move a rug or a mirror at the mouth gate without touching anything the building controls.

In a townhouse, the outer gate is the front stoop, the inner gate is the front door, and the mouth gate is the threshold where the entry hall opens onto the main living room. The townhouse Day Master overlay leans into the inner gate (the front door) and leaves the outer gate (the front stoop) alone except for the lighting fix from the townhouse article, because the front stoop is constrained by the shared walls. The control cycle in a townhouse is most often applied at the mouth gate (the hallway-to-living-room line), because the townhouse owner can move a rug or a lamp at the hallway line without touching the shared walls.

In a city lot, the outer gate is the front gate at the sidewalk (or the curb cut if the driveway is the entry), the inner gate is the front door, and the mouth gate is the threshold into the main living room. The city lot Day Master overlay leans into the outer gate (the front gate) and leaves the inner gate (the front door) alone except for the path-and-planter fix from the city lot article, because the front door sits on a path the city lot owner has already designed. The control cycle in a city lot is most often applied at the inner gate (the front door), because the city lot owner can move a path, a planter, a light, or a doormat at the front door with a single deliberate act that the property line permits.

The Day Master overlay does not change the read. The Day Master overlay changes which gate the homeowner should do first, and which gate the homeowner should leave for next month. The overlay is the bridge between the property-type foundation (the four articles before this one) and the personal overlay (the report the homeowner orders when the read needs the chart in full).

The forty-five minute Day Master overlay pass

The pass is one continuous walk that takes forty-five minutes, a notepad, the property-type article the homeowner lives in (apartment, townhouse, or city lot), and the Day Master from any free BaZi calculator. The walk has ten steps, and each step takes under five minutes. By the end of the walk the homeowner has a one-line read for each of the three gates, a one-line Day Master lean for each gate, and a one-line deliberate act for the control cycle. The walk is the overlay.

  1. Find the Day Master. Use a free BaZi calculator (the one on the home page works). Note the day pillar's Heavenly Stem. Jia or Yi = Wood. Bing or Ding = Fire. Wu or Ji = Earth. Geng or Xin = Metal. Ren or Gui = Water. Note the Yang or Yin version. One word.
  2. Re-read the property-type article for the home the homeowner lives in. Apartment owners re-read July 12. Townhouse owners re-read July 13. City lot owners re-read July 14. The re-read takes fifteen minutes and is the part most homeowners skip because they assume they remember. The homeowner who skips the re-read is doing the overlay on a fuzzy read, and a fuzzy read plus a Day Master is still a fuzzy read.
  3. Walk the property-type pass for the home. Apartment pass = thirty minutes. Townhouse pass = thirty-five minutes. City lot pass = forty-five minutes. Note the read for each of the three gates in one word each (settled, compressed, depleted). The Day Master overlay is applied to a real read, not a remembered read.
  4. Identify the Day Master's lean-into gate. Wood = outer gate. Fire = inner gate. Earth = mouth gate. Metal = outer gate. Water = inner gate. The lean-into gate is the gate the homeowner should do the next fix at. The fix is the one the property-type article named, applied this week.
  5. Identify the Day Master's leave-alone gate. Wood = inner gate. Fire = mouth gate. Earth = outer gate. Metal = inner gate. Water = outer gate. The leave-alone gate is the gate the homeowner should not touch for the next month, even if the read is depleted. The leave-alone gate is depleted because the Day Master is already in surplus there, and adding more of the surplus element makes the depletion worse, not better.
  6. Identify the Day Master's control cycle gate. Wood = mouth gate. Fire = outer gate. Earth = inner gate. Metal = mouth gate. Water = mouth gate. The control cycle gate is the gate the homeowner can re-shape with a single deliberate act. The deliberate act is named in the next step.
  7. Name the deliberate act at the control cycle gate. Wood Day Master: move a tall plant at the mouth gate, or raise a shelf, or hang a vertical line drawing. Fire Day Master: move a light at the outer gate, or straighten a path, or set a planter. Earth Day Master: move a heavy mirror at the inner gate, or reroute a path, or turn a doormat. Metal Day Master: place a round object at the mouth gate, or set a metal bowl, or hang a circular mirror. Water Day Master: extend a path at the mouth gate, or place a depth-creating rug, or move a mirror to double the depth. One deliberate act, this Saturday.
  8. Write the three one-line reads on the notepad. Outer gate: settled / compressed / depleted. Inner gate: settled / compressed / depleted. Mouth gate: settled / compressed / depleted. The three one-liners are the property read the Day Master overlay sits on top of.
  9. Write the three one-line Day Master leans on the notepad. Lean into: [gate name]. Leave alone: [gate name]. Control cycle: [gate name]. The three leans are the bridge between the property read and the personal act. The homeowner who has all six lines on the notepad has done the July thread at home.
  10. Set the calendar entry for the deliberate act. Pick a Saturday in the next two weeks. Block ninety minutes. The first sixty minutes are the deliberate act. The last thirty minutes are the re-walk: walk the three gates again, note whether the deliberate act changed the read at the control cycle gate, and note whether the read at the lean-into gate improved or worsened. The re-walk is the part most homeowners skip, and the re-walk is the part that tells the homeowner whether the overlay is working.
The Day Master overlay, in one sentence Find the Day Master (one word). Re-read the property-type article (fifteen minutes). Walk the property-type pass (thirty to forty-five minutes). Identify the lean-into gate, the leave-alone gate, and the control cycle gate (three words). Do the deliberate act at the control cycle gate this Saturday. Re-walk in two weeks. The homeowner who has done those six steps has done the Day Master overlay.

What the overlay is good for (and what it is not)

The Day Master overlay is good for three things. It is good for prioritizing which gate to fix first when the read is depleted at more than one gate. It is good for telling the homeowner which gate to leave alone when the read is settled at a gate the homeowner is tempted to "improve" by adding more of the same element. And it is good for naming a single deliberate act the homeowner can do this Saturday, at the control cycle gate, with the confidence the act will land.

The Day Master overlay is not a substitute for the property-type read. The overlay is built on top of the read, and a homeowner who tries to do the overlay without doing the property-type pass first is doing the overlay on a fuzzy read, and a fuzzy read plus a Day Master is still a fuzzy read. The overlay is also not a substitute for the full BaZi chart. The Day Master is one piece of the chart, and the homeowner whose chart has a complex Day Master relationship (a Day Master that is controlled heavily by the chart branch, a Day Master that is fed heavily by the luck pillar) will get a more useful overlay from a deeper report. The overlay is the bridge from the foundation articles to the personal report, not a replacement for the personal report.

The Day Master overlay is also not a calendar-override. The property-type read is read in the current month (July is Earth Month, in the third week after Li Qiu). The Day Master overlay is read in the homeowner's chart month, which is set by the day pillar and the current month pillar combined. A Wood Day Master in July (Earth Month) is in a month where Earth is dampening Wood's productive cycle, and the lean-into read for the Wood Day Master in July is slightly different from the lean-into read in March (Wood Month, when Wood is in its own element). The homeowner who wants the month-specific overlay can order the deeper report. The overlay in this article is the month-general overlay, which is the most useful read for a homeowner who is doing the property-type pass for the first time.

Frequently Asked Questions

I do not know my birth hour. Can I still find my Day Master?
Yes — the day pillar is set by the solar date of birth, not the birth hour, and the day pillar's Heavenly Stem is the Day Master. The free BaZi calculator on the home page works with just the birth date (year, month, day) and returns the day pillar without the hour pillar. The hour pillar adds depth to the chart, but the Day Master is set by the day. The homeowner who knows the solar date of birth (the date on the Chinese solar calendar, which is usually the same as the Western calendar date except for births in late January or early February) can find the Day Master in under a minute. The homeowner who does not know the solar date of birth can convert the Western date to the solar date with the same calculator.
My Day Master is one element but my Chinese zodiac sign is a different element. Which one do I use?
The Day Master is the element the homeowner is at their core, and the Chinese zodiac (the animal sign, the year-branch) is the element the homeowner is in their social expression. Both are useful, and the two are not the same. The Day Master is the piece that names the homeowner's elemental self. The zodiac sign is the piece that names the homeowner's public persona. The overlay in this article uses the Day Master, not the zodiac sign. The homeowner who has both pieces of information can do a deeper overlay (the year branch overlay is the next article in the thread), but the Day Master alone is enough for the gate-level fix this article names.
What if my Day Master's lean-into gate is also the gate the property-type read flagged as depleted?
That is the most useful case, not a contradiction. A depleted read at the lean-into gate is the property-type read telling the homeowner the gate needs work, and the Day Master overlay telling the homeowner the gate is the right one to do the work at. The homeowner who has both signals at the same gate has the strongest case for a deliberate act at that gate this week, and the deliberate act is the property-type fix from the prior article (the planter, the doormat, the rug, the mirror, the light), applied with the Day Master element in mind. A depleted read at the lean-alone gate is a different case — the property-type read says depleted, the Day Master says leave alone, and the right move is to wait until next month when the Day Master's relationship to the gate has shifted.
I share a home with someone whose Day Master is a different element. Whose Day Master wins?
Neither wins, but the homeowner who has lived in the home longer reads the home with the deeper familiarity, and the homeowner's Day Master is the more useful overlay for the gates the homeowner uses daily. A couple where one partner is a Wood Day Master and the other is a Metal Day Master is reading the same gate with opposite lean-into reads (Wood leans into the outer gate, Metal also leans into the outer gate — in this case the lean-into agrees, and the deliberate act is the same for both). A couple where one partner is a Wood Day Master and the other is a Fire Day Master is reading the same gate with competing lean-into reads (Wood leans into the outer gate, Fire leans into the inner gate), and the right move is for each partner to apply the deliberate act at the gate they lean into, in the room the partner uses most. The two reads coexist, and the couple who has done both reads has the chart-on-property overlay for the shared home.
Does the Day Master overlay change when the homeowner moves?
The Day Master is set at birth and does not change. The property read changes when the homeowner moves, because the new property has a new property-type read (apartment to townhouse, townhouse to city lot, city lot to apartment, etc.) and a new gate mapping. The overlay re-runs at the new property: walk the property-type pass for the new home, identify the three gates in the new mapping, apply the Day Master lean-into / leave-alone / control cycle reads from this article. The homeowner who has done the overlay at two properties has the overlay they can re-run at every future move, and the overlay is the chart-side piece that travels with the homeowner from home to home.
