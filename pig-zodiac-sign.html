The Pig in the Chinese Zodiac — Origin, Mythology & Position

The Pig (Zhu 猪), known in older Daoist texts as the Wei (豕) or "earth-wallower," is the twelfth and final animal of the zodiac — the patient in whose body the entire twelve-year cycle comes to rest before the cycle begins again with the Rat. Its position is more than honorific. The Pig is what the calendar produces when everything has run its course: the late-autumn well-water that has filtered through every stratum of earth, the deep-still, Yin pool that mirrors the sky without disturbing it. The Pig is the sign most associated, in the Bencao Gangmu and earlier herbals, with abundance, fertility and home — which is why the Pig governs the kitchen, the marriage bed and the family altar in folk tradition.

From a BaZi perspective, the Pig belongs to the Water element (Hai-Water 亥水) and is Yin in polarity. Its season is the transition from late autumn to early winter (the ninth lunar month and the Hai 亥 two-hour watch from 21:00 to 23:00), when cold settles over the land but deep, invisible water still moves through the bedrock below the frost. This is why Pig-native Water is described as still, generous and deep — not the surface rippling of a Spring river but the long, quiet current beneath it. It carries hidden reserves of feeling, fertility and resource, which is why Pigs are associated with stored capital, the harvest pantry, and the discovery of wealth through patience rather than chase.

The Pig is the foot of the Eastern trigram pair (Dui ☱, the Lake), sitting directly opposite the Snake (Si 巳, the Fire on the mountain ridge across the valley). Anything you learn about the Pig is in dialogue with the Snake — Yin Water with Yin Fire, the still pool with the climbing flame — and the famous Si-Hai clash (巳亥冲) that runs through Pig chart-reading is the deepest expression of that opposition. Pig and Snake, Hai and Si, are one of the zodiac's defining yin-yang axes; wherever you meet the Pig, its opposite is waiting in the same room. The Pig's Day Master — Personality and Core Nature

The zodiac sign tells us the branch — the social mask. The Day Master (Ri Yuan 日元) tells us the soul. The Day Master is the heavenly stem on the day you were born and is the single most important data point in any BaZi chart. It is the lens through which a BaZi reader interprets everything else in your Four Pillars: how wealth treats you, how your relationships feel, which careers feel meaningful, which years are dangerous and which are golden.

Water Day Masters (Ren and Gui) — the Classic Pig

Ren-Water (Yang) is the deep ocean; Gui-Water (Yin) is the mist, the dew, the underground stream. Both flow naturally with the Pig's Hai-Water branch and make for some of the most perceptive, gentle personalities in the entire zodiac. These Day Masters turn an already-soft Pig into a person whose intuition works faster than their conscious mind. Read the Ren Day Master and Gui Day Master guides for the full signature.

Wood Day Masters (Jia and Yi) — the Producer of Pig Water

Wood in the generating cycle is fed by Water, which means a Pig-born person with a Wood Day Master has a constant, gentle, upward-growing motivation. They tend to be the most idealistic of the Pig variants — teachers, founders, parents, gardeners — people whose ambition is structured around care rather than conquest. See Jia Day Master and Yi Day Master for the deeper analysis.

Earth, Metal & Fire Day Masters — When the Pig's Element Diverges

Less common but deeply significant are Pig-born people whose Day Master is Wu, Ji, Geng, Xin, Bing or Ding. These charts create a tension between the branch element and the Day Master element — sometimes a clash (Fire Day Master on Water branch is a daily inner dual), sometimes a beautiful harmony (Earth containing Water, Metal producing Water). This is why two 1971 "Metal Pigs" can live in completely different emotional worlds.

Pig Personality Through the Five Elements Lens (BaZi Psychology)

BaZi reading has a much sharper vocabulary than the sun-sign zodiac. The Pig, when read through the Five Elements, is not a single archetype but a family of personalities distinguished by which element is strongest in the birth chart. Each configuration produces a recognisable inner type:

Water-dominant Pig. Diplomatic, perceptive, almost invisible. The Water Pig can read a room the moment they enter it. They are the kind who listen more than speak and who, when they speak, suddenly realise everyone has been waiting for them to. This is the most classic Pig profile.

Wood-dominant Pig. Generous, visionary, networked. The Wood Pig pulls people into projects rather than commanding them. Their empathy is real and visible. They are the slow, sturdy growers of the zodiac.

Fire-dominant Pig. Warm, expressive, romantic. The Fire Pig is the warmest of all Pig variants — visible, audible, unmistakably affectionate. They struggle most with rejection.

Earth-dominant Pig. The anchor. Earth Pigs finish what other Pigs start. They are slower to commit but utterly reliable once they do.

Metal-dominant Pig. Disciplined, principled, structured. Metal Pigs have a quiet moral code and a reputation for absolute follow-through — they bring the Pig's natural generosity into the form of durable institutions.

The Pig's Most Famous Opposite — The Snake and the 巳亥冲 (Si Hai Chong)

No Pig reading is complete without a serious treatment of the Snake and the 巳亥冲 (Si Hai Chong), the Snake-Pig direct branch clash. Si (巳, Yin-Fire) and Hai (亥, Yin-Water) sit six apart on the Earthly Branch cycle and carry incompatible elements: Fire boils Water, Water extinguishes Fire. This is one of the zodiac's six direct oppositions, and it is the most emotionally charged of all of them.

In romantic compatibility, Snake-Pig pairings run unusually hot. The Yin-Fire Snake is intense, indirect, magnetic and proud; the Yin-Water Pig is calm, sincere, generous and slow to confide. The initial attraction is real — both are signs of deep inner life — but the long-term clash is real too. Snake-Pig partnerships tend to produce either the most transformative love affairs of a person's life or the most quietly wounding, depending on whether the rest of the two charts (Day Masters, Ten Gods, luck cycles) supports or destabilises the branch relationship.

In chart reading, the appearance of Si and Hai in the same pillar-pair (e.g., Si in the Year Pillar and Hai in the Day Pillar, or Si and Hai across adjacent pillars) signals a year or decade of significant change — often relocation, a relationship ending, a vocation shift, or a sudden health awakening. The clash is not a punishment; it is a release of compressed pressure. Whatever it sweeps away, it sweeps away because that thing had become unable to grow. Read the Snake's complementary treatment in our Snake Zodiac Sign guide for the perspective from the other side of the clash.

Read this carefully: if you are a Pig and your partner, parent, child or business partner is a Snake, the clash does not doom the relationship — it makes it the most honest one you'll have. Snake-Pig partnerships are demanding only if they are dishonest. When both parties know what the clash means and treat each other accordingly (the Snake gives the Pig clarity of intention; the Pig gives the Snake tenderness of contact), they are some of the most enduring pairings in the zodiac.

For marriage, the Pig is best matched with someone whose Day Master brings structure (Metal or Earth element), so the Pig's Water can be held without flooding. Pig Health — Elemental Balance and Wellbeing

Health in BaZi is read through the Five Elements — which element is in surplus and which is missing. A Pig-native born in a chart dominated by Earth and Metal is typically robust, with strong kidneys (Water), bones and skin (Metal) and digestion (Earth). A Pig-native born into a chart with weak Water or excessive Fire may experience kidney stress, anxiety, insomnia or chronic dehydration. Pig health is fundamentally a question of Yin-Water regulation.

Organ Systems the Pig Should Protect

The Pig governs the kidneys, bladder, blood, and the circulatory system. Late-autumn-born Pigs are more sensitive to cold and to damp. Excess Fire in the annual or luck pillar (such as a Fire-heavy year or decade) puts extra load on Water organs, which is why Pig-born people need more deliberate rest, hydration and adrenal support than most signs during Fire-dominant years.

Lifestyle Recommendations for the Pig

Prioritise sleep, hydration, salt-balanced diets, and gentle kidney-tonifying routines. Limit stimulants (especially heavy alcohol and late-night screen time — both dehydrate Water). Movement practices like swimming, tai chi, qi gong, slow strength training and long walks near water serve the Pig body better than high-intensity interval training. Pig Wealth — Money, Investment & Abundance

The Pig is one of the zodiac's two natural money signs (the other being the Rat) — but where the Rat's money is hoarded, the Pig's money flows. The Pig is at their wealthiest when they are giving, sharing, hosting and feeding. Wealth that is held too tight loses its Pig energy; wealth that is circulated returns multiplied.

Pig Money Personality

Pig money personality is generous by default and conservative when protecting others. The Pig will spend freely on family, food and home, and then suddenly tighten when a long-term goal appears — a house, a child's education, a community project. The biggest wealth risk for Pigs is mixing up the two: spending on impulse in the name of generosity, then resenting the loss of reserves. The fix is a Pig-shaped budget — large flow categories for shared experience, small protected categories for long-term building.

Best Vehicles for Pig Wealth

Real estate (especially property shared with family), long-tenor investment portfolios, food and hospitality businesses, dividend-yielding equities, education savings for children, and any investment that compounds quietly. The Pig should generally avoid leverage-heavy, short-tenor trades and any investment that requires them to out-hustle a market they cannot see. Pair portfolio decisions with the Pig's Da Yun luck cycles so the timing matches.

What are the Pig years?

Pig years follow the 12-year cycle and include 1923, 1935, 1947, 1959, 1971, 1983, 1995, 2007, 2019, and the next being 2031. Each Pig year is also tagged with one of the Five Elements through its heavenly stem — see the Pig element variants table above.

The Pig's fixed branch element is Hai-Water (亥水, Yin Water). What is the Water Pig (水猪 Shui Zhu)?

It is the most distilled, classic Pig: gentle, intuitive, generous, slow to anger, almost impossible to rush. Ren-Water (Yang) Day Masters on a Pig chart produce a similar, slightly more outward-facing personality. Read more in our Gui Day Master and Ren Day Master profiles.

The 巳亥冲 is the Snake-Pig branch clash — Yin-Fire opposite Yin-Water in the zodiac cycle.
