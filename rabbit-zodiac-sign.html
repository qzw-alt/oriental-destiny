The Rabbit in the Chinese Zodiac — Origin, Mythology & Position

The Rabbit (Tu 兔) is the fourth animal of the twelve-year zodiac cycle, and the symbolic gate through which spring enters the Chinese calendar. Its position is more than chronological — it embodies the principle of renewal. The Rabbit is the creature that, in the founding myth recorded in the Shiji and the popular banquet race, finished a respectable fourth by choosing pace over panic. While the Rat rode the Ox and the Tiger bounded ahead, the Rabbit measured its jumps and arrived intact, well-rested and dressed for the occasion. That single image — unhurried, graceful, conserving energy — has defined the Rabbit's reputation for nearly two thousand years.

From a BaZi perspective, the Rabbit belongs to the Wood element (Mao-Wood) and is Yin in polarity — the softer, more flexible, more intuitive half of Wood. Its season is early spring, when the first green shoots push through cold soil and the year's stored life returns to the surface. This is why Rabbit-native Wood is described as yin — bending, woven, and able to survive frost. It is the Wood of vines and flowers rather than of oaks, and it is also why Rabbits are associated with grace, taste, art and the quiet politics of emotional survival.

The Rabbit also belongs to the Eastern trigram (Zhen ☳, the arousing thunder), sitting beside the Tiger (Yin branch, first month) and ahead of the Dragon (Chen branch, third month). The Rabbit's life story is forever the spring's hinge — it carries the quiet after the Tiger's roar and sets the stage for the Dragon's ascent. This is also why the Rabbit is the natural bridge between the impulsive, action-driven spring signs and the more visible, ambition-driven middle of the year.

The Rabbit's Day Master — Personality and Core Nature

The zodiac sign tells us the branch — the social mask. The Day Master (Ri Yuan 日元) tells us the soul. The Day Master is the heavenly stem on the day you were born and is the single most important data point in any BaZi chart. It is the lens through which a BaZi reader interprets everything else in your Four Pillars: how wealth treats you, how your relationships feel, which careers feel meaningful, which years are dangerous and which are golden.

You can find your Day Master by reading our What Is the Day Master in BaZi primer or by submitting your birth details on the free instant reading page. For a Rabbit-born person, the most common Day Masters and their flavour are:

Wood Day Masters (Jia and Yi) — the Classic Rabbit

Yi-Wood (Yin) is the most common Day Master for Rabbit-born people because of how cycles align. Jia-Wood is the tall pine, Yi-Wood is the climbing vine — both aligned with the Rabbit's Mao-Yin-Wood branch and make for gentle, integrative personalities. Read the Jia Day Master and Yi Day Master guides for the full signature.

Water Day Masters (Ren and Gui) — the Nourisher of Wood

Water nourishes Wood. A Rabbit-born person with a Ren or Gui Day Master is constantly being supplied with the element they natively embody. They tend to look self-contained, deeply perceptive and quietly wise. See Ren Day Master and Gui Day Master for the deeper analysis.

Fire, Earth & Metal Day Masters — When the Rabbit's Element Diverges

Less common but deeply significant are Rabbit-born people whose Day Master is Bing, Ding, Wu, Ji, Geng or Xin. These charts create a tension between the branch element and the Day Master element — sometimes a clash, sometimes a beautiful harmony. This is why two 1999 "Earth Rabbits" can live in completely different emotional worlds.

Rabbit Personality Through the Five Elements Lens (BaZi Psychology)

BaZi reading has a much sharper vocabulary than the sun-sign zodiac. The Rabbit, when read through the Five Elements, is not a single archetype but a family of personalities distinguished by which element is strongest in the birth chart. Each configuration produces a recognisable inner type:

Wood-dominant Rabbit. Gentle, artistic, slow to anger and slow to forget. The Wood Rabbit can sense the emotional temperature of a room the moment they enter it. They are the kind who finish other people's sentences, who hold the family's grief, and who, when they finally speak, are usually right.

Fire-dominant Rabbit. Warm, expressive, sociable. The Fire Rabbit is the most visible of all Rabbit variants — talkative, romantic, quick to laugh. They are the only Rabbit who can compete for attention with the Tiger and win by being warmer.

Earth-dominant Rabbit. The anchor. Earth Rabbits finish what other Rabbits start. They are slower to commit but utterly reliable once they do. They tend to accumulate property, savings and friendships at a steady pace.

Metal-dominant Rabbit. Disciplined, principled, structured. Metal Rabbits have a quiet moral code and a reputation for absolute follow-through. They are the only Rabbit who can hold a grudge for decades and still be the most trusted voice in the room.

Water-dominant Rabbit. Intuitive, sage-like, profoundly perceptive. The Water Rabbit is the rarest and the most quietly brilliant. They are the counsellors, the writers, the people other Rabbits go to when they cannot understand their own hearts.

    Rabbit Compatibility — Love, Marriage, and BaZi Pairings

    Rabbit compatibility is not luck. It is chemistry — one branch (Mao) meeting another, modulating through the Ten Gods of each person's Day Master. The most harmonious pairings fall into two groups: the San He (Three Harmony) with Goat and Pig, where the three branches form a complete Wood–Fire–Water trine; and the Liu He (Six Harmony) with the Dog, where Rabbit and Dog (Mao-Xu) form a confidential, deeply protective partnership.

    The most challenging pairings are the Six Clash (Liu Chong) with the Rooster — direct opposite branch, Mao and You, Wood versus Metal in its most controlling form — and the Punishment (Zi-Mao-Wu) with the Rat, which is subtler but creates quiet resentment over time.     Rabbit leaders lead through care rather than command. They tend to build quiet loyalty, delegate with empathy, and outlast louder personalities. Their weakness is over-delegation and avoidance of hard calls — a peacemaker instinct that becomes a bottleneck at scale. The fix is the same as the chart fix: build a team whose Day Masters complement yours.

    Rabbit Love and Relationships

    In love, the Rabbit is the rarest contradiction: deeply romantic internally, hesitant externally. They fall hard and often quietly. They commit to a small circle with fierce loyalty and protect that circle the way they protect their garden. Their reputation for "playing it safe" in romance is less a personality flaw than a Rabbit-native Yin-Wood expression — vine-wood is slow to climb, but it climbs higher and lasts longer than oak-wood.

    For marriage, the Rabbit is best matched with someone whose Day Master brings steadiness (Earth element) or warmth (Fire element), so the Rabbit's Wood can flourish without withering. Goat + Rabbit works because both branches belong to the same Three-Harmony trine and the Goat's Earth feeds the Rabbit's Wood; Pig + Rabbit works because the Pig's Yin-Water provides constant nourishment. They show love by making things beautiful — a meal, a room, a moment — and they need partners who recognise that quiet as devotion.

    Rabbit Health — Elemental Balance and Wellbeing

    Health in BaZi is read through the Five Elements — which element is in surplus and which is missing. A Rabbit-native born in a chart dominated by Water and Wood is typically robust, with strong liver function (Wood), supple joints and clear skin. A Rabbit-native born into a chart with weak Wood or excessive Metal may experience liver stress, joint pain, anxiety, or chronic fatigue. Rabbit health is fundamentally a question of Yin-Wood regulation.

    Organ Systems the Rabbit Should Protect

    The Rabbit governs the liver, gallbladder, tendons, eyes and nervous system. Spring-born Rabbits are more sensitive to wind and seasonal allergies. Excess Metal in the annual or luck pillar (such as a Metal-heavy year) puts extra load on Wood organs, which is why Rabbit charts need more rest, hydration and green-food support than usual.

    Lifestyle Recommendations for the Rabbit

    Prioritise sleep, hydration, leafy-green diets, and gentle liver-cleansing routines. Limit stimulants (especially alcohol and late-night work). Movement practices like yoga, tai chi, qi gong, swimming, walking in nature and slow strength training serve the Rabbit body better than high-intensity interval training.     Rabbit Wealth — Money, Investment & Abundance

    The Rabbit is one of the zodiac's natural wealth signs, though they accumulate quietly rather than loudly. Wealth comes to the Rabbit not through single dramatic wins but through patient curation — of properties, of relationships, of taste, of the right opportunities. They read people, markets and trends better than almost any other sign, which is why they are best-positioned for long-horizon wealth, real estate, art and equity in their own ventures rather than quick trading.

    Rabbit Money Personality

    Rabbit money personality is conservative by default and decisive when they trust the people involved. The Rabbit will sit on cash patiently, then deploy it quietly the moment they identify the right opportunity. The biggest wealth risk for Rabbits is over-saving in growth environments, or — paradoxically — sentimental "feeling-right" investments driven by indirect Resource or Food God energy in their chart.

    Best Vehicles for Rabbit Wealth

    Real estate, art and antiques, equity in their own businesses, long-tenor bond positions, blue-chip equity, and tangible assets that appreciate quietly. The Rabbit should generally avoid leverage-heavy, short-tenor trades and any investment that requires them to out-pace a market they cannot see. Pair portfolio decisions with the Rabbit's Da Yun luck cycles so the timing matches.

    The zodiac sign is a single data point. Da Yun (大运) — the ten-year luck cycles calculated from your birth month and gender — are what decide whether a Rabbit year in your thirties is a quiet decade or a transformative one. Each Rabbit-native goes through roughly seven Da Yun periods, each controlled by a different Heavenly Stem and Earthly Branch and each with a different feel for work, wealth, relationship and health.
