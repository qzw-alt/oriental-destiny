Door Gods Feng Shui: Never Let Them Face Each Other (and 5 More Rules)

门神 (mén shén) — the door gods — face outward. That direction is the whole answer. They are the one feng shui object that looks out the door instead of in, because every other object this month is trying to pull good things in, and these two are trying to keep bad things out. You have seen them a hundred times: two painted generals, one with a sword, one with a mace, pasted on the red doors of every Chinese temple and old house and restaurant you have ever passed. There are always two, and they always look the same way — at the street, not at you. Here is who they are, where they belong in 2026, and the six rules that stop a guard from becoming just a picture.

This is the sixth stop in the object walk. We have already read the Lucky Cat, who calls money in, the Gold Ingot, who carries it in, the Wealth Bowl, who holds it, and the Lucky Knot, who ties it down. All four face inward. Today's object faces out. It is the guard, not the greeter.

Published: 2026-08-31 · Reading time: about 9 minutes

What the door gods are: two men who stayed awake

The first thing to know is that there are always two. Never one. The pair is the whole idea. One guards each panel of the door, left and right, and together they make one complete watch.

There are two famous pairs, and both started as real guards who would not sleep. The older pair comes from ancient ghost stories. Two brothers were put in charge of a gate between the world of the living and the world of spirits. Their job was to check every spirit that tried to pass, and any ghost that had hurt the living, they caught and fed to a tiger. The very first door charm was just those two names written on a strip of peach wood and hung at the gate. No picture. Just the names.

The pair most people use today came later, from a true story out of the Tang dynasty. An emperor named Li Shimin had done some brutal things to win the throne, and afterward he could not sleep. The ghosts of the people he had wronged were knocking on his bedroom door every night. Two of his generals — Qin Qiong and Yuchi Gong — offered to stand outside his door in full armor all night. The emperor slept. So the generals stood there the next night too, and the next, until someone had a smarter idea: paint their faces on the palace doors and let the pictures take the watch. It worked, the story says. And that is why you see the two generals on red doors today. They are the ones who stayed awake so the house could sleep.

The one-sentence version: every object this month faces inward to bring good things in. The door gods face outward to keep bad things out. The direction tells you which job the object is doing.

Two kinds of pair — and they do different jobs

Not every pair on a door is the same. The tradition splits them by what they carry and which door they guard. Get this right and the pair works. Get it wrong and you have a soldier standing where a scholar should be.

The simple rule: the front door gets the generals, the study gets the scholars, and the two never swap. The generals carry weapons and face the street, because the street is where trouble comes from. The scholars carry a scroll and face the desk, because that is where the work is earned. A general pasted on the inside of a bedroom door is the same mistake as a kylin on the nightstand — a warrior where a gentle thing belongs.

What they actually do: they keep trouble out

Think about the other objects in this month's walk and the door gods suddenly make sense. The Lucky Cat raises a paw and calls money in. The Gold Ingot points its tips inward, sailing the cargo home. The Wealth Bowl opens its mouth to the room and holds what arrives. The Lucky Knot ties it all down. Every one of them faces in, because their job is to bring things home. The door gods are the opposite. Their only job is to keep trouble out, so they face the street.

That is why they belong with the month's other protectors, just in a different spot. The kylin faces the door from inside the house. The Taishan Stone anchors the threshold from behind. The bagua mirror hangs above the door and deflects what is aimed at it. The door gods stand on the door itself, the front line, the first thing trouble meets before it meets any of the others. They do not replace the kylin or the stone. They stand in front of them.

And now the rule the title is built on, because it is the one everyone gets wrong. The two figures must face outward — toward the street, toward whatever is coming — and never each other, and never inward. A pair facing each other is two guards staring at each other while the door is wide open. A pair facing inward is even worse: you have pasted a greeter where a guard belongs, and a greeter's job is to call things in, not keep them out. The Lucky Cat faces in because it beckons. The door gods face out because they do not. The direction is the whole sentence.

Where they belong in 2026

The door gods are the one object that lives on the door, and the door is where this year's trouble walks in. The site's own 2026 Flying Star read names three trouble spots this year: the southwest (accidents and losses), the north (illness), and the south (arguments). That trouble does not walk through walls. It walks through openings — doors and windows — and your front door is the biggest opening in the house. The door gods face outward for exactly this reason: they meet the trouble at the doorstep, before it gets inside.

So in 2026, a few things follow. If your front door sits in, or faces, one of those three trouble spots — southwest, north, or south — the door gods are doing double duty this year. They are the traditional guard, and they are also the fix for the exact spot the year has marked. If your front door faces a straight road or a T-junction aimed right at it — what the tradition calls a "road charge" — the door gods are the classic answer. And even if your door is plain and ordinary, the pair still earns its place, because the front door is the one place in the house a guard is never wasted.

Timing: we are in the tail of the Metal month, the stretch from Li Qiu on August 7 to White Dew in early September. The door gods are an active, outward, martial object, and this is a good window to check the door and settle the direction. Do it before White Dew and the season is on your side.

The money objects face in to bring fortune home. The door gods face out to keep the blow away. Same door, two jobs — and the direction tells you which one you are pasting.

Where the pair goes — in order

Only one placement matters for most people: the front door. The rest are extras. And you do not need to buy anything tonight — a New Year print, a woodcut, even two respectful images of a watchman will do, as long as there are two and they face the right way.

Height and condition matter as much as the spot. Hang them at eye level, not down at the floor — they should look down the street, not up at the baseboard. And they must be clean and bright, not faded or torn. A faded door god is a tired guard, and a torn one is a guard who already took a hit. The pair gets replaced every Lunar New Year: burn the old one, paste the new one, and keep the watch going.

The chart side: who needs a guard

The door gods are a protection object, so the chart question is a protection question. In BaZi, protection has a name: the Resource star, the element that feeds and shields your day master — think of it as the mother, the teacher, the wall that takes the hit for you. A strong Resource means a person is naturally looked after. A weak one means they are a little exposed. The door gods are that star in picture form: the home's guardian, the protector on the doorstep.

Finding your own Resource is easy. Ask what produces your day master. Water produces Wood. Wood produces Fire. Fire produces Earth. Earth produces Metal. Metal produces Water. That producing element is your protector.

  • Wood day master — your protector is Water. A rooted, well-watered tree reads as guarded; a dry seedling is exposed.
  • Fire day master — your protector is Wood. A flame with steady fuel is guarded; a fire burning its own wick is not.
  • Earth day master — your protector is Fire. Warm, ripening soil is guarded; cold clay is exposed.
  • Metal day master — your protector is Earth. Ore held safe in the mountain is guarded; ore left out in the weather is not.
  • Water day master — your protector is Metal. A spring fed from a metal source is guarded; a stream that dries in summer is exposed.

If your chart reads as guarded, the door gods are a nice-to-have. If it reads as exposed, they — along with the kylin and the Taishan Stone — are the external protection your chart does not already have. If you do not know your day master, the free reading names it in about a minute.

The rules: six don'ts, five dos

This is the part people save and forward. The first one is the reason the article has the title it has.

Don't

  • Never let them face each other. Two guards staring at each other while the door is open. Both face outward, toward the street.
  • Never paste them facing inward. An inward pair is a greeter, not a guard. It calls trouble in instead of keeping it out.
  • Never hang just one. The pair is the point. One figure is half a watch.
  • Never put the generals in the bedroom. A weapon-carrying warrior does not belong in the rest of the bedroom — same rule as the kylin.
  • Never leave a faded or torn pair up. A tired guard is a worn-out shield. Renew at New Year, or when you notice the damage.
  • Never toss the old pair in the trash. Like the red string, they are a participant, not packaging. Burn the old pair — Fire back to Fire — and paste the new one with intention.

Do

  • Hang a pair, at eye level, facing out. Two figures, two panels, looking down the street. The direction is the whole job.
  • Match the pair to the door. Generals on the front door, scholars on the study door, the Three Stars on an inner door.
  • Keep them clean and bright. A quick wipe once a month, same as the Lucky Cat.
  • Renew them at Lunar New Year. Burn the old, paste the new, keep the watch going. It is a yearly practice, not a one-time decoration.
  • Add the other guards if the door is hard. A road aimed at the door, or a door in a trouble spot — the door gods, the bagua mirror, and the Taishan Stone work as a team. Do not make one charm do three jobs.

What the door gods can't do

Every article in this thread ends with the honest part, and the door gods have real limits worth saying out loud.

They do not bring money. A pair on the door of a house with no income does not create income. That is the cat's job, the ingot's job, the bowl's job. The door gods are the shield, not the harvest.

They do not stop every bad thing. They guard the door, not the whole house. The rooms still need their own care this year — the southwest still wants stillness, the north still wants an empty counter, the south still wants one red thing gone. The door gods hold the door; the room fixes hold the rooms.

They do not work when they are faded. The image is not the magic. The maintenance is. A torn, peeled pair is a tired guard, and a tired guard is just a picture on the door.

They are not a fix for a broken door. If your front door sticks, sags, or will not open and shut cleanly, fix the door first. The guard stands on the door. A guard on a broken door is standing on nothing.

Not having them is not bad luck. A house without door gods is just a house without door gods. The tradition rewards the guarding. It does not punish the unguarded.

Quick questions

Who are the two door gods?
Two pairs, one old and one newer. The old pair are the brothers who caught harmful ghosts at a gate between the worlds. The pair you actually see on doors today are Qin Qiong and Yuchi Gong, two Tang-dynasty generals who stood guard all night so an emperor could sleep, until someone had the idea to paint their faces on the door instead. Both are martial. There is no single door god — the pair is the whole point.
Do they face inward or outward?
Outward. Toward the street, away from the house. This is the one rule that matters and the one everyone gets wrong. They keep trouble out, so they face what is coming. An inward pair is a greeter, not a guard — the inward face belongs to the Lucky Cat, who beckons. Both figures face out, never each other, never in.
Do I need them on every door?
No. The front door is the essential one. Inner doors are optional — the scholars go on the study door, the Three Stars on an inner family door. One well-guarded front door beats a pair on every inner door.
When do I replace them?
At Lunar New Year, which is the traditional time. Burn the old pair — not the trash, the same rule as the red string — and paste the new one with intention. If a pair is torn before then, replace it when you notice, not when the calendar says to.
Do they work in an apartment?
Yes. Your apartment's front door is the threshold. Same rules: a pair, at eye level, facing out, renewed yearly. The only difference is that your "street" is the hallway outside the door. You do not need a courtyard to have a threshold.
Do they clash with a bagua mirror or a kylin?
No, they stack. The bagua mirror hangs above the door and deflects what is aimed at it. The kylin faces the door from inside. The Taishan Stone anchors the threshold from behind. The door gods stand on the door itself, the front line. Same team, different posts. Do not pile all of them on one door pointlessly — pick the one that fits your door's actual problem.

What to do tonight

Five small steps, and the first takes two minutes. First, walk out your front door and turn around. Look at the threshold the way the street looks at it, and ask: does this door have a guard, or just a welcome mat? Second, if you have a pair, check three things — is there a pair, are they facing out, and are they clean. Third, fix the direction first if it is wrong. The facing is the whole job. Fourth, if you have no pair, do not rush out and buy one tonight — the intent is the activation, and two respectful images of a watchman, hung as a pair facing out, will do. Fifth, put Lunar New Year on the calendar as the renewal date, wipe the pair once a month, and let the watch keep going.
