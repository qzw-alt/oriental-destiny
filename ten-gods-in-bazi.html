Ten Gods in BaZi — What They Mean for Your Personality and Life Path

The Ten Gods are the language of relationship within your BaZi chart — they describe how the elements in your chart interact with your core self. Understanding them unlocks a much deeper reading of your personality, career tendencies, and relationship patterns.

Estimated reading time: 9 minutes

Once you understand the Four Pillars and the Five Elements, the next layer that transforms a BaZi chart from a collection of symbols into a living portrait is the Ten Gods. The Ten Gods are not deities — they are names for the ten possible relationships between the elements in your chart and your Day Master, the core energy that represents you.

Each element that appears in your chart can play a specific role relative to your Day Master: it can act as a resource that nourishes you, an authority that governs you, a companion that mirrors you, or a challenge that depletes you. These roles — the Ten Gods — give texture to your personality in ways that elemental balance alone cannot.

How the Ten Gods Work

The Ten Gods arise from the interaction of two factors: the element of a stem in your chart, and its relationship to the element of your Day Master, according to the generating and controlling cycles of the Five Elements.

The Five Elements cycle in two directions within BaZi interpretation:

  • Generating cycle: Wood generates Fire, Fire generates Earth, Earth generates Metal, Metal generates Water, Water generates Wood
  • Controlling cycle: Wood controls Earth, Earth controls Water, Water controls Fire, Fire controls Metal, Metal controls Wood

When an element in your chart generates the element of your Day Master, it is called a supporting God. When it controls your Day Master's element, it is called a challenging God. The names of the Ten Gods reflect the nature of these interactions.

The Ten Gods Explained

Resource (Yin Resource = Yi Yin)
Also called:印 (Seal), Scholar's Resource
A supporting God. The Resource element generates your Day Master's element — it is the energy that feeds, educates, and protects you. Having strong Resource energy in your chart means you have access to knowledge, healing, recognition from elders, and things given freely rather than earned through struggle.
Personality: Thoughtful, slow to act but thorough. A person with strong Resource tends to be a learner — reflective, sometimes introverted, drawn to knowledge and theory. They are often the person others seek for calm wisdom rather than flashy leadership.
Print (Yang Resource = Jia Yin)
Also called:枭 (Sha Vermin / harsh Seal)
The challenging version of Resource. Print represents knowledge and protection that comes with a twist — it can indicate learning difficulties, unconventional thinking that unsettles others, or protection that feels constraining. In small doses, it adds creativity and originality. In excess, it can cause confusion or instability.
Personality: Perceptive and unconventional. People high in Print energy are often early adopters, artists, or thinkers who see patterns others miss. They can also be restless or feel misunderstood.
Officer (Yin Officer = Ding Yin)
Also called:官 (Official), Authority
A supporting God. The Officer element controls your Day Master's element — it is the energy of structure, authority, rules, and public recognition. Having strong Officer energy means you are suited to roles where you lead, govern, or represent something larger than yourself. It is the energy of reputation and responsibility.
Personality: Ambitious, disciplined, and drawn to positions of responsibility. A person with strong Officer energy thrives in structured environments — corporate management, government, law, or any field where clear hierarchies and standards exist. They want to be seen as credible and authoritative.
Rob Wealth (Yang Rob Wealth = Yi Yang)
Also called:劫 (Jie), Seven Killings in some systems
A challenging God. Rob Wealth depletes your Day Master's element — it represents sudden losses, unexpected challenges, and the energy of being tested. Rob Wealth is not purely negative; it is the force that strips away what does not serve you, forcing growth through difficulty. It is associated with major life disruptions that, in retrospect, were necessary.
Personality: Resilient and direct. People high in Rob Wealth energy tend to face life head-on rather than avoiding conflict or difficulty. They can be blunt or fearless, sometimes insensitive to social niceties. In excess, they may take risks that undermine their own stability.
Friend (Yin Friend = Bing Yin)
Also called:比 (Bi), Comparison, Peer
A neutral/intermediate God. The Friend element shares the same element as your Day Master — it is the energy of peers, colleagues, equals, and competition. Strong Friend energy means you are social, collaborative, and at ease in group settings. The downside is a tendency toward comparison, rivalry, and difficulty standing out from the crowd.
Personality: Outgoing, team-oriented, and socially comfortable in nearly any setting. A person with strong Friend energy makes connections easily and often has wide social circles. They thrive through collaboration and can struggle when forced into isolation or solitary decision-making.
Rob Wealth (Yin Rob Wealth = Ding Yang)
Also called:财 (Wealth), Wealth Star
A challenging God. Rob Wealth depletes your Day Master's element — it represents money, assets, and material resources that must be actively pursued rather than given freely. In some systems this is simply called Wealth. It is the energy of tangible result: financial追求, physical comfort, and practical achievement. When strong in a chart, it suggests strong financial ambition and practical talent.
Personality: Practical, material-savvy, and financially oriented. A person with strong Wealth energy cares about tangible outcomes — income, property, comfort. They tend to be good with money, realistic about their surroundings, and often drawn to business or finance.
Eating Ghost (Yang Eating = Wu Yin)
Also called:伤 (Shang), Hurting Officer
A challenging God. Eating Ghost represents the energy that challenges authority and disrupts structure. It is the energy of the disruptor, the critic, and the creative rebel. Having strong Eating Ghost energy can indicate someone who questions rules, speaks uncomfortable truths, or creates through destruction of the old. It is associated with art, performance, and expression that challenges norms.
Personality: Expressive, creative, and sometimes provocative. A person with strong Eating Ghost energy tends to have a strong point of view and is not afraid to voice it. They can be artists, critics, entertainers, or entrepreneurs who disrupt industries. They sometimes struggle with authority and established structures.
Escaping (Yin Escaping = Wu Yang)
Also called:食神 (Shi Shen), Food God
A supporting God. Escaping represents the energy of creation, enjoyment, and sustained output. It is the energy of making things — art, writing, music, craft — and of enjoying life through sensory pleasure and creative expression. People with strong Escaping energy tend to be artistic, generous with their time, and deeply appreciative of beauty and good food.
Personality: Creative, generous, and pleasure-oriented. A person with strong Escaping energy is often the person who makes life more enjoyable for those around them — a good cook, a talented artist, someone who knows how to enjoy a meal or a sunset. They tend to be optimistic and expressive.

Ten Gods and Career Direction

The Ten Gods provide some of the clearest career guidance in BaZi. Your dominant God shows where your natural talents and motivations lie:

  • Heavy Resource: Academia, research, medicine, publishing, counseling, education, law (public interest)
  • Heavy Officer: Management, government, law, military, corporate leadership, banking
  • Heavy Friend: Sales, marketing, community management, politics, entertainment, hospitality
  • Heavy Wealth: Finance, trading, real estate, entrepreneurship, manufacturing, retail
  • Heavy Eating Ghost: Arts, entertainment, writing, design, innovation, teaching (philosophical)
  • Heavy Escaping: Culinary arts, hospitality, music, performing arts, travel, wellness

Ten Gods in Relationships

In relationship compatibility, the Ten Gods describe how two charts interact. Some combinations create harmony — Resource meeting Officer, for example, produces a supportive, stabilizing relationship. Other combinations create friction — Rob Wealth meeting Rob Wealth, for instance, can produce two strong personalities competing for the same resources.

Understanding this helps explain why some relationships feel effortless while others feel like constant work. It is not about right or wrong — it is about whether the energies naturally support each other or naturally resist each other.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the Ten Gods actual deities?

No. The Ten Gods are a metaphorical naming system for elemental relationships in your BaZi chart. They describe how the different energies in your chart interact with your core self — they are not religious deities or supernatural forces. The names (Resource, Officer, Friend, etc.) describe the character of each energy relationship.

Can I have all ten Ten Gods in my chart?

No single chart contains all ten gods equally. Each chart has some gods prominent and others absent or weak. What matters is the pattern: which gods dominate, which are missing, and how they interact across the pillars. A missing God is not necessarily bad — it means that energy is less available to you, which shapes your tendencies and what you must work harder to develop.

Is having strong Officer energy always good?

Not necessarily. Strong Officer energy gives you ambition, discipline, and leadership capability — but too much can make you rigid, controlling, or unable to delegate. A chart with excessive Officer energy may struggle with flexibility and creative thinking. Balance is key: Officer energy is most powerful when tempered by Resource wisdom and Friend social skills.

What does it mean if I have no Resource energy in my chart?

A chart lacking Resource energy may indicate someone who is more self-taught than formally educated, more practical than theoretical, and less drawn to academic or institutional recognition. This is not a weakness — it simply means you do not naturally attract the "free" support that Resource represents. You may need to create your own opportunities through direct effort rather than through recognized credentials or structures.

How are the Ten Gods different from the Five Elements?

The Five Elements describe the fundamental energy types in your chart (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) and their overall balance. The Ten Gods describe the relationships between those elements and your Day Master specifically. A chart can be Water-dominant, for example, but whether that Water acts as a Resource, an Officer, or a Rob Wealth toward your Day Master depends on its relationship to your Day Master's element.

Can the Ten Gods change over time?

The Ten Gods in your birth chart are fixed — they describe your innate energy relationships. However, your luck cycles bring different gods to the foreground in different life periods. A God that is dormant in your birth chart may become active during a particular ten-year cycle, changing your tendencies, motivations, and social environment during that period.
