Tai Sui 2026: The Grand Duke and the Fire Horse Year

The Tai Sui (太歲, Grand Duke) is the year's directional deity, and every year he sits in a different compass sector. In 2026 he is the Bing Wu Fire Horse, stationed due south. Renovations in the south are off-limits this year, sitting directions in the south take a beating, and households born under Horse need to read this one carefully. Here is what that means for your home.

Published June 20, 2026 · 10 min read

What the Tai Sui is, in plain language

The Tai Sui is one of the older concepts in the feng shui tradition — older, in some readings, than the bagua map itself. The short version is that the year carries a Heavenly Stem and an Earthly Branch (the year's pillar), and the Tai Sui is the personification of that pillar. He is the year's "face," and he sits in the compass direction of the year's branch.

In 2026, the year is Bing Wu (丙午) — Yang Fire on top of the Horse. The Horse sits due south on the 24 Mountains ring. So the Tai Sui of 2026 sits due south, in the Wu mountain, at 180 degrees on the compass.

The Tai Sui is not a malevolent deity. He is the year's presiding officer, and like most presiding officers he is happiest when respected and most annoying when provoked. The classical texts compare him to a magistrate who is courteous if you are courteous, obstructionist if you push. The move all year is to stay on his good side, which in practice means three things: avoid disturbing his sector, avoid clashing with his branch, and avoid disrespecting his earthly symbol.

Why this matters for households that do not "believe" in deities

You do not have to take the Tai Sui as a literal spiritual being for the prescription to be useful. The Tai Sui framework is a way of tracking when the year's directional energy is concentrated, and the framework is older than the modern academic skepticism that sometimes surrounds it. The houses that ignore the Tai Sui prescriptions tend to be the same houses that ignore the Flying Stars prescriptions, and they tend to have a slightly harder time of the year's trouble sectors than the houses that take the prescriptions seriously.

That said, the prescription is mild. The Tai Sui does not punish. He does not reach across the home and break your stove because you drilled a hole in the south wall. The prescription is a recommendation, not a karmic threat, and the year is navigable even for households that do everything wrong — it just costs a bit more in rearranging afterward.

The 2026 Tai Sui sits due south

The Bing Wu Fire Horse sits in the south at 180 degrees true north. On the luo pan's 24 Mountains ring, this is the Wu mountain (午). The Wu mountain is a 15-degree sector centered on due south, running from 172.5 degrees to 187.5 degrees on the compass.

The classical advice for the year is to avoid renovations in the Wu mountain — no drilling, no demolition, no heavy construction, no major ground disturbance in the south sector of the home or property. The reason is that physical disturbance of the Tai Sui's seat is read as "challenging" the year's presiding officer, and the year's presiding officer, when challenged, tends to push back. The pushback shows up as obstacles, delays, friction with authority, and the small bad luck of misfiled paperwork and missed calls.

This applies to the south of the home, the south of the property, and the south of the bedroom or office if those rooms happen to sit in the south sector of the building. If you have been planning a kitchen renovation and the kitchen happens to be in the south of your home, 2026 is the year to either postpone it or to do the work in a way that disturbs the south wall as little as possible.

If the kitchen is in the south of your home, 2026 is not the year to renovate it. If the bedroom is in the south, do not move the bed to a new wall this year. The Wu mountain wants to be still.

How the Wu mountain shows up in a real home

In a typical apartment, the south sector of the home is whatever room or rooms are at the south end of the floor plan. For a north-facing apartment, this is usually the living room or the master bedroom at the back of the building. For a south-facing apartment, this is the entryway and the front rooms.

In a single-family home, the south sector is the part of the house closest to the south property line. The south-facing wall, the south-facing windows, the south garden, the south driveway — all of these are part of the Wu mountain this year, and all of them want to be still.

The opposite direction is the north, at zero degrees on the compass, which is the Zi mountain (子). The Zi mountain is the Tai Sui's "supporting" sector, and it is where the year's energy flows most freely. The north sector of the home is a good place to add activity in 2026 — a water feature if the home can carry one, an open window, a workspace, or a room that gets more use than usual.

The clash direction, the break direction, and the harm direction

The Tai Sui prescription has four directional moves, not one. The first is the seat itself (Wu, south). The other three are the directions that "argue" with the seat, and each one argues in a different way.

The clash direction (冲, chong) is the branch that is six branches away from the year branch. In 2026, the year branch is Wu (Horse, south). The clash branch is Zi (Rat, north). The north of the home is the clash direction this year. Renovations in the north are not as heavily cautioned against as renovations in the south, but they are still a bad idea — the clash is "arguing" with the Tai Sui rather than "disturbing" him, and arguing is the second-most-unwelcome move.

The break direction (破, po) is the branch that is three branches away from the year branch, on the "breaking" side. For Wu, the break direction is Mao (Rabbit, east). The east sector of the home is the break direction in 2026. Disturbances here are read as fracturing the year's energy, and the classical advice is to keep the east quiet if possible.

The harm direction (害, hai) is the branch that is six branches away on the "harming" side. For Wu, the harm direction is Wei (Goat, southwest). The southwest sector is the harm direction in 2026, and disturbances here are read as bringing harm to the year's presiding officer.

The four directions (seat, clash, break, harm) are the four compass sectors to treat gently in 2026. The other four (east-south-east, south-south-east, west, north-west) are the sectors that can absorb normal disturbance without friction. Most households have at least one renovation planned for the year, and most renovations can be placed in one of these four freer sectors.

Households born under the Horse, and the Sui Po problem

People born in Horse years (Wu in the BaZi chart) have a different relationship with the Tai Sui of a Horse year. The year's presiding officer shares the branch with the person's natal branch, which is called "ben ming nian Tai Sui" (本命年, the year of one's own branch). The classical reading is that Horse people in 2026 are in their "offending" year — the year offends the Tai Sui by sharing his branch, and the Tai Sui, in turn, pushes back on the Horse.

The pushback shows up as the usual suspects: instability in career, friction in relationships, health wobbles, and the kind of bad luck that is not dramatic but is consistent. The classical prescription for Horse people in a Horse year is to wear red (specifically a red belt or red undergarment, not a red shirt), to visit the temple at Li Chun for the annual blessing, and to avoid major life decisions in the first three months of the year.

The Rat is in a more uncomfortable position. The Rat's branch (Zi) is the clash direction of the 2026 Tai Sui, and people born in Rat years are in their "Fan Tai Sui" (犯太歲) year — literally "offending the Tai Sui." The clash is the most energetic of the four arguments with the Tai Sui, and the prescription is stronger: avoid the south direction for major decisions, avoid the north for renovations, and consider a visit to the temple for the annual化解 (jie huo, resolving-harm) blessing.

Horse people share the year's branch. Rat people clash with it. The two are the year's most-watched natal signs, and the prescriptions for both are stronger than the prescriptions for households that have neither sign in the chart.

What about people who are neither Horse nor Rat

The rest of the twelve signs have a softer relationship with the 2026 Tai Sui. Dragon, Dog, and Goat are in "Triple Harmony" with the Horse and have a supportive year. Tiger and Rabbit are in "Six Harmony" pairs with the Horse and have a friendly year. Ox, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig, Rat, Tiger — the full rotation — all have different relationships, and a serious BaZi consultation reads the year-pillar against the natal four pillars to find the exact relationship.

For most households, the practical takeaway is the directional one: keep the south quiet, keep the north quiet, and if a member of the household is a Horse or Rat, pay attention to the personal prescriptions too. The combination of "Tai Sui direction in the home" and "natal sign in the chart" is what determines how much attention a given household should pay to the year's prescriptions.

How the Tai Sui sits next to the Flying Stars of 2026

The Tai Sui prescription and the Flying Stars prescription are two separate systems, and they overlap in ways that matter. The 2026 Flying Stars put the 5 Yellow in the southwest, the 2 Black in the north, and the 9 Purple in the south (the helpful star). The Tai Sui puts the seat in the south, the clash in the north, the break in the east, and the harm in the southwest.

The southwest is both the harm direction of the Tai Sui AND the 5 Yellow of the Flying Stars. That double-occupation is one of the more demanding sector prescriptions of 2026, and it is the reason most practitioners are telling households to keep the southwest quiet all year — not just for one of the two reasons, but for both.

The south is the seat of the Tai Sui AND the location of the 9 Purple helpful star. This is more interesting. The Tai Sui prescription says "keep the south still," and the Flying Star prescription says "put a moving-water feature here, the year is asking for activity." These two prescriptions are not contradictory, but they need a careful read. The classical resolution is to keep the south still on the Tai Sui level (no renovations, no disturbance of the south wall) while activating the south on the Flying Star level (a quiet water feature, a clean and uncluttered south-facing window, a south-facing workspace). The two prescriptions can both be honored at once if the south sector is treated as a "quiet activity" zone rather than a "renovation zone."

The north is the clash direction of the Tai Sui AND the 2 Black of the Flying Stars. The 2 Black is the year's sickness star, and the prescription is also quiet — no renovation, no disturbance, ideally a quiet metal cure (a metal object, a six-coin Chinese sword, or a quiet metal wind chime) to dissolve the sickness star's energy. The Tai Sui clash prescription agrees: quiet is better than disturbance.

The southwest has two reasons to be quiet. The south has two prescriptions that look contradictory but resolve into "quiet activity." The two systems overlap most of the time, and where they disagree the practitioner reads both and finds the resolution.

What to do this week, with Xia Zhi tomorrow

Xia Zhi (the Summer Solstice) lands tomorrow, June 21. The classical advice is to do any Tai Sui work either before the solstice (today, the 20th) or after the solstice has settled (the 23rd or 24th). The solstice is a transition day, and the Tai Sui's energy is shifting across the transition. Doing the work in the transition window is read as "arguing" with both the old and the new presiding officer.

The practical move today, if you want to honor the Tai Sui of 2026, is three things. First, take a quick compass reading of the south sector of your home and confirm where the Wu mountain falls in practice. If the home sits at an unusual angle to compass north (a hillside home, a rotated subdivision, an apartment in a building that faces 10 or 15 degrees off the cardinal), the south sector may not be where you think it is. The luo pan or a phone compass corrects for this.

Second, audit the south sector for any planned renovation work. If a kitchen, bathroom, or living-room project sits in the south, consider moving it to the second half of 2026 or to 2027. The year is long, and the south will be back to a freer sector next year.

Third, if a member of the household is a Horse or Rat, this week is a good week to pick up a red accessory (for a Horse) or to schedule the temple visit (for a Rat). The red accessory is small and inexpensive and the temple visit is usually a 30-minute affair. Neither is mandatory, and both are easy to skip if the household does not follow the practice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Tai Sui a real deity, or a symbolic framework?
Both, depending on the practitioner. Classical texts present the Tai Sui as a celestial officer who rotates through the 12 branches on a 12-year cycle. Modern practitioners often read the Tai Sui as a symbolic framework for tracking which compass sector is most sensitive in a given year. The framework works whether you take the deity literally or as a metaphor — the directional prescriptions are the same in either reading.
My home faces south. Am I in trouble in 2026?
Not necessarily. South-facing homes have their front door in the Wu mountain, which is the seat of the 2026 Tai Sui. The classical advice is to avoid drilling or renovating the south-facing front wall this year, especially near the door itself. Living in the home and using the door daily is fine — the prescription is about disturbance, not about the door itself. Most south-facing homes move through the year without serious friction.
I was born in a Rat year. How seriously should I take the Fan Tai Sui warning?
The Fan Tai Sui year (Rat in a Horse year) is one of the more demanding annual prescriptions. The classical advice is to wear a piece of jade or metal, to avoid the south direction for major decisions, and to make a temple visit at Li Chun for the annual jie huo blessing. None of these are mandatory. The prescription is a recommendation, and the year is navigable for most Rat people without the blessings. If you have had a difficult year recently or are in a major life transition, the prescription is worth following more carefully.
What is the difference between the Tai Sui and the Flying Stars?
They are two separate systems. The Tai Sui tracks the year's presiding deity and the four directional arguments with him (seat, clash, break, harm). The Flying Stars track nine numbers that rotate through the nine sectors of the bagua each year. Both systems are about what to do in each compass sector, but they use different logic. The Tai Sui is mostly about avoidance (keep these sectors still). The Flying Stars are mostly about activation (place cures here, leave these alone). Most practitioners read both and find where the prescriptions agree.
Do I have to visit a temple to化解 the Fan Tai Sui?
No — the temple visit is the traditional prescription, not a requirement. Many practitioners offer a "set up Tai Sui" prayer or a simple incense-and-bow ritual at home, and the home ritual is read as adequate for most households. The temple visit is the more formal version, and it is what households do when the year is unusually demanding or when the household is in a major life transition. If you do not have a temple nearby, the home ritual is the working alternative.
What happens if I renovate the south anyway?
Nothing dramatic, in most cases. The Tai Sui prescription is a recommendation, and most households that renovate their south in a south-sector Tai Sui year do not experience obvious bad luck as a result. The classical reading is that the year becomes slightly more demanding than it would have been, with more friction in the areas of life associated with the south sector (career, reputation, visibility). If the renovation is unavoidable, doing the work in the second half of the year and keeping the disturbance minimal is the classical compromise.
